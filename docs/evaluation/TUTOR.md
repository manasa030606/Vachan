### ✅ hi-grammar-intermediate — hi, intermediate

**Q:** When do I use ने in a sentence?

**Status:** answered · **cited:** `hi/common-mistakes#forgetting-ne-in-the-past-tense`, `hi/verbs#past-tense-with-ne-the-ergative` · 174 words · 4495 ms · gemini/gemini-3.5-flash

> In Hindi, you use the postposition **ने** (ne) in the simple past tense when the verb takes an object (transitive verbs like eat, see, drink, buy, say, write).
> 
> When you use **ने**:
> - The subject gets **ने** (ne).
> - The verb agrees with the object in gender and number, rather than the subject. For example, in **मैंने चाय पी** (mainne chaay pee, I drank tea), the verb **पी** (pee) is feminine because **चाय** (chaay, tea) is feminine.
> - Pronouns combine with **ने** (ne) to form words like **मैंने** (mainne, I), **तुमने** (tumne, you), or **उसने** (usne, he/she).
> - If the object is followed by **को** (ko), the verb defaults to the masculine singular form, as in **मैंने माँ को देखा** (mainne maa ko dekhaa, I saw mother).
> 
> **When NOT to use it:**
> Verbs without an object, including verbs of motion like **जाना** (jaanaa, to go) and **आना** (aanaa, to come), never take **ने** (ne). For example, "I went" is **मैं गया** (main gayaa) for a man, or **मैं गई** (main gaee) for a woman.

> In Malayalam, verbs do **not** change based on who is doing the action! Whether the subject is "I", "you", "he", or anyone else, the verb form remains exactly the same.
> 
> There are no person, number, or gender endings to learn for verbs in modern Malayalam. Because the verb itself does not show who is performing the action, speakers usually include the subject pronoun in the sentence so the meaning is clear.

> For example, the verb for "go" is പോകുന്നു (pokunnu). It stays the same no matter who is going:

> - **അവൻ പോകുന്നു** (avan pokunnu, he goes)
> 
> This makes learning Malayalam verb conjugations much simpler!

### ✅ beginner-bn-formal — bn, elementary

**Q:** What is the difference between the formal and casual "you" in Bengali?

**Status:** answered · **cited:** `bn/grammar#three-levels-of-you`, `bn/common-mistakes#using-the-wrong-you`, `course/bn/words#you-casual-tumi`, `bn/beginner-guide#formal-and-informal-you` · 83 words · 2391 ms · gemini/gemini-3.5-flash-lite

> Bengali has three words for "you", and the verb ending changes with each:
> 
> - আপনি (aapni) is the polite form used for elders, strangers, teachers, and shopkeepers.
> - তুমি (tumi) is the familiar form used for friends, family, and people your age.
> - তুই (tui) is very intimate, used only for very close friends, younger siblings, and small children.
> 
> For example, with the verb "to eat" খাওয়া (khaowa):
> - তুমি খাও (tumi khaao)
> - আপনি খান (aapni khaan)
> - তুই খাস (tui khaash)
