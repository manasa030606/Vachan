### ✅ hi-pronunciation — hi, elementary

**Q:** Why is कमरा pronounced kamraa?

**Status:** answered · **cited:** `hi/pronunciation#silent-a-schwa-deletion` · 89 words · 3583 ms · gemini/gemini-3.5-flash

> In Hindi, every consonant letter naturally carries a short "a" sound. However, in spoken Hindi, this "a" is often dropped. This is known as **schwa deletion** or a silent "a". 
> 
> While the letters in **कमरा** (kamraa, room) might suggest a pronunciation like "kamaraa", the short "a" in the middle is dropped in speech. Therefore, it is pronounced as **kamraa** (kamraa, room). 
> 
> This silent "a" also happens at the end of words, such as in **नाम** (naam, name), and in the middle of other words like **समझना** (samajhnaa, to understand).

### ✅ hi-grammar-intermediate — hi, intermediate

**Q:** When do I use ने in a sentence?

**Status:** answered · **cited:** `hi/verbs#past-tense-with-ne-the-ergative` · 147 words · 7553 ms · gemini/gemini-3.5-flash

> In Hindi, you use the particle **ने** (ne) in the simple past tense when a verb takes an object (such as eat, see, drink, write).
> 
> Here is how it works:
> - **Subject Agreement:** The subject gets **ने** (ne), and the verb agrees with the object instead of the subject. For example, in **राम ने रोटी खाई** (Ram ne rotee khaayee, Ram ate a roti), the verb is feminine to agree with the feminine object **रोटी** (rotee).
> - **Pronouns:** Pronouns join with **ने** (ne) to form combined words like **मैंने** (mainne), **तुमने** (tumne), **आपने** (aapne), **उसने** (usne), **हमने** (hamne), and **उन्होंने** (unhonne).
> - **With को:** If the object has **को** (ko), the verb stays masculine singular, as in **मैंने माँ को देखा** (mainne maa ko dekhaa, I saw mother).
> 
> **When not to use it:**
> Verbs without an object, like **जाना** (jaanaa) and **आना** (aanaa), never take **ने** (ne).
