# The Kannada script

## How Kannada writing works

Kannada is written in the Kannada script, an abugida that grew out of the ancient Brahmi script and is very closely related to the Telugu script — many letters look almost the same. In an abugida every consonant letter already carries a short "a" sound: ಕ is read "ka", not just "k". To write another vowel after a consonant you add a vowel sign to it. Kannada is written from left to right and its letters have round shapes. A letter is called ಅಕ್ಷರ (akshara); the whole alphabet is ವರ್ಣಮಾಲೆ (varnamaale).

## Vowels — ಸ್ವರಗಳು (swaragalu)

<!-- topic: vowels -->

Kannada vowels are called ಸ್ವರಗಳು (swaragalu). The main ones are: ಅ (a), ಆ (aa), ಇ (i), ಈ (ee), ಉ (u), ಊ (oo), ಋ (ru), ಎ (e), ಏ (e, long), ಐ (ai), ಒ (o), ಓ (o, long), ಔ (au). Two extra signs follow them, called ಯೋಗವಾಹಕಗಳು (yogavaahakagalu): ಅಂ (am, the anusvara, a nasal "m/n" sound) and ಅಃ (ah, the visarga). Short and long vowels are different letters, so ಎ (short e) and ಏ (long e), ಒ (short o) and ಓ (long o) must not be mixed up. Beginners usually start with ಅ ಆ ಇ ಈ ಉ ಊ.

## Consonants — ವ್ಯಂಜನಗಳು (vyanjanagalu)

<!-- topic: consonants -->

Kannada consonants are called ವ್ಯಂಜನಗಳು (vyanjanagalu). The first 25 are grouped by where the sound is made in the mouth (ವರ್ಗೀಯ, vargeeya): ಕ ಖ ಗ ಘ ಙ (throat), ಚ ಛ ಜ ಝ ಞ (palate), ಟ ಠ ಡ ಢ ಣ (retroflex, tongue curled back), ತ ಥ ದ ಧ ನ (dental, tongue at the teeth), ಪ ಫ ಬ ಭ ಮ (lips). Each row goes plain, aspirated (with a puff of air), voiced, voiced-aspirated, nasal. The rest are not grouped (ಅವರ್ಗೀಯ, avargeeya): ಯ ರ ಲ ವ ಶ ಷ ಸ ಹ ಳ. Every consonant is read with "a" until a vowel sign changes it.

## Vowel signs and the talekattu — ಕಾಗುಣಿತ (kaagunita)

<!-- topic: vowel-signs -->

When a vowel follows a consonant, Kannada writes a vowel sign instead of the full vowel; a consonant with a vowel sign is a ಗುಣಿತಾಕ್ಷರ (gunitaakshara). The table of a consonant with every vowel sign is called ಕಾಗುಣಿತ (kaagunita): ಕ (ka), ಕಾ (kaa), ಕಿ (ki), ಕೀ (kee), ಕು (ku), ಕೂ (koo), ಕೃ (kru), ಕೆ (ke), ಕೇ (ke, long), ಕೈ (kai), ಕೊ (ko), ಕೋ (ko, long), ಕೌ (kau), ಕಂ (kam), ಕಃ (kah). Many Kannada consonants carry a small tick on top called the ತಲೆಕಟ್ಟು (talekattu), which shows the built-in "a"; when a sign such as ಿ (i) is added, the tick is replaced: ಕ → ಕಿ.

## Conjunct letters — ಒತ್ತಕ್ಷರ (ottakshara)

<!-- topic: conjuncts; level: elementary -->

When two consonants come together with no vowel between them, Kannada writes the second consonant as a small form, usually below the first. This small form is called an ಒತ್ತು (ottu), and the combined letter an ಒತ್ತಕ್ಷರ (ottakshara). Examples: ಅಮ್ಮ (amma, mother) has ಮ under ಮ; ಅಪ್ಪ (appa, father) has ಪ under ಪ; ಸ್ನೇಹಿತ (snehita, friend) has ನ under ಸ; ಕ್ಷ (ksha) appears in ಕ್ಷಮಿಸಿ (kshamisi, sorry). A ರ before another consonant is written as a mark after it, called ಅರ್ಕಾವೊತ್ತು (arkaavottu), as in ಅರ್ಥ (artha, meaning). A consonant with no vowel at all is shown with the halant mark ್, as in ಕ್ (k).
