# Common mistakes English speakers make in Kannada

## Formal vs informal "you" — ನೀವು vs ನೀನು (neevu vs neenu)

<!-- topic: politeness; level: beginner -->

The most common beginner mistake in Kannada is using the casual ನೀನು (neenu, you) with strangers. ನೀವು (neevu) is the respectful "you" (and also "you" plural): use it with elders, teachers, shopkeepers, auto drivers and anyone you don't know well. ನೀನು (neenu) is only for close friends, younger family members and children; with an elder it sounds rude. The verb changes too: ನೀವು ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀರಿ? (neevu hegiddeeri?, how are you? — formal) vs ನೀನು ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀಯ? (neenu hegiddeeya? — casual), and "your" is ನಿಮ್ಮ (nimma) vs ನಿನ್ನ (ninna). Rule of thumb: start with ನೀವು and let the other person invite you to use ನೀನು.

## Formal vs casual commands — ಬನ್ನಿ vs ಬಾ (banni vs baa)

<!-- topic: imperative; level: beginner -->

Kannada commands have a casual and a polite form, and mixing them up is a common mistake. Casual: ಬಾ (baa, come), ಕೊಡು (kodu, give), ಹೇಳು (helu, tell), ಕೂತ್ಕೋ (kootko, sit). Polite: ಬನ್ನಿ (banni, please come), ಕೊಡಿ (kodi, please give), ಹೇಳಿ (heli, please tell), ಕುಳಿತುಕೊಳ್ಳಿ (kulitukolli, please sit). The polite form usually ends in -ಇ (-i) or -ಇರಿ (-iri) and is already "please" — you don't need ದಯವಿಟ್ಟು (dayavittu) every time. Saying ನೀರು ಕೊಡು (neeru kodu) to a waiter sounds bossy; say ನೀರು ಕೊಡಿ (neeru kodi). Use the casual form only with friends and children.

## Two words for "no" — ಇಲ್ಲ vs ಅಲ್ಲ (illa vs alla)

<!-- topic: negation -->

Kannada has two words that English speakers translate as "no / not", and they are not interchangeable. ಇಲ್ಲ (illa) means "there isn't / doesn't exist / no": ನೀರು ಇಲ್ಲ (neeru illa, there is no water), ನನಗೆ ಗೊತ್ತಿಲ್ಲ (nanage gottilla, I don't know). It is also the normal "no" to a yes/no question. ಅಲ್ಲ (alla) means "is not (that)", denying identity: ಇದು ಹಾಲು ಅಲ್ಲ (idu haalu alla, this is not milk), ನಾನು ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್ ಅಲ್ಲ (naanu daaktar alla, I'm not a doctor). A third word, ಬೇಡ (beda), means "don't want / no thanks": ಸಕ್ಕರೆ ಬೇಡ (no sugar, please). Mistake: ಇದು ನನ್ನ ಪುಸ್ತಕ ಇಲ್ಲ ✗ → ಇದು ನನ್ನ ಪುಸ್ತಕ ಅಲ್ಲ ✓.

## Gender agreement in verbs — ಬಂದನು, ಬಂದಳು, ಬಂತು

<!-- topic: gender; level: elementary -->

In Kannada, "he", "she" and "it" each need their own verb ending, and English speakers often use one for all. Present/future: ಅವನು ಬರುತ್ತಾನೆ (avanu baruttaane, he comes), ಅವಳು ಬರುತ್ತಾಳೆ (avalu baruttaale, she comes), ಅದು ಬರುತ್ತದೆ (adu baruttade, it comes). Past: ಅವನು ಬಂದನು (bandanu, he came), ಅವಳು ಬಂದಳು (bandalu, she came), ಅದು ಬಂತು (bantu, it came). For respected people of either gender, use ಅವರು (avaru) with -ಆರೆ / -ಅರು: ಅವರು ಬರುತ್ತಾರೆ (he/she comes, respectful). "I", "you" and "we" forms do not change with gender: a man and a woman both say ನಾನು ಬರುತ್ತೇನೆ (I will come). Using ಅದು (adu, it) for a person is rude.

## Word order — the verb goes last

<!-- topic: word-order; level: beginner -->

English speakers often put the Kannada verb in the middle, as in English. Kannada is subject – object – verb: ನಾನು ಕಾಫಿ ಕುಡಿಯುತ್ತೇನೆ (naanu kaafi kudiyuttene) = I coffee drink = "I drink coffee". ✗ ನಾನು ಕುಡಿಯುತ್ತೇನೆ ಕಾಫಿ sounds wrong. Words for place and time come before the verb too: ನಾನು ನಾಳೆ ಮೈಸೂರಿಗೆ ಹೋಗುತ್ತೇನೆ (I tomorrow to-Mysuru will-go). "In, to, from" are endings after the noun, not words before it: ಮನೆಯಲ್ಲಿ (maneyalli, in the house), not "in house". Question words stay in place, before the verb: ನೀವು ಎಲ್ಲಿಗೆ ಹೋಗುತ್ತೀರಿ? (where will you go?).

## Dropping "is" — don't translate every "is"

<!-- topic: copula -->

In Kannada, "X is Y" sentences have no verb, and learners often add one. Correct: ಇದು ನೀರು (idu neeru, this is water), ನನ್ನ ಹೆಸರು ಆಶಾ (nanna hesaru Asha, my name is Asha), ನಾನು ವಿದ್ಯಾರ್ಥಿ (naanu vidyaarthi, I am a student). ✗ ಇದು ನೀರು ಇದೆ means something odd like "this, there is water". Use ಇದೆ (ide, is there) only for existence or location: ನೀರು ಇದೆ (there is water), ಪುಸ್ತಕ ಮೇಜಿನ ಮೇಲೆ ಇದೆ (the book is on the table), or for a current state: ಊಟ ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದೆ (the food is good). To deny identity, use ಅಲ್ಲ (alla): ಇದು ನೀರು ಅಲ್ಲ.

## ನಾನು vs ನನಗೆ — "I" vs "to me"

<!-- topic: dative -->

English "I want / I like / I know / I am hungry" are "to me" sentences in Kannada, using ನನಗೆ (nanage, to me), not ನಾನು (naanu, I). Correct: ನನಗೆ ನೀರು ಬೇಕು (nanage neeru beku, I want water), ನನಗೆ ಕಾಫಿ ಇಷ್ಟ (nanage kaafi ishta, I like coffee), ನನಗೆ ಗೊತ್ತಿಲ್ಲ (nanage gottilla, I don't know), ನನಗೆ ಹಸಿವಾಗಿದೆ (nanage hasivaagide, I am hungry). ✗ ನಾನು ನೀರು ಬೇಕು and ✗ ನಾನು ಹಸಿವು are mistakes. For actions, use ನಾನು: ನಾನು ಹೋಗುತ್ತೇನೆ (naanu hoguttene, I will go). The same applies to "you": ನಿಮಗೆ ಏನು ಬೇಕು? (nimage enu beku?, what do you want?).

## Long vs short vowels — ಕಾಲು vs ಕಲ್ಲು

<!-- topic: vowel-length; skill: pronunciation -->

In Kannada, vowel length changes meaning, and English speakers often shorten long vowels. Pairs to practise: ಕಾಲು (kaalu, leg) vs ಕಲ್ಲು (kallu, stone); ಹಾಲು (haalu, milk) vs ಹಲ್ಲು (hallu, tooth); ಬೆಲೆ (bele, price, short ಎ) vs ಬೇಳೆ (bele, lentils, long ಏ); and ಮೂರು (mooru, three) needs a long "oo". Long vowels (aa, ee, oo, and long ಏ / ಓ) are held about twice as long. Vachan writes both ಎ and ಏ as "e" and both ಒ and ಓ as "o", so check the Kannada letter. Slow, exaggerated practice helps: haa-lu, kaa-lu.

## Retroflex vs dental sounds — ಅಣ್ಣ vs ಅನ್ನ

<!-- topic: retroflex; skill: pronunciation -->

Kannada has dental ತ ದ ನ ಲ (tongue against the upper teeth) and retroflex ಟ ಡ ಣ ಳ (tongue tip curled back), and English speakers tend to use one in-between sound. The difference can change meaning: ಅಣ್ಣ (anna, elder brother, retroflex ಣ) vs ಅನ್ನ (anna, cooked rice, dental ನ); ಓದು (odu, read, dental ದ) vs ಓಡು (odu, run, retroflex ಡ); ಹಣ (hana, money) has retroflex ಣ. Vachan's romanization writes both the same (n, d, t, l), so learn the script letter. Tip: for retroflex sounds, curl the tongue back and touch the roof of the mouth; for dental ones, touch the back of the teeth.

## Literal translations that sound odd

<!-- topic: idiomatic-usage; level: intermediate -->

Some English phrases translated word for word sound strange in Kannada. "You're welcome" is not ಸ್ವಾಗತ (svaagata), which means welcome to a place; say ಪರವಾಗಿಲ್ಲ (paravaagilla, it's all right). "Good morning" as ಶುಭೋದಯ (shubhodaya) is correct but formal; people say ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ (namaskaara). "I love my family" as ನಾನು ನನ್ನ ಕುಟುಂಬವನ್ನು ಪ್ರೀತಿಸುತ್ತೇನೆ sounds bookish; say ನನಗೆ ನಮ್ಮ ಮನೆಯವರು ಅಂದ್ರೆ ತುಂಬಾ ಇಷ್ಟ (nanage namma maneyavaru andre tumbaa ishta). "My house" is usually ನಮ್ಮ ಮನೆ (namma mane, our house). "Goodbye" is ಹೋಗಿ ಬರುತ್ತೇನೆ (hogi baruttene, I'll go and come), and a host never says just ಹೋಗಿ (go), which sounds like sending you away.

## ಹೌದು vs ಸರಿ — "yes" vs "okay"

<!-- topic: everyday-phrases; level: beginner -->

Kannada ಹೌದು (haudu) means "yes, that's true", for confirming facts: ನೀವು ಆಶಾ ಅಲ್ವಾ? — ಹೌದು (you're Asha, right? — yes). To agree to a request or invitation, Kannada speakers say ಸರಿ (sari, okay) or ಆಯ್ತು (aaytu, okay, done), not ಹೌದು. ನಾಳೆ ಬನ್ನಿ (please come tomorrow) → ಸರಿ, ಬರ್ತೀನಿ (sari, barteeni, okay, I'll come). Saying ಹೌದು there sounds like "that's true" rather than "I agree". Similarly, to answer "do you have …?" repeat the verb: ಇದೆಯಾ? → ಇದೆ (ide, there is).

## Plurals after numbers

<!-- topic: plurals -->

English speakers often add the Kannada plural -ಗಳು (-galu) after a number, but in everyday speech the noun usually stays singular: ಐದು ರೂಪಾಯಿ (aidu roopaayi, five rupees), ಎರಡು ಟೀ (eradu tee, two teas), ಮೂರು ದಿನ (mooru dina, three days), ಎರಡು ಪುಸ್ತಕ (eradu pustaka, two books). Careful written Kannada may use the plural, ಎರಡು ಪುಸ್ತಕಗಳು (eradu pustakagalu), and that is also correct. People are counted with special forms or ಜನ (jana): ಇಬ್ಬರು (ibbaru, two people), ಐದು ಜನ (aidu jana, five people). Uncountable things like ನೀರು (neeru, water) are never pluralised.

## Written vs spoken Kannada forms

<!-- topic: spoken-kannada; level: intermediate -->

Learners are often confused because spoken Kannada, as in Bengaluru, shortens the written verb endings. Written ಬರುತ್ತೇನೆ (baruttene, I will come) is spoken ಬರ್ತೀನಿ (barteeni); ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದ್ದೇನೆ (chennaagiddene, I am fine) → ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದ್ದೀನಿ (chennaagiddeeni); ಆಯಿತು (aayitu, it happened) → ಆಯ್ತು (aaytu); ಬರುತ್ತದೆ (baruttade, it comes) → ಬರುತ್ತೆ (barutte); ತಿನ್ನುವುದಿಲ್ಲ (tinnuvudilla, won't eat) → ತಿನ್ನಲ್ಲ (tinnalla); ನನಗೆ (nanage) → ನಂಗೆ (nange). Both are correct: the written forms are always understood and polite, and the spoken forms sound natural in conversation. Using the written form in speech is never a mistake, just a little formal.

## Commonly confused word pairs

<!-- topic: vocabulary; level: elementary -->

Kannada word pairs beginners mix up: ಬೇಕು (beku, want) vs ಬೇಡ (beda, don't want); ಇದು (idu, this one) vs ಈ (ee, this + noun): ಇದು ಏನು? but ಈ ಪುಸ್ತಕ; ಇಲ್ಲಿ (illi, here) vs ಇಲ್ಲ (illa, no); ಹಣ (hana, money) vs ಹಣ್ಣು (hannu, fruit); ಗೊತ್ತು (gottu, know a fact) vs ಬರುತ್ತೆ (barutte, know a skill / language): ನನಗೆ ಕನ್ನಡ ಬರುತ್ತೆ (I know Kannada); ಕೇಳು (kelu) means both "listen" and "ask"; ನಿಲ್ಲು (nillu, stop / stand yourself) vs ನಿಲ್ಲಿಸು (nillisu, stop a vehicle). ತಮ್ಮ (tamma, younger brother) vs ನಮ್ಮ (namma, our).
