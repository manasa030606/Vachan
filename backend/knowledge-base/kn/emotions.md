# Feelings and relationships in Kannada

## How feelings work in Kannada — ನನಗೆ … ಆಗಿದೆ

<!-- type: grammar; skill: grammar -->

In Kannada most feelings are nouns that "happen" or "come" to you, so the person takes the -ಗೆ (-ge) ending: ನನಗೆ (nanage, to me), ನಿಮಗೆ (nimage, to you). ನನಗೆ ಸುಸ್ತಾಗಿದೆ (nanage sustaagide) = I am tired, literally "to me tiredness has happened". ನನಗೆ ಕೋಪ ಬಂದಿದೆ (nanage kopa bandide) = I am angry, literally "anger has come to me". ನನಗೆ ಭಯ ಆಗುತ್ತಿದೆ (nanage bhaya aaguttide) = I am scared. To ask a friend: ನಿನಗೆ ಏನಾಯ್ತು? (ninage enaaytu?, what happened to you?). Spoken Kannada shortens ಆಗುತ್ತಿದೆ (aaguttide, is happening) to ಆಗ್ತಿದೆ (aagtide).

## I am happy — ನನಗೆ ತುಂಬಾ ಖುಷಿ ಆಗಿದೆ (nanage tumbaa khushi aagide)

To say "I am happy" in Kannada, the everyday way is ನನಗೆ ತುಂಬಾ ಖುಷಿ ಆಗಿದೆ (nanage tumbaa khushi aagide, I am very happy), or after good news ತುಂಬಾ ಖುಷಿ ಆಯ್ತು (tumbaa khushi aaytu, that made me so happy). ಖುಷಿ (khushi) is the common spoken word for joy; ಸಂತೋಷ (santosha) is the standard word: ನಾನು ಸಂತೋಷವಾಗಿದ್ದೇನೆ (naanu santoshavaagiddene, I am happy), spoken ನಾನು ಖುಷಿಯಾಗಿದ್ದೀನಿ (naanu khushiyaagiddeeni). "I'm glad to hear that" is ಕೇಳಿ ಸಂತೋಷ ಆಯ್ತು (keli santosha aaytu).

## I am sad — ನನಗೆ ಬೇಸರವಾಗಿದೆ (nanage besaravaagide)

To say "I am sad" or "I feel low" in Kannada, say ನನಗೆ ಬೇಸರವಾಗಿದೆ (nanage besaravaagide); in speech, ನನಗೆ ಬೇಜಾರಾಗಿದೆ (nanage bejaaraagide) is the most common. ಬೇಸರ / ಬೇಜಾರು (besara / bejaaru) covers sad, upset and fed up. ದುಃಖ (duhkha) is deep sorrow, used for grief: ತುಂಬಾ ದುಃಖ ಆಯ್ತು (tumbaa duhkha aaytu, I am very sad / so sorry to hear it). To ask someone: ಯಾಕೆ ಬೇಜಾರಾಗಿದ್ದೀಯಾ? (yaake bejaaraagiddeeyaa?, why are you sad?) to a friend, or ಯಾಕೆ ಬೇಜಾರಾಗಿದ್ದೀರಾ? (yaake bejaaraagiddeeraa?) politely.

Also asked as: how to say I am sad in Kannada, I feel down, I'm upset, why are you sad, bejaaru meaning

## I am tired — ನನಗೆ ಸುಸ್ತಾಗಿದೆ (nanage sustaagide)

To say "I am tired" in Kannada, say ನನಗೆ ಸುಸ್ತಾಗಿದೆ (nanage sustaagide), literally "to me tiredness has happened"; ಸುಸ್ತು (sustu) means tiredness. Add ತುಂಬಾ (tumbaa, very): ತುಂಬಾ ಸುಸ್ತಾಗಿದೆ (I'm very tired). "I am sleepy" is ನಿದ್ದೆ ಬರ್ತಿದೆ (nidde bartide, sleep is coming). "I need rest" is ನನಗೆ ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ವಿಶ್ರಾಂತಿ ಬೇಕು (nanage svalpa vishraanti beku), or in speech ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ರೆಸ್ಟ್ ಬೇಕು (svalpa rest beku). To ask: ಸುಸ್ತಾಗಿದೆಯಾ? (sustaagideyaa?, are you tired?).

Also asked as: how to say I am tired in Kannada, I'm sleepy, I need rest, exhausted, sustu meaning

## I am angry — ನನಗೆ ಕೋಪ ಬಂದಿದೆ (nanage kopa bandide)

To say "I am angry" in Kannada, say ನನಗೆ ಕೋಪ ಬಂದಿದೆ (nanage kopa bandide), literally "anger has come to me"; ಕೋಪ (kopa) means anger. "I got angry" is ನನಗೆ ಕೋಪ ಬಂತು (nanage kopa bantu). To calm someone: ಕೋಪ ಮಾಡ್ಕೋಬೇಡಿ (kopa maadkobedi, please don't be angry), to a friend ಕೋಪ ಮಾಡ್ಕೋಬೇಡ (kopa maadkobeda). "Are you angry with me?" is ನನ್ನ ಮೇಲೆ ಕೋಪನಾ? (nanna mele kopanaa?) in casual speech. Saying sorry with ತಪ್ಪಾಯ್ತು (tappaaytu, my mistake) usually helps.

Also asked as: how to say I am angry in Kannada, don't be angry, are you angry with me, mad, kopa meaning

## I am bored — ನನಗೆ ಬೋರ್ ಆಗ್ತಿದೆ (nanage bor aagtide)

To say "I am bored" in Kannada, the most natural everyday phrase uses the English word: ನನಗೆ ಬೋರ್ ಆಗ್ತಿದೆ (nanage bor aagtide), literally "boredom is happening to me". The Kannada word ಬೇಜಾರು (bejaaru) also covers boredom: ಮನೆಯಲ್ಲಿ ಬೇಜಾರಾಗ್ತಿದೆ (maneyalli bejaaraagtide, I'm bored at home). The more formal form is ನನಗೆ ಬೇಸರವಾಗುತ್ತಿದೆ (nanage besaravaaguttide). "This is boring" is ಇದು ಬೋರಿಂಗ್ (idu boring) or ಇದು ಬೋರ್ ಹೊಡೀತಿದೆ (idu bor hodeetide, slang). A friend may suggest ಹೊರಗೆ ಹೋಗೋಣ (horage hogona, let's go out).

## I am scared — ನನಗೆ ಭಯ ಆಗುತ್ತಿದೆ (nanage bhaya aaguttide)

To say "I am scared" in Kannada, say ನನಗೆ ಭಯ ಆಗುತ್ತಿದೆ (nanage bhaya aaguttide), spoken ಭಯ ಆಗ್ತಿದೆ (bhaya aagtide); ಭಯ (bhaya) means fear. "I am scared of dogs" is ನನಗೆ ನಾಯಿ ಅಂದರೆ ಭಯ (nanage naayi andare bhaya), literally "to me, dogs means fear". The spoken word ಹೆದರಿಕೆ (hedarike) means the same. To reassure someone: ಹೆದರಬೇಡಿ (hedarabedi, don't be afraid), to a child or friend ಹೆದರಬೇಡ (hedarabeda), and ಏನೂ ಆಗಲ್ಲ (enoo aagalla, nothing will happen).

## I am excited — ತುಂಬಾ ಖುಷಿ ಆಗ್ತಿದೆ (tumbaa khushi aagtide)

Kannada has no single everyday word for "excited". People say ತುಂಬಾ ಖುಷಿ ಆಗ್ತಿದೆ (tumbaa khushi aagtide, I'm so happy about it) or, very commonly in Bengaluru, use English: ತುಂಬಾ ಎಕ್ಸೈಟ್ ಆಗಿದ್ದೀನಿ (tumbaa eksait aagiddeeni, I'm really excited). The more literary word ಉತ್ಸಾಹ (utsaaha, enthusiasm) appears in writing: ತುಂಬಾ ಉತ್ಸಾಹದಲ್ಲಿದ್ದೇನೆ (tumbaa utsaahadalliddene, I'm full of enthusiasm).

## I am worried — ನನಗೆ ಚಿಂತೆ ಆಗ್ತಿದೆ (nanage chinte aagtide)

To say "I am worried" in Kannada, say ನನಗೆ ಚಿಂತೆ ಆಗ್ತಿದೆ (nanage chinte aagtide); ಚಿಂತೆ (chinte) means worry. Young people often use the Indian English word: ಟೆನ್ಶನ್ ಆಗ್ತಿದೆ (tenshan aagtide, I'm stressed / worried). "I am worried about the exam" is ಪರೀಕ್ಷೆ ಬಗ್ಗೆ ಚಿಂತೆ ಆಗ್ತಿದೆ (pareekshe bagge chinte aagtide); ಬಗ್ಗೆ (bagge) means "about". To comfort someone: ಚಿಂತೆ ಮಾಡಬೇಡಿ (chinte maadabedi, don't worry), to a friend ಟೆನ್ಶನ್ ತಗೋಬೇಡ (tenshan tagobeda, don't take tension).

Also asked as: how to say I am worried in Kannada, I'm stressed, don't worry, tension, anxious

## I love you — ನಾನು ನಿನ್ನನ್ನು ಪ್ರೀತಿಸುತ್ತೇನೆ (naanu ninnannu preetisuttene)

<!-- topic: relationships -->

"I love you" in Kannada is ನಾನು ನಿನ್ನನ್ನು ಪ್ರೀತಿಸುತ್ತೇನೆ (naanu ninnannu preetisuttene), from ಪ್ರೀತಿ (preeti, love). It is romantic and serious — said to a partner, not casually. Couples usually use the intimate ನೀನು (neenu) form; the spoken version is ನಾನು ನಿನ್ನ ಪ್ರೀತಿಸ್ತೀನಿ (naanu ninna preetisteeni). Many young people simply say "I love you" in English. For family and friends, Kannada speakers show love differently: ನನಗೆ ನೀನು ಅಂದರೆ ತುಂಬಾ ಇಷ್ಟ (nanage neenu andare tumbaa ishta, I really love you / you mean a lot to me), or through care such as ಊಟ ಆಯ್ತಾ? (have you eaten?).

## I miss you — ನಿನ್ನ ನೆನಪು ತುಂಬಾ ಆಗುತ್ತಿದೆ (ninna nenapu tumbaa aaguttide)

<!-- topic: relationships -->

"I miss you" in Kannada is ನಿನ್ನ ನೆನಪು ತುಂಬಾ ಆಗುತ್ತಿದೆ (ninna nenapu tumbaa aaguttide), literally "your memory is coming to me a lot"; ನೆನಪು (nenapu) means memory. Spoken: ನಿನ್ನ ನೆನಪು ತುಂಬಾ ಆಗ್ತಿದೆ (aagtide). This warm native phrase suits family, friends and partners. To an elder or politely, use ನಿಮ್ಮ (nimma, your): ನಿಮ್ಮ ನೆನಪು ತುಂಬಾ ಆಗುತ್ತಿದೆ. Young people also say ನಿನ್ನ ತುಂಬಾ ಮಿಸ್ ಮಾಡ್ಕೊಳ್ತಿದ್ದೀನಿ (ninna tumbaa mis maadkoltiddeeni, I'm missing you a lot). "I missed you" (past) is ನಿನ್ನ ನೆನಪು ತುಂಬಾ ಆಯ್ತು (ninna nenapu tumbaa aaytu).

## I like you — ನನಗೆ ನೀನು ಇಷ್ಟ (nanage neenu ishta)

<!-- topic: relationships -->

"I like you" in Kannada is ನನಗೆ ನೀನು ಇಷ್ಟ (nanage neenu ishta), literally "to me you are liked". With ನೀನು (neenu) it can sound romantic, so with a friend, classmate or colleague use the polite ನನಗೆ ನೀವು ಇಷ್ಟ (nanage neevu ishta), or praise something specific: ನಿಮ್ಮ ಸ್ವಭಾವ ನನಗೆ ತುಂಬಾ ಇಷ್ಟ (nimma svabhaava nanage tumbaa ishta, I really like your nature). "Do you like me?" is ನಿನಗೆ ನಾನು ಇಷ್ಟನಾ? (ninage naanu ishtanaa?) in casual speech. "I like you a lot" is ನನಗೆ ನೀನು ತುಂಬಾ ಇಷ್ಟ.

## I'm sorry — ಕ್ಷಮಿಸಿ, ತಪ್ಪಾಯ್ತು (kshamisi, tappaaytu)

<!-- topic: apologies -->

To apologise in Kannada, say ಕ್ಷಮಿಸಿ (kshamisi, please forgive me / sorry); to a friend, ಕ್ಷಮಿಸು (kshamisu). For a real apology, add ತಪ್ಪಾಯ್ತು (tappaaytu, it was my mistake), written ತಪ್ಪಾಯಿತು (tappaayitu): ಕ್ಷಮಿಸಿ, ನನ್ನಿಂದ ತಪ್ಪಾಯ್ತು (kshamisi, nanninda tappaaytu, sorry, I made a mistake). In everyday speech the English "sorry" (ಸಾರಿ, saari) is extremely common. The usual reply is ಪರವಾಗಿಲ್ಲ ಬಿಡಿ (paravaagilla bidi, it's okay, leave it). To express sympathy for bad news, say ಕೇಳಿ ತುಂಬಾ ಬೇಸರ ಆಯ್ತು (keli tumbaa besara aaytu, I'm so sorry to hear that).

## Congratulations — ಅಭಿನಂದನೆಗಳು (abhinandanegalu)

"Congratulations" in Kannada is ಅಭಿನಂದನೆಗಳು (abhinandanegalu); friends often just say "congrats" in English. For good wishes on an occasion, Kannada uses ಶುಭಾಶಯಗಳು (shubhaashayagalu, good wishes): ಹುಟ್ಟುಹಬ್ಬದ ಶುಭಾಶಯಗಳು (huttuhabbada shubhaashayagalu) = happy birthday; ಹಬ್ಬದ ಶುಭಾಶಯಗಳು (habbada shubhaashayagalu) = festival greetings; ಹೊಸ ವರ್ಷದ ಶುಭಾಶಯಗಳು (hosa varshada shubhaashayagalu) = happy new year. For a wedding: ಮದುವೆಯ ಶುಭಾಶಯಗಳು (maduveya shubhaashayagalu). To praise success: ತುಂಬಾ ಒಳ್ಳೆಯ ಸುದ್ದಿ! (tumbaa olleya suddi!, great news!).

## Take care — ಹುಷಾರಾಗಿರಿ (hushaaraagiri)

"Take care" in Kannada is ಹುಷಾರಾಗಿರಿ (hushaaraagiri, stay well / be careful), the polite form; to a friend or younger person, ಹುಷಾರಾಗಿರು (hushaaraagiru) or simply ಹುಷಾರು! (hushaaru!). When someone sets off on a journey, people say ಹುಷಾರಾಗಿ ಹೋಗಿ ಬನ್ನಿ (hushaaraagi hogi banni, go carefully and come back). Elders also say ಜೋಪಾನ (jopaana, be careful, look after yourself). It is often added to a goodbye: ಸರಿ, ಹುಷಾರಾಗಿರಿ (okay, take care).

## Get well soon — ಬೇಗ ಹುಷಾರಾಗಿ (bega hushaaraagi)

To wish a sick person "get well soon" in Kannada, say ಬೇಗ ಹುಷಾರಾಗಿ (bega hushaaraagi), politely, or ಬೇಗ ಹುಷಾರಾಗು (bega hushaaraagu) to a friend; ಬೇಗ (bega) means soon and ಹುಷಾರು (hushaaru) means well. First ask how they are: ಈಗ ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀರಿ? (eega hegiddeeri?, how are you now?) or ಈಗ ಹೇಗಿದೆ? (eega hegide?, how is it now?). Add advice like ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿ ವಿಶ್ರಾಂತಿ ತಗೊಳ್ಳಿ (chennaagi vishraanti tagolli, take good rest) and ಮಾತ್ರೆ ತಗೊಳ್ಳಿ (maatre tagolli, take your tablets). "I am not well" is ನನಗೆ ಹುಷಾರಿಲ್ಲ (nanage hushaarilla).

## Comforting someone — ಚಿಂತೆ ಮಾಡಬೇಡಿ, ಎಲ್ಲಾ ಸರಿ ಹೋಗುತ್ತೆ

<!-- topic: comforting -->

To comfort someone in Kannada, say ಚಿಂತೆ ಮಾಡಬೇಡಿ (chinte maadabedi, don't worry) and ಎಲ್ಲಾ ಸರಿ ಹೋಗುತ್ತೆ (ellaa sari hogutte, everything will be all right). To a friend: ಬೇಜಾರ್ ಮಾಡ್ಕೋಬೇಡ (bejaar maadkobeda, don't feel bad) and ನಾನು ಇದ್ದೀನಿ (naanu iddeeni, I'm here for you). ಏನಾಯ್ತು? (enaaytu?, what happened?) invites them to talk. ಪರವಾಗಿಲ್ಲ (paravaagilla, it's okay) helps after a small failure. For grief, a simple ತುಂಬಾ ದುಃಖ ಆಯ್ತು (tumbaa duhkha aaytu, I'm very sorry) is kind and respectful.

## Talking about relationships — ಮದುವೆ (maduve)

<!-- topic: relationships; level: intermediate -->

In Kannada, marriage or a wedding is ಮದುವೆ (maduve). "Are you married?" is ನಿಮಗೆ ಮದುವೆ ಆಗಿದೆಯಾ? (nimage maduve aagideyaa?), literally "has marriage happened to you?"; answers: ಹೌದು, ಮದುವೆ ಆಗಿದೆ (haudu, maduve aagide, yes, I'm married) or ಇನ್ನೂ ಆಗಿಲ್ಲ (innoo aagilla, not yet). Husband is ಗಂಡ (ganda) and wife ಹೆಂಡತಿ (hendati); many people just say "my husband / my wife" in English. For dating, young people use English words: ಬಾಯ್‌ಫ್ರೆಂಡ್ (boyfrend), ಗರ್ಲ್‌ಫ್ರೆಂಡ್ (garlfrend). Personal questions like this are common from elders, so a smiling ಇನ್ನೂ ಇಲ್ಲ is fine.
