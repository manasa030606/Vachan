# Food and eating out in Kannada

## I am hungry — ನನಗೆ ಹಸಿವಾಗಿದೆ (nanage hasivaagide)

To say "I am hungry" in Kannada, say ನನಗೆ ಹಸಿವಾಗಿದೆ (nanage hasivaagide), literally "to me hunger has happened"; ಹಸಿವು (hasivu) means hunger. Do not say ನಾನು ಹಸಿವು — the person takes the -ಗೆ (to me) form. In everyday speech people also say ಹೊಟ್ಟೆ ಹಸಿತಿದೆ (hotte hasitide, my stomach is hungry) or ತುಂಬಾ ಹಸಿವಾಗ್ತಿದೆ (tumbaa hasivaagtide, I'm getting very hungry). To ask someone politely: ನಿಮಗೆ ಹಸಿವಾಗಿದೆಯಾ? (nimage hasivaagideyaa?, are you hungry?). A host will then usually say ಬನ್ನಿ, ಊಟ ಮಾಡೋಣ (banni, oota maadona, come, let's eat).

## I am thirsty — ನನಗೆ ಬಾಯಾರಿಕೆಯಾಗಿದೆ (nanage baayaarikeyaagide)

To say "I am thirsty" in Kannada, say ನನಗೆ ಬಾಯಾರಿಕೆಯಾಗಿದೆ (nanage baayaarikeyaagide), literally "to me thirst has happened"; ಬಾಯಾರಿಕೆ (baayaarike) comes from ಬಾಯಿ (baayi, mouth) + ಆರು (aaru, to dry). In everyday speech people usually just ask for water: ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ನೀರು ಕೊಡಿ (svalpa neeru kodi, some water please) or ನೀರು ಬೇಕು (neeru beku, I need water). In hot weather a cool drink of ಮಜ್ಜಿಗೆ (majjige, spiced buttermilk) or ಎಳನೀರು (elaneeru, tender coconut water) is popular. "Drinking water" is ಕುಡಿಯುವ ನೀರು (kudiyuva neeru).

## Have you eaten? — ಊಟ ಆಯ್ತಾ? (oota aaytaa?)

<!-- topic: everyday-conversation -->

"Have you eaten?" is one of the most common caring greetings in Kannada: ಊಟ ಆಯ್ತಾ? (oota aaytaa?), written ಊಟ ಆಯಿತಾ? (oota aayitaa?), literally "has the meal happened?". In the morning people ask ತಿಂಡಿ ಆಯ್ತಾ? (tindi aaytaa?, had breakfast?). It shows care and is not an invitation. Answer ಹಾ, ಆಯ್ತು (haa, aaytu, yes, done) or ಇನ್ನೂ ಇಲ್ಲ (innoo illa, not yet), and return the question: ನಿಮ್ದು? (nimdu?, and you?). Politely: ನೀವು ಊಟ ಮಾಡಿದ್ರಾ? (neevu oota maadidraa?, did you eat?).

Also asked as: how to ask have you eaten in Kannada, did you have lunch, did you have breakfast, oota aayta meaning

## Ordering at a restaurant — … ಕೊಡಿ (… kodi)

<!-- topic: restaurant -->

In a Kannada restaurant (called a ಹೋಟೆಲ್, hotel, in Karnataka), the waiter may ask ಏನು ಬೇಕು? (enu beku?) or ಏನ್ ಕೊಡ್ಲಿ? (en kodli?, what shall I bring?). Order by naming the dish with a number and ಕೊಡಿ (kodi, please give): ಒಂದು ಮಸಾಲೆ ದೋಸೆ ಕೊಡಿ (ondu masaale dose kodi, one masala dosa please); ಎರಡು ಕಾಫಿ ಕೊಡಿ (eradu kaafi kodi, two coffees). To see what's available: ಏನೇನು ಇದೆ? (enenu ide?, what all is there?) or ಮೆನು ಕೊಡಿ (menu kodi). For more: ಇನ್ನೊಂದು ಕೊಡಿ (innondu kodi, one more please). In a darshini, you order and pay first at the counter.

## The bill, please — ಬಿಲ್ ಕೊಡಿ (bil kodi)

<!-- topic: restaurant -->

To ask for the bill in a Kannada restaurant, say ಬಿಲ್ ಕೊಡಿ (bil kodi, please give the bill). To ask the total: ಎಷ್ಟು ಆಯ್ತು? (eshtu aaytu?, how much has it come to?), written ಎಷ್ಟು ಆಯಿತು? To ask about payment: ಯುಪಿಐ ಆಗುತ್ತಾ? (yupiai aaguttaa?, can I pay by UPI?) or ಕಾರ್ಡ್ ಆಗುತ್ತಾ? (kaard aaguttaa?). In a ದರ್ಶಿನಿ (darshini, self-service eatery) you pay first at the counter and get a token. To take food away, say ಪಾರ್ಸೆಲ್ ಮಾಡಿ (paarsel maadi, please pack it). Tipping is not expected in small eateries.

## Taste words — ರುಚಿ (ruchi)

<!-- type: vocabulary -->

Kannada taste words: ರುಚಿ (ruchi, taste), ಸಿಹಿ (sihi, sweet), ಖಾರ (khaara, spicy-hot), ಉಪ್ಪು (uppu, salt / salty), ಹುಳಿ (huli, sour), ಕಹಿ (kahi, bitter). To praise food: ತುಂಬಾ ರುಚಿಯಾಗಿದೆ (tumbaa ruchiyaagide, it's very tasty) or simply ತುಂಬಾ ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದೆ (tumbaa chennaagide, it's very nice). To comment politely: ಉಪ್ಪು ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ಜಾಸ್ತಿ (uppu svalpa jaasti, a bit too salty), ಉಪ್ಪು ಕಡಿಮೆ ಇದೆ (uppu kadime ide, it needs salt). Note ಹುಳಿ (huli) is also the name of a thick vegetable-lentil curry in Karnataka homes.

## Spicy or not spicy — ಖಾರ ಕಡಿಮೆ (khaara kadime)

<!-- topic: restaurant -->

To ask whether food is spicy in Kannada, say ಇದು ಖಾರ ಇದೆಯಾ? (idu khaara ideyaa?, is this spicy?); the answer may be ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ಖಾರ ಇದೆ (svalpa khaara ide, a little spicy). To ask for less: ಖಾರ ಕಡಿಮೆ ಹಾಕಿ (khaara kadime haaki, please make it less spicy) or ಖಾರ ಬೇಡ (khaara beda, no spice please). If something is too hot: ತುಂಬಾ ಖಾರ! (tumbaa khaara!, very spicy!). A quick fix is ಮೊಸರು (mosaru, curd) or ಮಜ್ಜಿಗೆ (majjige, buttermilk). ಖಾರ (khaara) is chilli heat; "hot to the touch" is a different word, ಬಿಸಿ (bisi).

Also asked as: how to say not spicy in Kannada, less spicy please, is it spicy, too hot, mild food

## I am vegetarian — ನಾನು ಸಸ್ಯಾಹಾರಿ (naanu sasyaahaari)

To say "I am vegetarian" in Kannada, say ನಾನು ಸಸ್ಯಾಹಾರಿ (naanu sasyaahaari); a non-vegetarian is ಮಾಂಸಾಹಾರಿ (maamsaahaari). In daily speech, everyone uses "veg" and "non-veg": ನಾನು ವೆಜ್ (naanu vej), ನಾನು ನಾನ್‌ವೆಜ್ ತಿನ್ನಲ್ಲ (naanu naanvej tinnalla, I don't eat non-veg). The careful form is ನಾನು ಮಾಂಸ ತಿನ್ನುವುದಿಲ್ಲ (naanu maamsa tinnuvudilla, I don't eat meat). To check: ಇದರಲ್ಲಿ ಮೊಟ್ಟೆ ಇದೆಯಾ? (idaralli motte ideyaa?, is there egg in this?). Many restaurants are fully vegetarian and display "veg" boards; ಮೀನು (meenu, fish) and ಚಿಕನ್ (chikan) are non-veg.

## It's tasty! — ತುಂಬಾ ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದೆ (tumbaa chennaagide)

<!-- topic: restaurant -->

To compliment food in Kannada, say ತುಂಬಾ ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದೆ (tumbaa chennaagide, it's really good) or ತುಂಬಾ ರುಚಿಯಾಗಿದೆ (tumbaa ruchiyaagide, it's very tasty). After the meal: ಊಟ ತುಂಬಾ ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿತ್ತು (oota tumbaa chennaagittu, the meal was very good) — note the past ಇತ್ತು (ittu, was). Praising the cook is warmly received at home: ನೀವು ತುಂಬಾ ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿ ಅಡುಗೆ ಮಾಡ್ತೀರಾ (neevu tumbaa chennaagi aduge maadteeraa, you cook really well). A friend might say ಸೂಪರ್! (soopar!) in casual speech.

Also asked as: how to say delicious in Kannada, the food is good, compliment the cook, it was tasty, yummy

## I'm full / enough — ಹೊಟ್ಟೆ ತುಂಬಿತು, ಸಾಕು (hotte tumbitu, saaku)

<!-- topic: food -->

In Karnataka, hosts keep serving, so learn to stop politely in Kannada. ಸಾಕು (saaku) means "enough": ಸಾಕು, ಸಾಕು! (saaku, saaku!), with a hand over your plate. ಹೊಟ್ಟೆ ತುಂಬಿತು (hotte tumbitu), literally "the stomach got filled", means "I'm full". Add a compliment so you do not seem to dislike the food: ಸಾಕು, ತುಂಬಾ ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿತ್ತು (saaku, tumbaa chennaagittu, enough, it was very good). If you want a little more: ಇನ್ನು ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ಹಾಕಿ (innu svalpa haaki, serve a little more). Hosts may insist with ಇನ್ನೂ ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ತಗೊಳ್ಳಿ (innoo svalpa tagolli, have a little more).

## Meals at home — ತಿಂಡಿ, ಊಟ (tindi, oota)

<!-- type: vocabulary; topic: daily-routine -->

In a Kannada home the day's meals are ಬೆಳಗಿನ ತಿಂಡಿ (belagina tindi, breakfast), ಮಧ್ಯಾಹ್ನದ ಊಟ (madhyaahnada oota, lunch) and ರಾತ್ರಿ ಊಟ (raatri oota, dinner); in context people just say ತಿಂಡಿ (tindi) and ಊಟ (oota). ತಿಂಡಿ also means any snack. A typical lunch is ಅನ್ನ (anna, rice) with ಸಾರು (saaru), ಪಲ್ಯ (palya, dry vegetable dish) and ಮೊಸರು (mosaru, curd), ending with ಮೊಸರನ್ನ (mosaranna, curd rice). "To have a meal" is ಊಟ ಮಾಡು (oota maadu): ಊಟ ಮಾಡಿ (oota maadi, please eat) is what hosts say. "The food is ready" is ಊಟ ರೆಡಿ (oota redi).

## Breakfast dishes in Karnataka

<!-- type: culture; topic: food -->

A Kannada breakfast (ತಿಂಡಿ, tindi) is usually a hot cooked dish. Favourites: ಇಡ್ಲಿ (idli, steamed rice cakes), ದೋಸೆ (dose, dosa — note the Kannada spelling), ಮಸಾಲೆ ದೋಸೆ (masaale dose, masala dosa), ಉಪ್ಪಿಟ್ಟು (uppittu, upma), ಅವಲಕ್ಕಿ (avalakki, flattened rice, poha), ಅಕ್ಕಿ ರೊಟ್ಟಿ (akki rotti, rice-flour flatbread) and ಬಿಸಿಬೇಳೆಬಾತ್ (bisibelebaat, spiced rice-lentil dish). They come with ಚಟ್ನಿ (chatni, chutney) and ಸಾಂಬಾರ್ (saambaar). Order one as ಒಂದು ಪ್ಲೇಟ್ ಇಡ್ಲಿ ಕೊಡಿ (ondu plet idli kodi, one plate of idli please). Filter coffee usually follows.

## Coffee and tea — ಕಾಫಿ, ಟೀ (kaafi, tee)

<!-- topic: drinks -->

Karnataka is coffee country, so in Kannada homes and Bengaluru darshinis the default hot drink is ಫಿಲ್ಟರ್ ಕಾಫಿ (filtar kaafi, filter coffee) in a steel tumbler. Order: ಒಂದು ಕಾಫಿ ಕೊಡಿ (ondu kaafi kodi, one coffee please) or ಒಂದು ಟೀ ಕೊಡಿ (ondu tee kodi); ಚಹಾ (chahaa) is the word for tea in North Karnataka. Sugar is usually added already, so say ಸಕ್ಕರೆ ಹಾಕಬೇಡಿ (sakkare haakabedi, no sugar please) or ಸಕ್ಕರೆ ಕಡಿಮೆ ಹಾಕಿ (sakkare kadime haaki, less sugar). Guests are offered ಕಾಫಿ ಕುಡಿಯುತ್ತೀರಾ? (kaafi kudiyutteeraa?, will you have coffee?); accepting is polite.

Also asked as: how to order coffee in Kannada, tea in Kannada, no sugar please, filter coffee, would you like coffee

## Inviting someone to eat — ಊಟಕ್ಕೆ ಬನ್ನಿ (ootakke banni)

<!-- topic: invitations -->

To invite someone for a meal in Kannada, say ಊಟಕ್ಕೆ ಬನ್ನಿ (ootakke banni, please come for a meal) or, for a home invitation, ನಾಳೆ ನಮ್ಮ ಮನೆಗೆ ಊಟಕ್ಕೆ ಬನ್ನಿ (naale namma manege ootakke banni, come to our house for a meal tomorrow). When food is served: ಊಟ ಮಾಡಿ (oota maadi, please eat) or ಬನ್ನಿ, ಊಟ ಮಾಡೋಣ (banni, oota maadona, come, let's eat). To a friend: ಬಾ, ತಿನ್ನೋಣ (baa, tinnona, come, let's eat). It is polite to wait until the host says ಶುರು ಮಾಡಿ (shuru maadi, please start).

Also asked as: how to invite someone for dinner in Kannada, come and eat, let's eat, lunch invitation, please eat

## Food shopping words — ತರಕಾರಿ, ಹಣ್ಣು (tarakaari, hannu)

<!-- type: vocabulary; topic: shopping -->

Common Kannada food-shopping words: ತರಕಾರಿ (tarakaari, vegetables), ಹಣ್ಣು (hannu, fruit), ಅಕ್ಕಿ (akki, raw rice), ಬೇಳೆ (bele, lentils), ಹಾಲು (haalu, milk), ಮೊಸರು (mosaru, curd), ಈರುಳ್ಳಿ (eerulli, onion), ಟೊಮೆಟೊ (tometo, tomato), ಆಲೂಗಡ್ಡೆ (aaloogadde, potato), ಬಾಳೆಹಣ್ಣು (baalehannu, banana), ತೆಂಗಿನಕಾಯಿ (tenginakaayi, coconut). Ask: ಟೊಮೆಟೊ ಕೆಜಿಗೆ ಎಷ್ಟು? (tometo kejige eshtu?, how much per kilo of tomatoes?). Order: ಅರ್ಧ ಕೆಜಿ ಈರುಳ್ಳಿ ಕೊಡಿ (ardha keji eerulli kodi, half a kilo of onions please).
