# Everyday life in Kannada

## Daily routine — ಬೆಳಿಗ್ಗೆ ಏಳುತ್ತೇನೆ (beligge eluttene)

To describe your morning in Kannada, use the present-habitual verb: ನಾನು ಬೆಳಿಗ್ಗೆ ಆರು ಗಂಟೆಗೆ ಏಳುತ್ತೇನೆ (naanu beligge aaru gantege eluttene, I get up at six in the morning), spoken ಏಳ್ತೀನಿ (elteeni). Then: ಹಲ್ಲುಜ್ಜುತ್ತೇನೆ (hallujjuttene, I brush my teeth), ಸ್ನಾನ ಮಾಡುತ್ತೇನೆ (snaana maaduttene, I bathe), ತಿಂಡಿ ತಿನ್ನುತ್ತೇನೆ (tindi tinnuttene, I eat breakfast), ಕಾಲೇಜಿಗೆ ಹೋಗುತ್ತೇನೆ (kaalejige hoguttene, I go to college). Kannada links steps with the -ಇ / -ಉ form: ಸ್ನಾನ ಮಾಡಿ, ತಿಂಡಿ ತಿಂದು ಕಾಲೇಜಿಗೆ ಹೋಗುತ್ತೇನೆ (after bathing and eating breakfast, I go to college). "Every day" is ಪ್ರತಿದಿನ (pratidina), spoken ದಿನಾ (dinaa).

Also asked as: daily routine in Kannada, what I do every morning, I wake up at six, describe my day

## Evening and night — ರಾತ್ರಿ ಮಲಗುತ್ತೇನೆ (raatri malaguttene)

<!-- topic: daily-routine -->

To describe your evening in Kannada: ಸಂಜೆ ಮನೆಗೆ ಬರುತ್ತೇನೆ (sanje manege baruttene, I come home in the evening), ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ರೆಸ್ಟ್ ಮಾಡುತ್ತೇನೆ (svalpa rest maaduttene, I rest a little), ಟಿವಿ ನೋಡುತ್ತೇನೆ (tivi noduttene, I watch TV), ರಾತ್ರಿ ಊಟ ಮಾಡುತ್ತೇನೆ (raatri oota maaduttene, I have dinner), ಹತ್ತು ಗಂಟೆಗೆ ಮಲಗುತ್ತೇನೆ (hattu gantege malaguttene, I go to bed at ten). ಮಲಗು (malagu) means to lie down / go to bed; ನಿದ್ದೆ (nidde) means sleep: ನಿದ್ದೆ ಬರ್ತಿದೆ (nidde bartide, I'm sleepy). At bedtime, family members may say ಶುಭ ರಾತ್ರಿ (shubha raatri, good night) or simply "good night".

Also asked as: evening routine in Kannada, I go to bed at ten, I'm sleepy, at night, after work

## At home — rooms and things — ಮನೆ (mane)

<!-- type: vocabulary; topic: home -->

Kannada words for the home: ಮನೆ (mane, house), ಅಡುಗೆ ಮನೆ (aduge mane, kitchen), ಮಲಗುವ ಕೋಣೆ (malaguva kone, bedroom; people also say ಬೆಡ್‌ರೂಮ್, bedroom), ಹಾಲ್ (haal, living room), ಬಾತ್ರೂಮ್ (baatroom, bathroom), ಬಾಗಿಲು (baagilu, door), ಕಿಟಕಿ (kitaki, window), ಮಹಡಿ (mahadi, floor / upstairs). Useful phrases: ಬಾಗಿಲು ಮುಚ್ಚಿ (baagilu mucchi, please close the door), ಲೈಟ್ ಹಾಕಿ (lait haaki, please switch on the light), ಫ್ಯಾನ್ ಆಫ್ ಮಾಡಿ (fyaan aaf maadi, please switch off the fan). Remember to take off footwear at the door.

Also asked as: rooms of the house in Kannada, kitchen, bedroom, door and window, switch on the light

## Household chores — ಮನೆ ಕೆಲಸ (mane kelasa)

<!-- topic: home -->

Household chores in Kannada are ಮನೆ ಕೆಲಸ (mane kelasa). Common ones: ಅಡುಗೆ ಮಾಡು (aduge maadu, cook), ಪಾತ್ರೆ ತೊಳೆ (paatre tole, wash the dishes), ಬಟ್ಟೆ ಒಗೆ (batte oge, wash clothes), ಕಸ ಗುಡಿಸು (kasa gudisu, sweep), ಮನೆ ಕ್ಲೀನ್ ಮಾಡು (mane kleen maadu, clean the house), ತರಕಾರಿ ತರು (tarakaari taru, bring vegetables). Offering help is appreciated: ನಾನು ಸಹಾಯ ಮಾಡಲಾ? (naanu sahaaya maadalaa?, shall I help?). Example: ಇವತ್ತು ನಾನು ಪಾತ್ರೆ ತೊಳೆಯುತ್ತೇನೆ (ivattu naanu paatre toleyuttene, I'll wash the dishes today).

Also asked as: chores in Kannada, wash the dishes, do the laundry, clean the house, shall I help

## Talking about the weather — ಮಳೆ, ಚಳಿ, ಸೆಕೆ (male, chali, seke)

<!-- topic: weather -->

Kannada weather talk uses nouns with ಇದೆ (ide, is there) or ಬರು (baru, come): ಮಳೆ ಬರ್ತಿದೆ (male bartide, it's raining), ಇವತ್ತು ಚಳಿ ಇದೆ (ivattu chali ide, it's cold today), ತುಂಬಾ ಸೆಕೆ ಇದೆ (tumbaa seke ide, it's very hot and humid), ಬಿಸಿಲು ಜಾಸ್ತಿ (bisilu jaasti, the sun is strong). Bengaluru is known for pleasant weather: ಬೆಂಗಳೂರಿನಲ್ಲಿ ಹವಾಮಾನ ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದೆ (bengaloorinalli havaamaana chennaagide, the weather is nice in Bengaluru). Take an umbrella: ಕೊಡೆ ತಗೊಳ್ಳಿ (kode tagolli). "Will it rain?" is ಮಳೆ ಬರುತ್ತಾ? (male baruttaa?).

Also asked as: weather in Kannada, it's raining, it's hot today, it's cold, talking about the weather

## On the phone — ಫೋನ್ ಮಾಡು (fon maadu)

<!-- topic: phone -->

Phone phrases in Kannada: ಹಲೋ? (halo?) to answer; ಯಾರು ಮಾತಾಡ್ತಿರೋದು? (yaaru maataadtirodu?, who's speaking?); ನಾನು ಆಶಾ ಮಾತಾಡ್ತಿರೋದು (naanu Asha maataadtirodu, this is Asha speaking). "To call" is ಫೋನ್ ಮಾಡು (fon maadu) or ಕಾಲ್ ಮಾಡು (kaal maadu): ಆಮೇಲೆ ಫೋನ್ ಮಾಡ್ತೀನಿ (aamele fon maadteeni, I'll call later). Problems: ಕೇಳಿಸ್ತಿಲ್ಲ (kelistilla, I can't hear), ನೆಟ್‌ವರ್ಕ್ ಇಲ್ಲ (netvark illa, no network), ಚಾರ್ಜ್ ಇಲ್ಲ (chaarj illa, the battery is dead). "Send a message" is ಮೆಸೇಜ್ ಮಾಡಿ (mesej maadi). People end calls with ಸರಿ, ಇಡ್ತೀನಿ (sari, idteeni, okay, I'll hang up).

Also asked as: phone call in Kannada, who is speaking, I'll call you later, I can't hear you, send a message

## Time with friends — ಹೊರಗೆ ಹೋಗೋಣ (horage hogona)

<!-- topic: friends -->

To make plans with friends in Kannada, use the "let's" form -ಓಣ (-ona): ಹೊರಗೆ ಹೋಗೋಣ (horage hogona, let's go out), ಕಾಫಿ ಕುಡಿಯೋಣ (kaafi kudiyona, let's have coffee), ಸಿನಿಮಾ ನೋಡೋಣ (sinimaa nodona, let's watch a film), ಕ್ರಿಕೆಟ್ ಆಡೋಣ (kriket aadona, let's play cricket). Ask: ಇವತ್ತು ಸಂಜೆ ಫ್ರೀ ಇದ್ದೀಯಾ? (ivattu sanje free iddeeyaa?, are you free this evening?). Friends greet each other with ಏನ್ ಸಮಾಚಾರ? (en samaachaara?, what's up?) or, in Bengaluru slang, ಏನ್ ಗುರು? (en guru?). Friends use ನೀನು (neenu) with each other.

Also asked as: making plans with friends in Kannada, let's go out, are you free this evening, hanging out, what's up

## Invitations — ನಮ್ಮ ಮನೆಗೆ ಬನ್ನಿ (namma manege banni)

<!-- topic: invitations -->

To invite someone home in Kannada, say ನಮ್ಮ ಮನೆಗೆ ಬನ್ನಿ (namma manege banni, please come to our house) — Kannada says "our house", not "my house". Add a time or reason: ಭಾನುವಾರ ಊಟಕ್ಕೆ ಬನ್ನಿ (bhaanuvaara ootakke banni, come for a meal on Sunday), ನನ್ನ ಹುಟ್ಟುಹಬ್ಬಕ್ಕೆ ಬನ್ನಿ (nanna huttuhabbakke banni, come to my birthday). To a friend: ನಮ್ಮ ಮನೆಗೆ ಬಾ (baa). Accepting: ಸರಿ, ಖಂಡಿತ ಬರ್ತೀನಿ (sari, khandita barteeni, sure, I'll definitely come). Declining: ಸಾರಿ, ನನಗೆ ಬರೋಕೆ ಆಗಲ್ಲ (saari, nanage baroke aagalla, sorry, I can't come).

Also asked as: how to invite someone in Kannada, come to my house, accepting an invitation, declining politely

## Visiting someone's home — ಬನ್ನಿ ಬನ್ನಿ (banni banni)

<!-- type: culture; topic: etiquette -->

When you visit a Kannada home, the host welcomes you with ಬನ್ನಿ ಬನ್ನಿ, ಒಳಗೆ ಬನ್ನಿ (banni banni, olage banni, come in, come in) and ಕುಳಿತುಕೊಳ್ಳಿ (kulitukolli, please sit). Soon comes ಕಾಫಿ ಕುಡಿಯುತ್ತೀರಾ? (kaafi kudiyutteeraa?, will you have coffee?) — accepting something small is polite. Take off your footwear at the door. Hosts ask about family: ಮನೆಯಲ್ಲಿ ಎಲ್ಲಾ ಆರಾಮಾ? (maneyalli ellaa aaraamaa?, all well at home?). When leaving, say ಹೋಗಿ ಬರ್ತೀನಿ (hogi barteeni, I'll go and come back); the host replies ಹೋಗಿ ಬನ್ನಿ (hogi banni) and often ಮತ್ತೆ ಬನ್ನಿ (matte banni, come again).

Also asked as: visiting a Kannada home, what the host says, guest etiquette, come in please sit, come again

## Where are you off to? — ಎಲ್ಲಿಗೆ ಹೊರಟಿದ್ದೀರಿ? (ellige horatiddeeri?)

<!-- topic: everyday-conversation -->

Neighbours in Karnataka greet each other with ಎಲ್ಲಿಗೆ ಹೊರಟಿದ್ದೀರಿ? (ellige horatiddeeri?, where are you off to?) or just ಎಲ್ಲಿಗೆ? (ellige?) — a friendly greeting, not nosiness; ಹೊರಡು (horadu) means to set off. A vague answer is fine: ಇಲ್ಲೇ, ಅಂಗಡಿಗೆ (ille, angadige, just to the shop) or ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ಕೆಲಸ ಇದೆ (svalpa kelasa ide, I have a small errand). Coming back, you may hear ಬಂದ್ರಾ? (bandraa?, you're back?). If you step out briefly, say ಈಗ ಬಂದೆ (eega bande, I'll be right back — literally "I came now").

Also asked as: neighbour greetings in Kannada, where are you off to, I'll be right back, just going to the shop

## I'm busy / I'm free — ಬ್ಯುಸಿ, ಫ್ರೀ (byusi, free)

<!-- topic: everyday-conversation -->

To say you are busy in Kannada, people use English words: ನಾನು ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ಬ್ಯುಸಿ ಇದ್ದೀನಿ (naanu svalpa byusi iddeeni, I'm a bit busy); the Kannada alternative is ನನಗೆ ಕೆಲಸ ಇದೆ (nanage kelasa ide, I have work). "I'm free" is ನಾನು ಫ್ರೀ ಇದ್ದೀನಿ (naanu free iddeeni); the native word for free time is ಬಿಡುವು (biduvu): ನಾಳೆ ನಿಮಗೆ ಬಿಡುವು ಇದೆಯಾ? (naale nimage biduvu ideyaa?, are you free tomorrow?). "I don't have time" is ನನಗೆ ಟೈಮ್ ಇಲ್ಲ (nanage taim illa). "Call me when you're free" is ಫ್ರೀ ಆದಾಗ ಫೋನ್ ಮಾಡಿ (free aadaaga fon maadi).

Also asked as: I'm busy in Kannada, are you free, I don't have time, free time, call me when you're free

## Power cuts and water — ಕರೆಂಟ್ ಇಲ್ಲ (karent illa)

<!-- topic: home; level: intermediate -->

In Karnataka homes you will often hear ಕರೆಂಟ್ ಇಲ್ಲ (karent illa, there's no power) — electricity is called ಕರೆಂಟ್ (karent); the formal word is ವಿದ್ಯುತ್ (vidyut). ಕರೆಂಟ್ ಹೋಯ್ತು (karent hoytu) = the power went; ಕರೆಂಟ್ ಬಂತು (karent bantu) = the power is back. For water: ನೀರು ಬರ್ತಿಲ್ಲ (neeru bartilla, the water isn't coming) and ಟ್ಯಾಂಕ್ ಖಾಲಿ ಆಗಿದೆ (tyaank khaali aagide, the tank is empty). Internet trouble: ವೈಫೈ ಕೆಲಸ ಮಾಡ್ತಿಲ್ಲ (vaifai kelasa maadtilla, the wifi isn't working).

Also asked as: power cut in Kannada, no electricity, the water isn't coming, the wifi is not working

## Family time — ಮನೆಯವರ ಜೊತೆ (maneyavara jote)

<!-- topic: family -->

In Kannada, family is usually called ಮನೆಯವರು (maneyavaru, the people at home). "With family" is ಮನೆಯವರ ಜೊತೆ (maneyavara jote): ಭಾನುವಾರ ಮನೆಯವರ ಜೊತೆ ಇರ್ತೀನಿ (bhaanuvaara maneyavara jote irteeni, I spend Sundays with my family). Asking about someone's family is warm and common: ಮನೆಯಲ್ಲಿ ಎಲ್ಲರೂ ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದ್ದಾರಾ? (maneyalli ellaroo chennaagiddaaraa?, is everyone at home well?). Answer ಎಲ್ಲರೂ ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದ್ದಾರೆ (ellaroo chennaagiddaare, everyone is fine). Talking about relatives: ನಮ್ಮ ಅಜ್ಜಿ ಮೈಸೂರಿನಲ್ಲಿ ಇರುತ್ತಾರೆ (namma ajji maisoorinalli iruttaare, my grandmother lives in Mysuru).

Also asked as: family time in Kannada, how is your family, everyone at home well, with my family

## What did you do today? — ಇವತ್ತು ಏನು ಮಾಡಿದಿರಿ? (ivattu enu maadidiri?)

<!-- topic: past-tense -->

To ask about someone's day in Kannada, say ಇವತ್ತು ಏನು ಮಾಡಿದಿರಿ? (ivattu enu maadidiri?, what did you do today?), spoken ಏನ್ ಮಾಡಿದ್ರಿ? (en maadidri?); to a friend, ಇವತ್ತು ಏನ್ ಮಾಡಿದೆ? (ivattu en maadide?). Answer in the past tense with -ಎ for "I": ನಾನು ಮಾರ್ಕೆಟ್‌ಗೆ ಹೋದೆ (naanu maarketge hode, I went to the market), ಸ್ನೇಹಿತರನ್ನು ನೋಡಿದೆ (snehitarannu nodide, I met friends), ಮನೆಯಲ್ಲೇ ಇದ್ದೆ (maneyalle idde, I stayed at home). A common casual reply is ಏನಿಲ್ಲ (enilla, nothing much). "How was your day?" is ದಿನ ಹೇಗಿತ್ತು? (dina hegittu?).

Also asked as: what did you do today in Kannada, how was your day, I stayed at home, I went to the market
