# Kannada sentence patterns

## I want … — ನನಗೆ … ಬೇಕು (nanage … beku)

<!-- type: phrase; topic: wanting -->

In Kannada, "I want" is said as "to me … is wanted": ನನಗೆ (nanage, to me) + the thing + ಬೇಕು (beku, wanted/needed). ನನಗೆ ಒಂದು ಕಾಫಿ ಬೇಕು (nanage ondu kaafi beku) = I want a coffee. ನನಗೆ ನೀರು ಬೇಕು (nanage neeru beku) = I want water. To ask someone politely: ನಿಮಗೆ ಏನು ಬೇಕು? (nimage enu beku?, what do you want?); to a friend: ನಿನಗೆ ಏನು ಬೇಕು? (ninage enu beku?). In fast speech ನನಗೆ often sounds like ನಂಗೆ (nange). The same ಬೇಕು after a verb means "want to / need to": ನನಗೆ ಮಲಗಬೇಕು (nanage malagabeku, I want to sleep).

## I don't want … — ನನಗೆ … ಬೇಡ (nanage … beda)

<!-- type: phrase; topic: wanting -->

The opposite of ಬೇಕು (beku, wanted) in Kannada is ಬೇಡ (beda, not wanted). ನನಗೆ ಸಕ್ಕರೆ ಬೇಡ (nanage sakkare beda) = I don't want sugar. On its own, ಬೇಡ is the normal polite way to refuse an offer: ಬೇಡ, ಧನ್ಯವಾದ (beda, dhanyavaada) = no, thank you. Hosts in Karnataka often insist, so you may need to repeat it with a smile, or say ಸಾಕು (saaku, enough). After a verb, -ಬೇಡಿ (-bedi, polite) or -ಬೇಡ (-beda, casual) means "don't": ಹೋಗಬೇಡಿ (hogabedi, please don't go).

## I have to … / I need to … — … ಬೇಕು (… beku)

<!-- topic: obligation -->

To say "I have to / I must / I need to" in Kannada, add -ಬೇಕು (-beku) to the verb stem: ನಾನು ಆಫೀಸಿಗೆ ಹೋಗಬೇಕು (naanu aafeesige hogabeku) = I have to go to the office. ನಾನು ಓದಬೇಕು (naanu odabeku) = I have to study. ಬೇಕು never changes for the person: ಅವನು ಹೋಗಬೇಕು (he has to go), ನೀವು ಬರಬೇಕು (you must come). In speech the vowel before ಬೇಕು is often dropped: ಹೋಗ್ಬೇಕು (hogbeku). "I don't have to" uses -ಬೇಕಿಲ್ಲ (-bekilla): ನಾಳೆ ಬರಬೇಕಿಲ್ಲ (naale barabekilla, you don't have to come tomorrow). For things you need, use ನನಗೆ … ಬೇಕು: ನನಗೆ ಸಹಾಯ ಬೇಕು (nanage sahaaya beku, I need help).

## I have … (with me) — ನನ್ನ ಹತ್ತಿರ … ಇದೆ (nanna hattira … ide)

<!-- type: phrase; topic: possession -->

Kannada has no verb "to have". For things you have with you, say "near me … is there": ನನ್ನ ಹತ್ತಿರ ಪೆನ್ನು ಇದೆ (nanna hattira pennu ide) = I have a pen. ನನ್ನ ಹತ್ತಿರ ದುಡ್ಡು ಇಲ್ಲ (nanna hattira duddu illa) = I don't have money. To ask: ನಿಮ್ಮ ಹತ್ತಿರ ಚಿಲ್ಲರೆ ಇದೆಯಾ? (nimma hattira chillare ideyaa?, do you have change?). For family, time, illness and events, use ನನಗೆ (nanage, to me) instead: ನನಗೆ ಒಬ್ಬ ತಮ್ಮ ಇದ್ದಾನೆ (nanage obba tamma iddaane, I have a younger brother), ನನಗೆ ಜ್ವರ ಇದೆ (nanage jvara ide, I have a fever). Formal writing uses ನನ್ನ ಬಳಿ (nanna bali) for "with me".

## I like … — ನನಗೆ … ಇಷ್ಟ (nanage … ishta)

<!-- type: phrase; topic: likes -->

In Kannada, "I like" is "to me … is liked": ನನಗೆ ಕಾಫಿ ಇಷ್ಟ (nanage kaafi ishta) = I like coffee. "Very much" is ತುಂಬಾ (tumbaa): ನನಗೆ ಬೆಂಗಳೂರು ತುಂಬಾ ಇಷ್ಟ (I like Bengaluru a lot). To like doing something, use the -ಉವುದು (-uvudu) form of the verb: ನನಗೆ ಓದುವುದು ಇಷ್ಟ (nanage oduvudu ishta, I like reading), spoken ಓದೋದು (ododu). Negative: ನನಗೆ ಇದು ಇಷ್ಟ ಇಲ್ಲ (nanage idu ishta illa, I don't like this). Question: ನಿಮಗೆ ಕ್ರಿಕೆಟ್ ಇಷ್ಟವಾ? (nimage kriket ishtavaa?, do you like cricket?). "I liked it" (after trying something) is ಇಷ್ಟ ಆಯ್ತು (ishta aaytu).

## I can … / I can't … — ಬರುತ್ತದೆ, ಆಗುತ್ತದೆ

<!-- topic: ability -->

Kannada has two ways to say "can". For a skill you know, it "comes" to you: ನನಗೆ ಈಜಲು ಬರುತ್ತದೆ (nanage eejalu baruttade, I can swim), spoken ಈಜೋಕೆ ಬರುತ್ತೆ (eejoke barutte). ನನಗೆ ಕನ್ನಡ ಬರುತ್ತದೆ (nanage kannada baruttade) = I can speak Kannada. For whether something is possible, use ಆಗು (aagu, to happen): ನನಗೆ ನಾಳೆ ಬರಲು ಆಗುತ್ತದೆ (nanage naale baralu aaguttade, I can come tomorrow). The negative is very common: ನನಗೆ ಬರಲು ಆಗುವುದಿಲ್ಲ (nanage baralu aaguvudilla, I can't come), spoken ನನಗೆ ಬರೋಕೆ ಆಗಲ್ಲ (nanage baroke aagalla). Note the verb takes -ಅಲು (-alu), spoken -ಓಕೆ (-oke).

## Can I …? May I …? — … ಬಹುದಾ? (… bahudaa?)

<!-- type: phrase; topic: permission -->

To ask permission in Kannada, add -ಬಹುದಾ? (-bahudaa?) to the verb stem: ಒಳಗೆ ಬರಬಹುದಾ? (olage barabahudaa?) = may I come in? ಇಲ್ಲಿ ಕೂರಬಹುದಾ? (illi koorabahudaa?) = can I sit here? ಫೋಟೋ ತೆಗೆಯಬಹುದಾ? (foto tegeyabahudaa?) = can I take a photo? The formal written form ends in -ಬಹುದೇ? (-bahude?). The usual "yes" is ಹೂಂ, ಬನ್ನಿ (hoom, banni, yes, come) or ಪರವಾಗಿಲ್ಲ (paravaagilla, it's fine); "no" is ಬೇಡ (beda, please don't). Without the question, -ಬಹುದು (-bahudu) means "may / might": ಮಳೆ ಬರಬಹುದು (male barabahudu, it may rain).

Also asked as: how to ask permission in Kannada, can I come in, may I sit here, is it okay if I, can I take a photo

## I am going to … (plans) — … ಅಂತ ಇದ್ದೀನಿ

<!-- topic: future-plans; level: intermediate -->

Kannada uses the same verb form for "I do" and "I will do", so ನಾನು ನಾಳೆ ಮೈಸೂರಿಗೆ ಹೋಗುತ್ತೇನೆ (naanu naale maisoorige hoguttene) = I am going to / will go to Mysuru tomorrow; spoken ಹೋಗ್ತೀನಿ (hogteeni). A time word (ನಾಳೆ, tomorrow; ಆಮೇಲೆ, later) makes the future clear. To talk about a plan or intention, people say "-ಬೇಕು ಅಂತ ಇದ್ದೀನಿ" (I am thinking that I should …): ಮುಂದಿನ ವಾರ ಮೈಸೂರಿಗೆ ಹೋಗಬೇಕು ಅಂತ ಇದ್ದೀನಿ (mundina vaara maisoorige hogabeku anta iddeeni, I'm planning to go to Mysuru next week). ಅಂತ (anta) is the spoken quote word "that"; the written form is ಎಂದು (endu).

## There is … / there isn't … — ಇದೆ / ಇಲ್ಲ

<!-- type: phrase; topic: existence -->

In Kannada, "there is" is ಇದೆ (ide) and "there isn't" is ಇಲ್ಲ (illa), placed at the end: ಇಲ್ಲಿ ಹತ್ತಿರ ಒಂದು ಬ್ಯಾಂಕ್ ಇದೆ (illi hattira ondu byaank ide) = there is a bank near here. ಇವತ್ತು ಕ್ಲಾಸ್ ಇಲ್ಲ (ivattu klaas illa) = there is no class today. For people, use ಇದ್ದಾರೆ (iddaare, they are there): ಮನೆಯಲ್ಲಿ ಅಮ್ಮ ಇದ್ದಾರೆ (amma is at home). "Nobody is there" = ಯಾರೂ ಇಲ್ಲ (yaaroo illa). To ask "is there …?", add -ಆ: ಹಾಲು ಇದೆಯಾ? (haalu ideyaa?, is there milk?); written form ಇದೆಯೇ? (ideye?).

## Let's … — -ಓಣ (-ona)

<!-- type: phrase; topic: suggestions -->

To say "let's" in Kannada, add -ಓಣ (-ona) to the verb: ಹೋಗೋಣ (hogona, let's go), ತಿನ್ನೋಣ (tinnona, let's eat), ಕಾಫಿ ಕುಡಿಯೋಣ (kaafi kudiyona, let's have coffee), ಸಿನಿಮಾ ನೋಡೋಣ (sinimaa nodona, let's watch a film), ಸಿಗೋಣ (sigona, let's meet). Add -ಆ to make it a suggestion question: ಹೋಗೋಣವಾ? (hogonavaa?, shall we go?). It is often started with ಬಾ (baa, come) to a friend or ಬನ್ನಿ (banni) politely: ಬನ್ನಿ, ಊಟ ಮಾಡೋಣ (banni, oota maadona, come, let's eat). ನೋಡೋಣ (nodona) also means "we'll see", a polite way to avoid saying no.

Also asked as: how to say let's go in Kannada, shall we, let's eat, making suggestions, let us

## X is Y — no verb needed

<!-- topic: copula -->

In simple Kannada sentences of the type "X is Y", there is no word for "is": ಇದು ನನ್ನ ಮನೆ (idu nanna mane) = this is my house; ಅವರು ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್ (avaru daaktar) = he/she is a doctor; ನಾನು ವಿದ್ಯಾರ್ಥಿ (naanu vidyaarthi) = I am a student. To describe how something is right now, use ಇದೆ (ide) joined with -ಆಗಿ: ಊಟ ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದೆ (oota chennaagide, the food is good), ಕಾಫಿ ಬಿಸಿಯಾಗಿದೆ (kaafi bisiyaagide, the coffee is hot). A describing word can also stand as "the … one": ಈ ಮನೆ ದೊಡ್ಡದು (ee mane doddadu, this house is a big one). Do not add ಇದೆ to "this is my book": ಇದು ನನ್ನ ಪುಸ್ತಕ is correct as it is.

Also asked as: how to say is in Kannada, this is my house, I am a student, Kannada without is, the food is good

## X is not Y — ಅಲ್ಲ (alla)

<!-- topic: negation -->

To say "X is not Y" in Kannada, put ಅಲ್ಲ (alla) at the end: ಇದು ನನ್ನ ಬ್ಯಾಗ್ ಅಲ್ಲ (idu nanna byaag alla) = this is not my bag; ನಾನು ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್ ಅಲ್ಲ (naanu daaktar alla) = I am not a doctor. ಅಲ್ಲ denies what something is. Do not confuse it with ಇಲ್ಲ (illa), which denies that something exists: ಇಲ್ಲಿ ನೀರು ಇಲ್ಲ (illi neeru illa, there is no water here). A handy test: if English could say "there is no …", use ಇಲ್ಲ; if it says "it is not …", use ಅಲ್ಲ. The spoken tag ಅಲ್ವಾ? (alvaa?, isn't it?) comes from ಅಲ್ಲ: ಇದು ನಿಮ್ಮ ಪೆನ್ನು, ಅಲ್ವಾ? (this is your pen, isn't it?).

Also asked as: how to say is not in Kannada, alla vs illa, this is not mine, I am not, negative sentences

## Negative verbs: I don't / I won't / I didn't

<!-- topic: negation; level: intermediate -->

Kannada verbs have two main negatives. For "I don't / I won't", add -ಉವುದಿಲ್ಲ (-uvudilla) to the verb: ನಾನು ಮಾಂಸ ತಿನ್ನುವುದಿಲ್ಲ (naanu maamsa tinnuvudilla, I don't eat meat); ನಾನು ನಾಳೆ ಬರುವುದಿಲ್ಲ (naanu naale baruvudilla, I won't come tomorrow). In speech this shrinks to -ಅಲ್ಲ: ತಿನ್ನಲ್ಲ (tinnalla), ಬರಲ್ಲ (baralla). For "I didn't", add -ಅಲಿಲ್ಲ (-alilla): ನಾನು ತಿನ್ನಲಿಲ್ಲ (naanu tinnalilla, I didn't eat). For "I am not doing (right now)", use -ಉತ್ತಿಲ್ಲ: ಮಳೆ ಬರುತ್ತಿಲ್ಲ (male baruttilla, it isn't raining). These negative forms are the same for every person: ಅವಳು ಬರುವುದಿಲ್ಲ (she won't come), ಅವರು ಬರಲಿಲ್ಲ (they didn't come).

Also asked as: how to say I don't in Kannada, I didn't eat, I won't come, negative verbs, not doing

## Don't … — -ಬೇಡಿ (-bedi)

<!-- type: phrase; topic: imperative -->

To tell someone "don't" in Kannada, add -ಬೇಡಿ (-bedi) to the verb stem for polite speech and -ಬೇಡ (-beda) for friends and children. ಚಿಂತೆ ಮಾಡಬೇಡಿ (chinte maadabedi) = don't worry; ಹೆದರಬೇಡಿ (hedarabedi) = don't be scared; ಇಲ್ಲಿ ಕಸ ಹಾಕಬೇಡಿ (illi kasa haakabedi) = don't throw rubbish here, a common sign. To a friend: ಹೋಗಬೇಡ (hogabeda, don't go), ಮರೀಬೇಡ (mareebeda, don't forget). In speech the vowel before ಬೇಡ is often dropped: ಮಾಡ್ಬೇಡಿ (maadbedi). Signs in formal Kannada may use -ಬಾರದು (-baaradu, must not): ಧೂಮಪಾನ ಮಾಡಬಾರದು (dhoomapaana maadabaaradu, no smoking).

Also asked as: how to say don't in Kannada, don't worry, don't go, telling someone not to, prohibition

## I think … — … ಅಂತ ಅನ್ಸುತ್ತೆ (… anta ansutte)

<!-- type: phrase; topic: opinions; level: intermediate -->

To say "I think" in Kannada, put the thought first, then ಅಂತ (anta, that) and ಅನ್ಸುತ್ತೆ (ansutte, it seems): ಇವತ್ತು ಮಳೆ ಬರುತ್ತೆ ಅಂತ ಅನ್ಸುತ್ತೆ (ivattu male barutte anta ansutte) = I think it will rain today. You can add ನನಗೆ (nanage, to me) in front. The written form is ಎಂದು ಅನಿಸುತ್ತದೆ (endu anisuttade). Shorter ways to give an opinion: ನನ್ನ ಪ್ರಕಾರ (nanna prakaara, in my opinion) and ಬಹುಶಃ (bahushah, probably): ಬಹುಶಃ ಅವರು ನಾಳೆ ಬರುತ್ತಾರೆ (they will probably come tomorrow). To ask someone's opinion: ನಿಮಗೆ ಏನು ಅನ್ಸುತ್ತೆ? (nimage enu ansutte?, what do you think?).

## I don't like … — ನನಗೆ … ಇಷ್ಟ ಇಲ್ಲ (nanage … ishta illa)

<!-- type: phrase; topic: likes -->

To say "I don't like …" in Kannada, use the "I like" pattern and add ಇಲ್ಲ (illa, not): ನನಗೆ … ಇಷ್ಟ ಇಲ್ಲ (nanage … ishta illa), literally "to me … is not liked". Examples: ನನಗೆ ಖಾರ ಇಷ್ಟ ಇಲ್ಲ (nanage khaara ishta illa) = I don't like spicy food; ನನಗೆ ಮಳೆ ಇಷ್ಟ ಇಲ್ಲ (nanage male ishta illa) = I don't like rain; ನನಗೆ ಕಾಯುವುದು ಇಷ್ಟ ಇಲ್ಲ (nanage kaayuvudu ishta illa) = I don't like waiting, spoken ಕಾಯೋದು (kaayodu). It is also written as one word, ಇಷ್ಟವಿಲ್ಲ (ishtavilla). To refuse something offered, the softer ಇದು ಬೇಡ (idu beda, I don't want this) is more polite than saying you dislike it. "I didn't like it" is ಇಷ್ಟ ಆಗಲಿಲ್ಲ (ishta aagalilla).

## I am going to … (a place) — … ಗೆ ಹೋಗುತ್ತಿದ್ದೇನೆ (… ge hoguttiddene)

<!-- type: phrase; topic: directions -->

To say "I am going to (a place)" in Kannada, add -ಗೆ / -ಕ್ಕೆ (-ge / -kke, to) to the place and finish with ಹೋಗುತ್ತಿದ್ದೇನೆ (hoguttiddene, I am going), spoken ಹೋಗ್ತಿದ್ದೀನಿ (hogtiddeeni). Examples: ನಾನು ಮನೆಗೆ ಹೋಗುತ್ತಿದ್ದೇನೆ (naanu manege hoguttiddene) = I am going home; ನಾನು ಕಾಲೇಜಿಗೆ ಹೋಗುತ್ತಿದ್ದೇನೆ (naanu kaalejige hoguttiddene) = I am going to college; ನಾನು ಮಾರುಕಟ್ಟೆಗೆ ಹೋಗುತ್ತಿದ್ದೇನೆ (naanu maarukattege hoguttiddene) = I am going to the market. Words ending in -ಉ take -ಇಗೆ: ಮೈಸೂರು → ಮೈಸೂರಿಗೆ (maisoorige, to Mysuru). For a future trip use ಹೋಗುತ್ತೇನೆ (hoguttene, I will go): ನಾಳೆ ಬೆಂಗಳೂರಿಗೆ ಹೋಗುತ್ತೇನೆ (I'm going to Bengaluru tomorrow).

Also asked as: how to say I am going to in Kannada, I am going home, I'm going to the market, going to college

## I am coming from … — … ಇಂದ ಬರುತ್ತಿದ್ದೇನೆ (… inda baruttiddene)

<!-- type: phrase; topic: postpositions -->

To say "I am coming from …" in Kannada, add -ಇಂದ (-inda, from) to the place and finish with ಬರುತ್ತಿದ್ದೇನೆ (baruttiddene, I am coming), spoken ಬರ್ತಿದ್ದೀನಿ (bartiddeeni). Examples: ನಾನು ಮನೆಯಿಂದ ಬರುತ್ತಿದ್ದೇನೆ (naanu maneyinda baruttiddene) = I am coming from home; ನಾನು ಆಫೀಸಿನಿಂದ ಬರುತ್ತಿದ್ದೇನೆ (naanu aafeesininda baruttiddene) = I am coming from the office; ನಾನು ಮೈಸೂರಿನಿಂದ ಬಂದಿದ್ದೇನೆ (naanu maisoorininda bandiddene) = I have come from Mysuru. For your home country: ನಾನು ಭಾರತದಿಂದ ಬಂದಿದ್ದೇನೆ (naanu bhaaratadinda bandiddene, I have come from India). To ask: ಎಲ್ಲಿಂದ ಬರ್ತಿದ್ದೀರಾ? (ellinda bartiddeeraa?, where are you coming from?).

Also asked as: how to say I am coming from in Kannada, from home, I came from, where are you coming from, from in Kannada

## I don't have … — ನನ್ನ ಹತ್ತಿರ … ಇಲ್ಲ (nanna hattira … illa)

<!-- type: phrase; topic: possession -->

To say "I don't have …" in Kannada, use the "I have" pattern with ಇಲ್ಲ (illa, there isn't) instead of ಇದೆ (ide). For things with you: ನನ್ನ ಹತ್ತಿರ ದುಡ್ಡು ಇಲ್ಲ (nanna hattira duddu illa) = I don't have money; ನನ್ನ ಹತ್ತಿರ ಚಿಲ್ಲರೆ ಇಲ್ಲ (nanna hattira chillare illa) = I don't have change; ನನ್ನ ಹತ್ತಿರ ಪೆನ್ನು ಇಲ್ಲ (nanna hattira pennu illa) = I don't have a pen. For relatives, time and illness use ನನಗೆ (nanage, to me): ನನಗೆ ಟೈಮ್ ಇಲ್ಲ (nanage taim illa, I don't have time), ನನಗೆ ಅಣ್ಣ ಇಲ್ಲ (nanage anna illa, I don't have an elder brother). ಇಲ್ಲ is the same for every person: ಅವರ ಹತ್ತಿರ ಕಾರು ಇಲ್ಲ (they don't have a car).

## Where is …? — … ಎಲ್ಲಿದೆ? (… ellide?)

<!-- type: phrase; topic: directions -->

To ask "where is …?" in Kannada, name the thing or place first and add ಎಲ್ಲಿದೆ? (ellide?), which is ಎಲ್ಲಿ (elli, where) + ಇದೆ (ide, is). Examples: ಬಾತ್ರೂಮ್ ಎಲ್ಲಿದೆ? (baatroom ellide?) = where is the bathroom?; ಬಸ್ ನಿಲ್ದಾಣ ಎಲ್ಲಿದೆ? (bas nildaana ellide?) = where is the bus stand?; ನನ್ನ ಫೋನ್ ಎಲ್ಲಿದೆ? (nanna fon ellide?) = where is my phone?; ಆಸ್ಪತ್ರೆ ಎಲ್ಲಿದೆ? (aaspatre ellide?) = where is the hospital? For a person use ಎಲ್ಲಿದ್ದಾರೆ? (elliddaare?): ಅಮ್ಮ ಎಲ್ಲಿದ್ದಾರೆ? (amma elliddaare?, where is mother?). Start with ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ನೋಡಿ (svalpa nodi) or ಕ್ಷಮಿಸಿ (kshamisi, excuse me) when asking a stranger.

## What is …? — … ಏನು? (… enu?)

<!-- type: phrase; topic: questions -->

To ask "what is …?" in Kannada, put the thing first and ಏನು? (enu?, what) at the end — no word for "is" is needed. Examples: ಇದು ಏನು? (idu enu?) = what is this?; ನಿಮ್ಮ ಹೆಸರು ಏನು? (nimma hesaru enu?) = what is your name?; ನಿಮ್ಮ ಫೋನ್ ನಂಬರ್ ಏನು? (nimma fon nambar enu?) = what is your phone number?; ಇದರ ಅರ್ಥ ಏನು? (idara artha enu?) = what is the meaning of this? In speech ಏನು often merges with the word before it: ಇದೇನು? (idenu?, what's this?), ನಿಮ್ ಹೆಸರೇನು? (nim hesarenu?, what's your name?). To a friend use ನಿನ್ನ (ninna, your) instead of ನಿಮ್ಮ (nimma).

## Can you …? — … ಮಾಡುತ್ತೀರಾ? (… maadutteeraa?)

<!-- type: phrase; topic: requests -->

To ask "can you …?" politely in Kannada, use the polite "you" verb with the question ending -ಆ: -ತೀರಾ? (-teeraa?, will you …?). Examples: ನನಗೆ ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ಸಹಾಯ ಮಾಡುತ್ತೀರಾ? (nanage svalpa sahaaya maadutteeraa?) = can you help me?; ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ನಿಧಾನವಾಗಿ ಹೇಳುತ್ತೀರಾ? (svalpa nidhaanavaagi helutteeraa?) = can you say it slowly?; ದಾರಿ ತೋರಿಸುತ್ತೀರಾ? (daari torisutteeraa?) = can you show me the way? For ability ("are you able to"), use ಬರುತ್ತಾ / ಆಗುತ್ತಾ: ನಿಮಗೆ ಇಂಗ್ಲಿಷ್ ಬರುತ್ತಾ? (nimage inglish baruttaa?, can you speak English?), ನಾಳೆ ಬರೋಕೆ ಆಗುತ್ತಾ? (naale baroke aaguttaa?, can you come tomorrow?). To a friend: ಮಾಡ್ತೀಯಾ? (maadteeyaa?).

## Please … — … ಇ / ದಯವಿಟ್ಟು … (dayavittu …)

<!-- type: phrase; topic: requests -->

To say "please …" in Kannada, use the polite command form of the verb, which ends in -ಇ (-i) or -ಇರಿ (-iri); ದಯವಿಟ್ಟು (dayavittu, please) can go in front for extra politeness. Examples: ದಯವಿಟ್ಟು ಕುಳಿತುಕೊಳ್ಳಿ (dayavittu kulitukolli) = please sit down; ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ನೀರು ಕೊಡಿ (svalpa neeru kodi) = please give me some water; ಒಳಗೆ ಬನ್ನಿ (olage banni) = please come in; ದಯವಿಟ್ಟು ಇನ್ನೊಮ್ಮೆ ಹೇಳಿ (dayavittu innomme heli) = please say it again. The casual forms (ಕೂತ್ಕೋ, ಕೊಡು, ಬಾ, ಹೇಳು) are only for friends and children. In everyday speech, ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ (svalpa, a little) is used more than ದಯವಿಟ್ಟು to soften requests.

## Do you have …? — … ಇದೆಯಾ? (… ideyaa?)

<!-- type: phrase; topic: shopping -->

To ask "do you have …?" in Kannada, name the thing and add ಇದೆಯಾ? (ideyaa?, is there?). In a shop: ಮಾವಿನಹಣ್ಣು ಇದೆಯಾ? (maavinahannu ideyaa?) = do you have mangoes?; ಚಿಲ್ಲರೆ ಇದೆಯಾ? (chillare ideyaa?) = do you have change? To a person about what they carry: ನಿಮ್ಮ ಹತ್ತಿರ ಪೆನ್ನು ಇದೆಯಾ? (nimma hattira pennu ideyaa?) = do you have a pen? About time, family or plans: ನಾಳೆ ನಿಮಗೆ ಬಿಡುವು ಇದೆಯಾ? (naale nimage biduvu ideyaa?) = are you free tomorrow?; ನಿಮಗೆ ಮಕ್ಕಳು ಇದ್ದಾರಾ? (nimage makkalu iddaaraa?) = do you have children? Answers: ಇದೆ (ide, yes, there is) / ಇಲ್ಲ (illa, no). The formal written form is ಇದೆಯೇ? (ideye?).

## How much is …? — … ಎಷ್ಟು? (… eshtu?)

<!-- type: phrase; topic: shopping -->

To ask "how much is …?" in Kannada, name the item and add ಎಷ್ಟು? (eshtu?, how much). Examples: ಇದು ಎಷ್ಟು? (idu eshtu?) = how much is this?; ಈ ಸೀರೆ ಎಷ್ಟು? (ee seere eshtu?) = how much is this saree?; ಟಿಕೆಟ್ ಎಷ್ಟು? (tiket eshtu?) = how much is the ticket?; ಟೊಮೆಟೊ ಕೆಜಿಗೆ ಎಷ್ಟು? (tometo kejige eshtu?) = how much are tomatoes per kilo? The fuller form uses ಬೆಲೆ (bele, price): ಇದರ ಬೆಲೆ ಎಷ್ಟು? (idara bele eshtu?, what is its price?). For a fare by auto: ಸ್ಟೇಷನ್‌ಗೆ ಎಷ್ಟು? (steshange eshtu?, how much to the station?). For the total bill: ಎಷ್ಟು ಆಯ್ತು? (eshtu aaytu?).
