# Kannada culture and context

## Showing respect: ಅವರು, ಅಣ್ಣ, ಅಕ್ಕ and family words

In Kannada, respect is shown with words added to names. ಅವರು (avaru) after a name is polite: ರವಿ ಅವರು (Ravi avaru, respected Ravi). People also call strangers by family words — ಅಣ್ಣ (anna, elder brother), ಅಕ್ಕ (akka, elder sister) — for example a shopkeeper or an auto driver. Teachers and officials are often addressed in English-style forms written in Kannada: ಸರ್ (sar, sir) and ಮೇಡಂ (medam, madam). Use ನೀವು (neevu), not ನೀನು (neenu), with anyone older or unfamiliar, and the polite verb forms such as ಬನ್ನಿ (banni, please come).

## Where Kannada is spoken

<!-- topic: about-language -->

Kannada is the official language of the state of Karnataka in southern India, and it is also spoken by communities in neighbouring states and abroad. It is a Dravidian language, related to Telugu, Tamil and Malayalam, and its script is very close to the Telugu script. Kannada has a long literary tradition and has borrowed many words from Sanskrit. The Government of India declared Kannada a classical language in 2008. The name of the language in Kannada is ಕನ್ನಡ (kannada).

## Festivals: Ugadi

<!-- topic: festivals; level: elementary -->

ಯುಗಾದಿ (Ugadi) is the Kannada New Year, celebrated in spring (March or April). On this day families share ಬೇವು-ಬೆಲ್ಲ (bevu-bella), a mix of bitter neem leaves and sweet jaggery. Eating the two together is a reminder that the new year will bring both bitter and sweet experiences, and that both should be accepted. Homes are cleaned and decorated with mango leaves, and people wear new clothes.

## Festivals: Mysuru Dasara and Rajyotsava

<!-- topic: festivals; level: elementary -->

ಮೈಸೂರು ದಸರಾ (Mysuru Dasara) is Karnataka's best-known festival, celebrated over ten days in autumn and called the ನಾಡಹಬ್ಬ (naadahabba, state festival). The Mysuru palace is lit up and the celebration ends with a grand procession with decorated elephants. ಕನ್ನಡ ರಾಜ್ಯೋತ್ಸವ (Kannada Rajyotsava) on 1 November celebrates the formation of the state in 1956, when Kannada-speaking regions were brought together; the red and yellow Kannada flag is seen everywhere.

## Everyday etiquette

<!-- topic: etiquette -->

Some useful Kannada etiquette: greet elders with ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ (namaskaara) and folded hands; take your shoes off before entering a home or temple; offer and receive things with the right hand. When you are offered food, a polite "enough" is ಸಾಕು (saaku). Guests are often offered coffee or tea first, and it is polite to accept something small. When leaving, say ಹೋಗಿ ಬರುತ್ತೇನೆ (hogi baruttene, "I'll go and come back").
