# Kannada pronunciation for beginners

## Short and long vowels change the meaning

In Kannada, vowel length matters: a long vowel is held about twice as long as a short one, and swapping them can change the word. ಕಲ್ಲು (kallu) means "stone", but ಕಾಲು (kaalu) means "leg"; ಹಾಲು (haalu) is "milk", while ಹಲ್ಲು (hallu) is "tooth". Vachan writes long vowels doubled: aa, ee, oo. Kannada also has short e and o (ಎ, ಒ) as well as long e and o (ಏ, ಓ), which English speakers often merge. The course usually writes both ಎ and ಏ as "e" — ಹೆಸರು (hesaru, name) has short e, ಏನು (enu, what) has long e — so always check the Kannada letter.

## Dental and retroflex sounds

<!-- topic: retroflex -->

Kannada has two sets of t, d, n and l sounds. Dental sounds ತ (ta), ದ (da), ನ (na), ಲ (la) are made with the tongue touching the back of the upper teeth, as in ತಲೆ (tale, head) and ನೀರು (neeru, water). Retroflex sounds ಟ (ta), ಡ (da), ಣ (na), ಳ (la) are made with the tip of the tongue curled back to the roof of the mouth, as in ಹಣ (hana, money) and ಹಳದಿ (haladi, yellow). The difference can change meaning: ಅನ್ನ (anna) is "cooked rice", but ಅಣ್ಣ (anna) is "elder brother". English "t" and "d" sit between the two, so practise feeling where your tongue touches.

## Aspirated sounds

<!-- topic: aspiration -->

Kannada letters such as ಖ (kha), ಘ (gha), ಧ (dha) and ಭ (bha) are aspirated: they are said with a clear puff of air. The plain letters ಕ (ka) and ಪ (pa) have no puff — like the k in "skin", not the k in "kin". Aspirated letters mostly appear in words borrowed from Sanskrit, such as ಧನ್ಯವಾದ (dhanyavaada, thank you) and ಭಾಷೆ (bhaashe, language). Hold your hand in front of your mouth: you should feel air on ಖ but not on ಕ.

## Words end in vowels

<!-- topic: word-endings -->

Almost every native Kannada word ends in a vowel, which gives the language a smooth, flowing sound. Many nouns end in -u, such as ನೀರು (neeru, water), ಹಾಲು (haalu, milk) and ಹೆಸರು (hesaru, name); others end in -a or -e, such as ಊಟ (oota, meal) and ಮನೆ (mane, house). Do not drop the final vowel: say "haalu", not "haal". Words borrowed from English often get an extra -u too, for example ಬಸ್ಸು (bassu, bus).

## Double consonants are held longer

<!-- topic: double-consonants; level: elementary -->

When a Kannada word has a doubled consonant (an ottakshara of the same letter), hold it for a moment before releasing it. ಅಮ್ಮ (amma, mother), ಅಪ್ಪ (appa, father), ಅಕ್ಕ (akka, elder sister) and ಅಣ್ಣ (anna, elder brother) all have a held middle consonant. Saying the double sound as a single one can change the word: ಹಲ್ಲು (hallu) means "tooth", while ಹಾಲು (haalu) means "milk". Practise a short pause: am-ma, ap-pa, ak-ka.

## How to pronounce common Kannada phrases

<!-- topic: phrase-pronunciation; level: beginner -->

Tips for pronouncing everyday Kannada phrases: ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ (namaskaara, hello) is na-mas-KAA-ra — hold the long "aa" and tap the ರ lightly. ಧನ್ಯವಾದ (dhanyavaada, thank you) starts with a breathy ಧ (dh) and has a long "vaa". ನೀವು ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀರಿ? (neevu hegiddeeri?, how are you?) has long "ee" in ನೀ and ದ್ದೀ, a long ಏ in ಹೇ, and a held double "dd". ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದ್ದೇನೆ (chennaagiddene, I'm fine) has a held "nn" and long "aa". ನನಗೆ ಅರ್ಥ ಆಗಲಿಲ್ಲ (nanage artha aagalilla) has a soft dental ಥ (th, tongue at the teeth, with a puff). Kannada gives every syllable fairly even weight, so don't swallow final vowels: say "hesaru", not "hesar".

## The retroflex ಳ (la) in everyday words

<!-- topic: retroflex; level: elementary -->

The Kannada retroflex ಳ (la) is said with the tongue tip curled back to touch the roof of the mouth, then flapped forward. It appears in many everyday words: ಹಳ್ಳಿ (halli, village), ಬೆಂಗಳೂರು (bengalooru, Bengaluru), ಏಳು (elu, seven), ಮಗಳು (magalu, daughter), ಕೇಳಿ (keli, please listen / ask), ಹೇಳಿ (heli, please tell). Compare dental ಲ (la) with the tongue touching the teeth: ಹಲ್ಲು (hallu, tooth) uses ಲ, while ಹಳದಿ (haladi, yellow) uses ಳ. Romanization writes both as "l", so check the letter. If you can't make ಳ yet, a normal "l" will still be understood in context.

## The conjuncts ಕ್ಷ and ಜ್ಞ

<!-- topic: conjuncts; level: elementary -->

Two Kannada conjunct letters often puzzle beginners. ಕ್ಷ (ksha) is ಕ + ಷ, said "ksh": ಕ್ಷಮಿಸಿ (kshamisi, sorry), ಪರೀಕ್ಷೆ (pareekshe, exam), ಲಕ್ಷ (laksha, one lakh). Say the k clearly, then "sh". ಜ್ಞ (jnya) is ಜ + ಞ; in Kannada it is usually pronounced "gnya", as in ಜ್ಞಾನ (gnyaana, knowledge) and ವಿಜ್ಞಾನ (vignyaana, science). Words with ಋ (ru) such as ಕೃಷ್ಣ (krushna) and ಗೃಹ (gruha, home) are read with "ru". These letters come mostly from Sanskrit words.

## Spoken shortcuts you will hear

<!-- topic: spoken-kannada; level: intermediate -->

In fast spoken Kannada, especially in Bengaluru, words shrink, which can make familiar phrases hard to recognise. ಏನು (enu, what) → ಏನ್ (en); ನನಗೆ (nanage, to me) → ನಂಗೆ (nange); ಆಯಿತು (aayitu, happened) → ಆಯ್ತು (aaytu); ಬರುತ್ತೇನೆ (baruttene, I'll come) → ಬರ್ತೀನಿ (barteeni); ಮಾತನಾಡು (maatanaadu, speak) → ಮಾತಾಡು (maataadu); ಇದನ್ನು (idannu, this — object) → ಇದನ್ನ (idanna). Example: ಏನ್ ಮಾಡ್ತಿದ್ದೀರಾ? (en maadtiddeeraa?) = ನೀವು ಏನು ಮಾಡುತ್ತಿದ್ದೀರಿ? (what are you doing?). You can use the full forms; recognising the short ones helps you understand people.
