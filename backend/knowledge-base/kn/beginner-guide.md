# Getting started with Kannada

## How to start learning Kannada

A good order for a beginner in Kannada: first learn a few vowels and consonants (ಅ ಆ ಇ ಈ ಉ ಊ, then ಕ ಮ ನ ಪ ರ ಲ) and how vowel signs attach to consonants (the ಕಾಗುಣಿತ, kaagunita); then learn greetings such as ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ (namaskaara) and ಧನ್ಯವಾದ (dhanyavaada); then simple sentences like ನನ್ನ ಹೆಸರು … (nanna hesaru …, my name is …) and ನನಗೆ ನೀರು ಬೇಕು (nanage neeru beku, I want water). A few minutes every day works better than one long session. Reading the letters aloud helps you remember both the shape and the sound.

## Reading the romanization

<!-- topic: romanization -->

Vachan writes Kannada in English letters (romanization) so beginners can read it before they know the script. Doubled vowels mean a long sound: aa as in "father" (ಹಾಲು, haalu), ee as in "see" (ನೀರು, neeru), oo as in "moon" (ಊಟ, oota). The long ಏ and ಓ are usually written simply as e and o, as in ಏನು (enu, what) and ಬೇಕು (beku, wanted). A doubled consonant (amma, akka) is held a little longer. Capital letters are not used for retroflex sounds, so always check the Kannada letter too: romanization is a helper, the script is the real spelling.

## Formal and informal "you"

<!-- topic: politeness; skill: conversation -->

The most important beginner rule in Kannada conversation: use ನೀವು (neevu) for "you" with anyone older, a stranger, a teacher or a shopkeeper, and use ನೀನು (neenu) only with close friends, younger family members and children. Verbs change with it: ನೀವು ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀರಿ? (neevu hegiddeeri?) is polite, ನೀನು ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀಯ? (neenu hegiddeeya?) is informal. Commands change too: ಬನ್ನಿ (banni, please come) versus ಬಾ (baa, come). When unsure, choose ನೀವು.
