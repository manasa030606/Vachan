# Shopping in Kannada

## How much is this? — ಇದು ಎಷ್ಟು? (idu eshtu?)

<!-- level: beginner -->

To ask the price in Kannada, point and say ಇದು ಎಷ್ಟು? (idu eshtu?, how much is this?) or the fuller ಇದರ ಬೆಲೆ ಎಷ್ಟು? (idara bele eshtu?, what is its price?); ಬೆಲೆ (bele) means price and ಎಷ್ಟು (eshtu) means how much. For things sold by weight: ಕೆಜಿಗೆ ಎಷ್ಟು? (kejige eshtu?, how much per kilo?); per piece: ಒಂದಕ್ಕೆ ಎಷ್ಟು? (ondakke eshtu?, how much for one?). For the total: ಎಷ್ಟು ಆಯ್ತು? (eshtu aaytu?, how much has it come to?). The answer is a number with ರೂಪಾಯಿ (roopaayi, rupees): ಐವತ್ತು ರೂಪಾಯಿ (aivattu roopaayi, fifty rupees). Prices are often said in English numbers too.

Also asked as: how much does this cost in Kannada, how do I ask the price, what is the price, how much per kilo, cost in Kannada

## Too expensive — ತುಂಬಾ ದುಬಾರಿ (tumbaa dubaari)

To say something is too expensive in Kannada, say ಇದು ತುಂಬಾ ದುಬಾರಿ (idu tumbaa dubaari); ದುಬಾರಿ (dubaari) means expensive. In everyday bargaining people more often say ತುಂಬಾ ಜಾಸ್ತಿ ಆಯ್ತು (tumbaa jaasti aaytu, that's too much) or just ಜಾಸ್ತಿ! (jaasti!, too much!). "Cheap" is ಅಗ್ಗ (agga) or ಕಡಿಮೆ ಬೆಲೆ (kadime bele, low price); people also say ಚೀಪ್ (cheep). "Is there anything cheaper?" is ಇದಕ್ಕಿಂತ ಕಡಿಮೆ ಬೆಲೆಯದು ಇದೆಯಾ? (idakkinta kadime beleyadu ideyaa?). A friendly tone works best: add ಅಣ್ಣ (anna) or ಅಕ್ಕ (akka) to the end.

Also asked as: how to say too expensive in Kannada, that's too much, it's costly, cheap in Kannada, anything cheaper

## Bargaining — ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ಕಡಿಮೆ ಮಾಡಿ (svalpa kadime maadi)

<!-- topic: bargaining -->

The standard Kannada bargaining phrase is ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ಕಡಿಮೆ ಮಾಡಿ (svalpa kadime maadi), "please make it a little less". Add ಅಣ್ಣ (anna) or ಅಕ್ಕ (akka) to sound friendly: ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ಕಡಿಮೆ ಮಾಡಿ ಅಣ್ಣ. To ask for the final price: ಕೊನೆಗೆ ಎಷ್ಟು? (konege eshtu?, how much finally?) or the common spoken ಲಾಸ್ಟ್ ಎಷ್ಟು? (laast eshtu?). To make an offer: ನೂರು ರೂಪಾಯಿಗೆ ಕೊಡಿ (nooru roopaayige kodi, give it for a hundred rupees). If they refuse, ಬೇಡ ಬಿಡಿ (beda bidi, never mind) often brings a better price. Bargaining is normal at street markets, not in supermarkets or malls.

Also asked as: how to bargain in Kannada, can you reduce the price, final price, negotiate, make it cheaper

## Discount — ಡಿಸ್ಕೌಂಟ್ ಇದೆಯಾ? (diskaunt ideyaa?)

In Kannada shops, "discount" is said in English: ಡಿಸ್ಕೌಂಟ್ ಇದೆಯಾ? (diskaunt ideyaa?, is there a discount?). The standard Kannada word is ರಿಯಾಯಿತಿ (riyaayiti), seen on sale banners. Useful phrases: ಎಷ್ಟು ಡಿಸ್ಕೌಂಟ್? (eshtu diskaunt?, how much discount?), ಆಫರ್ ಏನಾದರೂ ಇದೆಯಾ? (aafar enaadaroo ideyaa?, is there any offer?). In shops with fixed prices, the seller may say ಫಿಕ್ಸ್ಡ್ ರೇಟ್ (fiksd ret, fixed rate) or ಒಂದೇ ಬೆಲೆ (onde bele, one price only), which means no bargaining.

Also asked as: how to ask for a discount in Kannada, is there a sale, any offer, fixed price

## Do you have …? — … ಇದೆಯಾ? (… ideyaa?)

To ask if a shop has something in Kannada, name it and add ಇದೆಯಾ? (ideyaa?, is there?): ಮಾವಿನಹಣ್ಣು ಇದೆಯಾ? (maavinahannu ideyaa?, do you have mangoes?), ಹಾಲು ಇದೆಯಾ? (haalu ideyaa?, is there milk?). The written form is ಇದೆಯೇ? (ideye?). The shopkeeper answers ಇದೆ (ide, yes, we have) or ಇಲ್ಲ (illa, no) — or ಖಾಲಿ ಆಯ್ತು (khaali aaytu, sold out). To ask when it will come: ಯಾವಾಗ ಬರುತ್ತೆ? (yaavaaga barutte?). For a different kind: ಬೇರೆ ಏನಾದರೂ ಇದೆಯಾ? (bere enaadaroo ideyaa?, is there anything else?).

Also asked as: how to ask do you have in Kannada, is it available, sold out, is there milk, in stock

## Sizes — ಸೈಜ್ (saij)

<!-- topic: clothes -->

When buying clothes in Kannada, size is ಸೈಜ್ (saij). Ask: ಇದರಲ್ಲಿ ದೊಡ್ಡ ಸೈಜ್ ಇದೆಯಾ? (idaralli dodda saij ideyaa?, is there a bigger size in this?) or ಚಿಕ್ಕ ಸೈಜ್ ಇದೆಯಾ? (chikka saij ideyaa?, a smaller size?). ದೊಡ್ಡ (dodda) means big, ಚಿಕ್ಕ (chikka) means small; "the big one" is ದೊಡ್ಡದು (doddadu) and "the small one" ಚಿಕ್ಕದು (chikkadu). If it doesn't fit: ಇದು ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ಟೈಟ್ ಆಗುತ್ತೆ (idu svalpa tait aagutte, this is a bit tight) or ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ಲೂಸ್ ಆಗುತ್ತೆ (svalpa loos aagutte, a bit loose). To try it on: ಹಾಕಿ ನೋಡಬಹುದಾ? (haaki nodabahudaa?, can I try it on?).

Also asked as: sizes in Kannada, bigger size, smaller size, can I try it on, it doesn't fit, big and small

## Colours — ಬೇರೆ ಬಣ್ಣ ಇದೆಯಾ? (bere banna ideyaa?)

<!-- topic: colours -->

To ask about colours in a Kannada shop, say ಬೇರೆ ಬಣ್ಣ ಇದೆಯಾ? (bere banna ideyaa?, is there another colour?); ಬಣ್ಣ (banna) means colour and ಬೇರೆ (bere) means other. For a specific colour, add ಬಣ್ಣದ್ದು (bannaddu, the one of … colour): ಕೆಂಪು ಬಣ್ಣದ್ದು ಇದೆಯಾ? (kempu bannaddu ideyaa?, do you have a red one?). Colour words: ಕೆಂಪು (kempu, red), ನೀಲಿ (neeli, blue), ಹಸಿರು (hasiru, green), ಹಳದಿ (haladi, yellow), ಬಿಳಿ (bili, white), ಕಪ್ಪು (kappu, black), ಕಿತ್ತಳೆ (kittale, orange). "Which colour?" is ಯಾವ ಬಣ್ಣ? (yaava banna?). Light and dark shades are often said in English: ಲೈಟ್ (lait), ಡಾರ್ಕ್ (daark).

Also asked as: how to ask for another colour in Kannada, do you have a red one, colours in Kannada, which colour

## Show me that one — ಅದನ್ನು ತೋರಿಸಿ (adannu torisi)

To ask to see something in a Kannada shop, say ಅದನ್ನು ತೋರಿಸಿ (adannu torisi, please show me that one) or ಇದನ್ನು ತೋರಿಸಿ (idannu torisi, show me this one); ತೋರಿಸಿ (torisi) is the polite "show". In speech the object ending is shortened: ಅದನ್ನ ತೋರಿಸಿ (adanna torisi). For more choice: ಇನ್ನೂ ಬೇರೆ ತೋರಿಸಿ (innoo bere torisi, show me some others). To point to a position: ಮೇಲಿನದು (melinadu, the one on top), ಕೆಳಗಿನದು (kelaginadu, the one below), ಎಡಗಡೆದು (edagadedu, the one on the left). If you are only browsing: ಸುಮ್ಮನೆ ನೋಡ್ತಿದ್ದೀನಿ (summane nodtiddeeni, I'm just looking).

Also asked as: how to say show me in Kannada, can I see that one, show me others, I'm just looking

## I'll take it — ಇದನ್ನು ತೆಗೆದುಕೊಳ್ಳುತ್ತೇನೆ (idannu tegedukolluttene)

To say "I'll take it" in Kannada, say ಇದನ್ನು ತೆಗೆದುಕೊಳ್ಳುತ್ತೇನೆ (idannu tegedukolluttene); in everyday speech, ಇದನ್ನ ತಗೊಳ್ತೀನಿ (idanna tagolteeni). Shorter options: ಇದನ್ನೇ ಕೊಡಿ (idanne kodi, give me this very one) or ಸರಿ, ಕೊಡಿ (sari, kodi, okay, give it). To buy two: ಎರಡು ಕೊಡಿ (eradu kodi). If you decide not to buy: ಬೇಡ, ಆಮೇಲೆ ಬರ್ತೀನಿ (beda, aamele barteeni, no, I'll come back later), a polite way to leave. In Kannada "to buy" is usually said with ತೆಗೆದುಕೊಳ್ಳು (take): ನಿನ್ನೆ ಒಂದು ಸೀರೆ ತಗೊಂಡೆ (ninne ondu seere tagonde, I bought a saree yesterday).

Also asked as: how to say I'll take it in Kannada, I want to buy this, give me this one, I'll come back later, buy in Kannada

## Paying: cash or UPI — ಕ್ಯಾಶ್, ಯುಪಿಐ

<!-- topic: money -->

When paying in Kannada-speaking shops, ask ಯುಪಿಐ ಆಗುತ್ತಾ? (yupiai aaguttaa?, can I pay by UPI?) or ಕಾರ್ಡ್ ಆಗುತ್ತಾ? (kaard aaguttaa?, do you take cards?). Most shops display a QR code and say ಸ್ಕ್ಯಾನ್ ಮಾಡಿ (skyaan maadi, please scan). Cash is ಕ್ಯಾಶ್ (kyaash) or ದುಡ್ಡು (duddu, money): ಕ್ಯಾಶ್ ಕೊಡ್ತೀನಿ (kyaash kodteeni, I'll pay cash). To confirm payment: ದುಡ್ಡು ಬಂತಾ? (duddu bantaa?, did the money come through?). The seller may reply ಬಂತು (bantu, it came). The standard word for money is ಹಣ (hana).

Also asked as: how to pay in Kannada, can I pay by UPI, do you take cards, cash, did the payment go through

## Change — ಚಿಲ್ಲರೆ (chillare)

In Kannada, small change is ಚಿಲ್ಲರೆ (chillare). Shopkeepers and bus conductors often ask ಚಿಲ್ಲರೆ ಇದೆಯಾ? (chillare ideyaa?, do you have change?). Answers: ಇದೆ (ide, yes) or ಚಿಲ್ಲರೆ ಇಲ್ಲ (chillare illa, I don't have change). If you only have a big note: ಐನೂರು ರೂಪಾಯಿ ಇದೆ, ಚಿಲ್ಲರೆ ಇದೆಯಾ? (ainooru roopaayi ide, chillare ideyaa?, I have a five-hundred note, do you have change?). The change given back is ಬಾಕಿ (baaki, balance): ಬಾಕಿ ಕೊಡಿ (baaki kodi, please give the balance).

Also asked as: change in Kannada, do you have change, small money, balance, chillare meaning

## Quantities — ಅರ್ಧ ಕೆಜಿ, ಒಂದು ಕೆಜಿ (ardha keji, ondu keji)

<!-- topic: numbers -->

At a Kannada market, things are sold by the kilo (ಕೆಜಿ, keji) or by the piece. ಒಂದು ಕೆಜಿ ಟೊಮೆಟೊ ಕೊಡಿ (ondu keji tometo kodi) = one kilo of tomatoes please; ಅರ್ಧ ಕೆಜಿ (ardha keji) = half a kilo; ಕಾಲು ಕೆಜಿ (kaalu keji) = a quarter kilo; ಎರಡು ಕೆಜಿ (eradu keji) = two kilos. Bananas and coconuts are often sold by number: ಒಂದು ಡಜನ್ ಬಾಳೆಹಣ್ಣು (ondu dajan baalehannu, a dozen bananas). ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ಜಾಸ್ತಿ (svalpa jaasti) = a bit more, ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ ಕಡಿಮೆ (svalpa kadime) = a bit less, ಸಾಕು (saaku) = enough.

Also asked as: how to say one kilo in Kannada, half a kilo, quantities, a dozen, buying vegetables

## A bag, please — ಒಂದು ಕವರ್ ಕೊಡಿ (ondu kavar kodi)

In Kannada shops, a plastic or paper carry bag is called a ಕವರ್ (kavar): ಒಂದು ಕವರ್ ಕೊಡಿ (ondu kavar kodi, please give me a bag). A cloth or shopping bag is ಚೀಲ (cheela), and a school or travel bag is ಬ್ಯಾಗ್ (byaag). Many shops now charge for bags or don't give plastic, so people carry their own ಚೀಲ. "I have a bag" is ನನ್ನ ಹತ್ತಿರ ಬ್ಯಾಗ್ ಇದೆ (nanna hattira byaag ide), and "no bag needed" is ಕವರ್ ಬೇಡ (kavar beda).

Also asked as: how to ask for a bag in Kannada, carry bag, plastic bag, I don't need a bag, kavar meaning

## Exchange and return — ಬದಲಾಯಿಸಬಹುದಾ? (badalaayisabahudaa?)

<!-- level: intermediate -->

To ask about exchanging something in Kannada, say ಇದನ್ನು ಬದಲಾಯಿಸಬಹುದಾ? (idannu badalaayisabahudaa?, can I exchange this?); ಬದಲಾಯಿಸು (badalaayisu) means to change or exchange. In speech people often use English: ಎಕ್ಸ್‌ಚೇಂಜ್ ಮಾಡಬಹುದಾ? (ekschenj maadabahudaa?). Explain the problem: ಇದು ಸರಿ ಇಲ್ಲ (idu sari illa, this isn't right), ಸೈಜ್ ಸರಿ ಹೋಗಲ್ಲ (saij sari hogalla, the size doesn't fit), ಇದು ಕೆಲಸ ಮಾಡ್ತಿಲ್ಲ (idu kelasa maadtilla, this isn't working). Keep the bill (ಬಿಲ್, bil): shops usually ask ಬಿಲ್ ಇದೆಯಾ? (bil ideyaa?).

Also asked as: how to exchange something in Kannada, return a product, it doesn't work, wrong size, refund
