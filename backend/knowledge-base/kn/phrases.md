# Kannada greetings and common phrases

## Hello — ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ (namaskaara)

The usual way to say hello in Kannada is ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ (namaskaara), often said with the palms pressed together. It works at any time of day and with anyone, and it is the polite choice with elders and strangers. ನಮಸ್ತೆ (namaste) is also understood. Among friends people often just say "hi" or ask ಏನು ಸಮಾಚಾರ? (enu samaachaara? — "what's the news?").

## Thank you — ಧನ್ಯವಾದ (dhanyavaada)

To say thank you in Kannada, say ಧನ್ಯವಾದ (dhanyavaada); the plural form ಧನ್ಯವಾದಗಳು (dhanyavaadagalu, "many thanks") is also very common. It is polite and a little formal. In everyday speech many people simply say "thanks" in English. To answer a thank you, or to say "it's all right / no problem", use ಪರವಾಗಿಲ್ಲ (paravaagilla).

## How are you? — ನೀವು ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀರಿ? (neevu hegiddeeri?)

<!-- topic: everyday-phrases -->

To ask "How are you?" politely in Kannada, say ನೀವು ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀರಿ? (neevu hegiddeeri?). With a friend or a younger person you can say ನೀನು ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀಯ? (neenu hegiddeeya?). Another common greeting is ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದ್ದೀರಾ? (chennaagiddeeraa?, "are you well?"). The usual answer is ನಾನು ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದ್ದೇನೆ (naanu chennaagiddene, "I am fine"), and you can return the question with ನೀವು? (neevu? — "and you?").

## My name is… — ನನ್ನ ಹೆಸರು… (nanna hesaru…)

<!-- topic: introductions -->

To introduce yourself in Kannada, say ನನ್ನ ಹೆಸರು ಆಶಾ (nanna hesaru Asha), literally "my name Asha" — Kannada needs no word for "is" here. To ask someone's name politely: ನಿಮ್ಮ ಹೆಸರು ಏನು? (nimma hesaru enu?, "what is your name?"). Informally: ನಿನ್ನ ಹೆಸರು ಏನು? (ninna hesaru enu?). You can add ನಾನು ಬೆಂಗಳೂರಿನಿಂದ ಬಂದಿದ್ದೇನೆ (naanu bengaloorininda bandiddene, "I have come from Bengaluru").

## Yes and no — ಹೌದು, ಇಲ್ಲ, ಅಲ್ಲ

<!-- topic: everyday-phrases -->

"Yes" in Kannada is ಹೌದು (haudu). There are two words for "no": ಇಲ್ಲ (illa) means "there isn't / no (absence)" — ನೀರು ಇಲ್ಲ (neeru illa, "there is no water"). ಅಲ್ಲ (alla) means "it is not (so)", used for identity — ಇದು ಹಾಲು ಅಲ್ಲ (idu haalu alla, "this is not milk"). As a plain answer to a question, ಇಲ್ಲ (illa) is the usual "no". ಸರಿ (sari) means "okay / all right".

## Please and sorry — ದಯವಿಟ್ಟು, ಕ್ಷಮಿಸಿ

<!-- topic: politeness -->

"Please" in Kannada is ದಯವಿಟ್ಟು (dayavittu), as in ದಯವಿಟ್ಟು ಕುಳಿತುಕೊಳ್ಳಿ (dayavittu kulitukolli, "please sit"). Very often politeness comes from the polite verb ending -ಇ (-i) instead: ಬನ್ನಿ (banni, "please come"), ಹೇಳಿ (heli, "please tell"). "Sorry" or "excuse me" is ಕ್ಷಮಿಸಿ (kshamisi). Use these polite forms with elders, strangers and teachers.

## Goodbye — ಹೋಗಿ ಬರುತ್ತೇನೆ (hogi baruttene)

<!-- topic: greetings -->

Kannada speakers rarely say a direct "goodbye". Instead they say ಹೋಗಿ ಬರುತ್ತೇನೆ (hogi baruttene), meaning "I will go and come (back)", which sounds warmer than a final farewell; in casual speech it is often shortened. The host often replies ಹೋಗಿ ಬನ್ನಿ (hogi banni, "go and come back"). Among friends, "bye" in English is common.

## I don't understand — ನನಗೆ ಅರ್ಥ ಆಗಲಿಲ್ಲ (nanage artha aagalilla)

<!-- topic: everyday-phrases; level: elementary -->

If you do not understand something in Kannada, say ನನಗೆ ಅರ್ಥ ಆಗಲಿಲ್ಲ (nanage artha aagalilla, "I didn't understand"). To ask someone to speak slowly: ನಿಧಾನವಾಗಿ ಹೇಳಿ (nidhaanavaagi heli, "please say it slowly"). To ask what a word means: ಇದರ ಅರ್ಥ ಏನು? (idara artha enu?, "what does this mean?").

## Shopping and directions — ಇದು ಎಷ್ಟು? (idu eshtu?)

<!-- topic: shopping; level: elementary -->

At a shop or market in Kannada, ask ಇದು ಎಷ್ಟು? (idu eshtu?, "how much is this?") or ಇದರ ಬೆಲೆ ಎಷ್ಟು? (idara bele eshtu?, "what is its price?"). To ask for something: ನನಗೆ ನೀರು ಬೇಕು (nanage neeru beku, "I want water"). To ask for a place, put the place first and add ಎಲ್ಲಿದೆ? (ellide?, "where is it?"): ಬಸ್ ನಿಲ್ದಾಣ ಎಲ್ಲಿದೆ? (bas nildaana ellide?, "where is the bus stand?").
