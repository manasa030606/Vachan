# Kannada greetings and common phrases

## Hello — ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ (namaskaara)

The usual way to say hello in Kannada is ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ (namaskaara), often said with the palms pressed together. It works at any time of day and with anyone, and it is the polite choice with elders and strangers. ನಮಸ್ತೆ (namaste) is also understood. Among friends people often just say "hi" or ask ಏನು ಸಮಾಚಾರ? (enu samaachaara? — "what's the news?").

Also asked as: how do I say hello in Kannada, hi in Kannada, how to greet someone, namaskara meaning

## Thank you — ಧನ್ಯವಾದ (dhanyavaada)

To say thank you in Kannada, say ಧನ್ಯವಾದ (dhanyavaada); the plural form ಧನ್ಯವಾದಗಳು (dhanyavaadagalu, "many thanks") is also very common. It is polite and a little formal. In everyday speech many people simply say "thanks" in English. To answer a thank you, or to say "it's all right / no problem", use ಪರವಾಗಿಲ್ಲ (paravaagilla).

Also asked as: how do I say thank you in Kannada, thanks, thank you very much, dhanyavaada meaning

## How are you? — ನೀವು ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀರಿ? (neevu hegiddeeri?)

<!-- topic: everyday-phrases -->

To ask "How are you?" politely in Kannada, say ನೀವು ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀರಿ? (neevu hegiddeeri?). With a friend or a younger person you can say ನೀನು ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀಯ? (neenu hegiddeeya?). Another common greeting is ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದ್ದೀರಾ? (chennaagiddeeraa?, "are you well?"). The usual answer is ನಾನು ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದ್ದೇನೆ (naanu chennaagiddene, "I am fine"), and you can return the question with ನೀವು? (neevu? — "and you?").

Also asked as: how do I ask how are you in Kannada, how are you doing, I am fine, how is it going

## My name is… — ನನ್ನ ಹೆಸರು… (nanna hesaru…)

<!-- topic: introductions -->

To introduce yourself in Kannada, say ನನ್ನ ಹೆಸರು ಆಶಾ (nanna hesaru Asha), literally "my name Asha" — Kannada needs no word for "is" here. To ask someone's name politely: ನಿಮ್ಮ ಹೆಸರು ಏನು? (nimma hesaru enu?, "what is your name?"). Informally: ನಿನ್ನ ಹೆಸರು ಏನು? (ninna hesaru enu?). You can add ನಾನು ಬೆಂಗಳೂರಿನಿಂದ ಬಂದಿದ್ದೇನೆ (naanu bengaloorininda bandiddene, "I have come from Bengaluru").

Also asked as: how do I say my name is in Kannada, what is your name, introduce myself, my name

## Yes and no — ಹೌದು, ಇಲ್ಲ, ಅಲ್ಲ

<!-- topic: everyday-phrases -->

"Yes" in Kannada is ಹೌದು (haudu). There are two words for "no": ಇಲ್ಲ (illa) means "there isn't / no (absence)" — ನೀರು ಇಲ್ಲ (neeru illa, "there is no water"). ಅಲ್ಲ (alla) means "it is not (so)", used for identity — ಇದು ಹಾಲು ಅಲ್ಲ (idu haalu alla, "this is not milk"). As a plain answer to a question, ಇಲ್ಲ (illa) is the usual "no". ಸರಿ (sari) means "okay / all right".

## Please and sorry — ದಯವಿಟ್ಟು, ಕ್ಷಮಿಸಿ

<!-- topic: politeness -->

"Please" in Kannada is ದಯವಿಟ್ಟು (dayavittu), as in ದಯವಿಟ್ಟು ಕುಳಿತುಕೊಳ್ಳಿ (dayavittu kulitukolli, "please sit"). Very often politeness comes from the polite verb ending -ಇ (-i) instead: ಬನ್ನಿ (banni, "please come"), ಹೇಳಿ (heli, "please tell"). "Sorry" or "excuse me" is ಕ್ಷಮಿಸಿ (kshamisi). Use these polite forms with elders, strangers and teachers.

## Goodbye — ಹೋಗಿ ಬರುತ್ತೇನೆ (hogi baruttene)

<!-- topic: greetings -->

Kannada speakers rarely say a direct "goodbye". Instead they say ಹೋಗಿ ಬರುತ್ತೇನೆ (hogi baruttene), meaning "I will go and come (back)", which sounds warmer than a final farewell; in casual speech it becomes ಹೋಗಿ ಬರ್ತೀನಿ (hogi barteeni). The host often replies ಹೋಗಿ ಬನ್ನಿ (hogi banni, "go and come back"). Among friends, "bye" in English is common.

## I don't understand — ನನಗೆ ಅರ್ಥ ಆಗಲಿಲ್ಲ (nanage artha aagalilla)

<!-- topic: everyday-phrases; level: elementary -->

If you do not understand something in Kannada, say ನನಗೆ ಅರ್ಥ ಆಗಲಿಲ್ಲ (nanage artha aagalilla, "I didn't understand"). To ask someone to speak slowly: ನಿಧಾನವಾಗಿ ಹೇಳಿ (nidhaanavaagi heli, "please say it slowly"). To ask what a word means: ಇದರ ಅರ್ಥ ಏನು? (idara artha enu?, "what does this mean?"). In everyday speech you will hear ಅರ್ಥ ಆಗ್ಲಿಲ್ಲ (artha aaglilla), and the polite request ದಯವಿಟ್ಟು ನಿಧಾನವಾಗಿ ಮಾತನಾಡಿ (dayavittu nidhaanavaagi maatanaadi, please speak slowly).

Also asked as: how do I say I don't understand in Kannada, I didn't get it, what does this mean, I can't follow

## Shopping and directions — ಇದು ಎಷ್ಟು? (idu eshtu?)

<!-- topic: shopping; level: elementary -->

At a shop or market in Kannada, ask ಇದು ಎಷ್ಟು? (idu eshtu?, "how much is this?") or ಇದರ ಬೆಲೆ ಎಷ್ಟು? (idara bele eshtu?, "what is its price?"). To ask for something: ನನಗೆ ನೀರು ಬೇಕು (nanage neeru beku, "I want water"). To ask for a place, put the place first and add ಎಲ್ಲಿದೆ? (ellide?, "where is it?"): ಬಸ್ ನಿಲ್ದಾಣ ಎಲ್ಲಿದೆ? (bas nildaana ellide?, "where is the bus stand?").

## Greeting an older person — ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ (namaskaara)

<!-- topic: greetings; type: phrase -->

To greet an elder respectfully in Kannada, say ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ (namaskaara) with palms pressed together, and add ಸಾರ್ (saar) or ಮೇಡಂ (medam) for someone you don't know, or a family title: ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ ಅಜ್ಜಿ (namaskaara ajji, hello grandmother), ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ ಅಂಕಲ್ (namaskaara ankal, hello uncle). Always use ನೀವು (neevu), never ನೀನು: ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀರಿ? (hegiddeeri?, how are you?) and ಆರೋಗ್ಯ ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದೆಯಾ? (aarogya chennaagideyaa?, is your health good?). At festivals or before a journey, younger people often touch an elder's feet to receive blessings. Avoid casual greetings like "hi" or ಏನ್ ಗುರು? (en guru?) with elders.

## Good night — ಶುಭ ರಾತ್ರಿ (shubha raatri)

<!-- topic: greetings; type: phrase -->

"Good night" in Kannada is ಶುಭ ರಾತ್ರಿ (shubha raatri), literally "auspicious night". It is used in messages and when parting at night, though in conversation many people simply say "good night" in English. When leaving someone's house at night, the usual Kannada farewell still works: ಹೋಗಿ ಬರುತ್ತೇನೆ (hogi baruttene, I'll go and come back). Before sleep, family members may say ಮಲಗು (malagu, go to sleep — casual) or ಮಲಗಿ (malagi, polite), and to a friend late at night: ಸರಿ, ನಾಳೆ ಸಿಗೋಣ (sari, naale sigona, okay, see you tomorrow).

Also asked as: how do I say good night in Kannada, goodnight message, sweet dreams, saying bye at night

## Good morning — ಶುಭೋದಯ (shubhodaya)

<!-- topic: greetings; type: phrase -->

"Good morning" in Kannada is ಶುಭೋದಯ (shubhodaya), but it is formal — used on the radio, in speeches and in messages. In daily life people greet each other in the morning with ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ (namaskaara), the English "good morning", or a caring question like ತಿಂಡಿ ಆಯ್ತಾ? (tindi aaytaa?, had breakfast?). Kannada does not have separate everyday greetings for afternoon and evening: ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ works all day.

Also asked as: how to say good morning in Kannada, morning greeting, shubhodaya meaning, good evening in Kannada

## See you later — ಆಮೇಲೆ ಸಿಗೋಣ (aamele sigona)

<!-- topic: greetings; type: phrase -->

"See you later" in Kannada is ಆಮೇಲೆ ಸಿಗೋಣ (aamele sigona), literally "let's meet later"; ಸಿಗು (sigu) means to meet up / be found. "See you tomorrow" is ನಾಳೆ ಸಿಗೋಣ (naale sigona), and "see you again" ಮತ್ತೆ ಸಿಗೋಣ (matte sigona). These work with friends and colleagues alike. With elders, the respectful farewell ಹೋಗಿ ಬರುತ್ತೇನೆ (hogi baruttene, I'll go and come back) is better. Among young people "bye" and "see you" in English are common.

Also asked as: how to say see you later in Kannada, see you tomorrow, see you again, bye for now

## Where are you going? — ನೀವು ಎಲ್ಲಿಗೆ ಹೋಗುತ್ತಿದ್ದೀರಿ? (neevu ellige hoguttiddeeri?)

<!-- topic: everyday-phrases; type: phrase -->

"Where are you going?" in Kannada is ನೀವು ಎಲ್ಲಿಗೆ ಹೋಗುತ್ತಿದ್ದೀರಿ? (neevu ellige hoguttiddeeri?), polite; ಎಲ್ಲಿಗೆ (ellige) means "to where". Spoken: ಎಲ್ಲಿಗೆ ಹೋಗ್ತಿದ್ದೀರಾ? (ellige hogtiddeeraa?). To a friend: ಎಲ್ಲಿಗೆ ಹೋಗ್ತಿದ್ದೀಯಾ? (ellige hogtiddeeyaa?), or just ಎಲ್ಲಿಗೆ? (ellige?). Neighbours ask it as a friendly greeting. Answer with the place + -ಗೆ: ನಾನು ಮಾರುಕಟ್ಟೆಗೆ ಹೋಗುತ್ತಿದ್ದೇನೆ (naanu maarukattege hoguttiddene, I'm going to the market), spoken ಮಾರ್ಕೆಟ್‌ಗೆ ಹೋಗ್ತಿದ್ದೀನಿ (maarketge hogtiddeeni), or ಮನೆಗೆ (manege, home).

Also asked as: how do I say where are you going in Kannada, where are you off to, where to, I'm going to the market

## What are you doing? — ನೀವು ಏನು ಮಾಡುತ್ತಿದ್ದೀರಿ? (neevu enu maaduttiddeeri?)

<!-- topic: everyday-phrases; type: phrase -->

"What are you doing?" in Kannada is ನೀವು ಏನು ಮಾಡುತ್ತಿದ್ದೀರಿ? (neevu enu maaduttiddeeri?), polite. Spoken: ಏನ್ ಮಾಡ್ತಿದ್ದೀರಾ? (en maadtiddeeraa?). To a friend: ಏನು ಮಾಡುತ್ತಿದ್ದೀಯ? or ಏನ್ ಮಾಡ್ತಿದ್ದೀಯಾ? (en maadtiddeeyaa?) — very common on the phone. Answers: ನಾನು ಓದುತ್ತಿದ್ದೇನೆ (naanu oduttiddene, I'm studying), ಊಟ ಮಾಡುತ್ತಿದ್ದೇನೆ (oota maaduttiddene, I'm eating), ಕೆಲಸ ಮಾಡುತ್ತಿದ್ದೇನೆ (kelasa maaduttiddene, I'm working), or simply ಏನೂ ಇಲ್ಲ (enoo illa, nothing much), spoken ಏನಿಲ್ಲ (enilla). "What do you do (for a living)?" is different: ನೀವು ಏನು ಕೆಲಸ ಮಾಡುತ್ತೀರಿ?

Also asked as: how do I say what are you doing in Kannada, what's up, what are you up to, nothing much

## Where are you from? — ನೀವು ಎಲ್ಲಿಯವರು? (neevu elliyavaru?)

<!-- topic: introductions; type: phrase -->

"Where are you from?" in Kannada is ನೀವು ಎಲ್ಲಿಯವರು? (neevu elliyavaru?), literally "you are of where?"; another common question is ನಿಮ್ಮ ಊರು ಯಾವುದು? (nimma ooru yaavudu?, which is your home town?). To answer: ನಾನು ಮೈಸೂರಿನವನು (naanu maisoorinavanu, I'm from Mysuru — a man) or ನಾನು ಮೈಸೂರಿನವಳು (naanu maisoorinavalu — a woman). A form that is the same for everyone: ನಾನು ಭಾರತದಿಂದ ಬಂದಿದ್ದೇನೆ (naanu bhaaratadinda bandiddene, I have come from India). "I live in Bengaluru" is ನಾನು ಬೆಂಗಳೂರಿನಲ್ಲಿ ಇರುತ್ತೇನೆ (naanu bengaloorinalli iruttene), spoken ಬೆಂಗಳೂರಲ್ಲಿ ಇರ್ತೀನಿ (bengalooralli irteeni).

Also asked as: how do I say where I am from in Kannada, where are you from, I am from India, where do you live, home town

## Introducing yourself fully — ನನ್ನ ಬಗ್ಗೆ (nanna bagge)

<!-- topic: introductions; type: phrase; level: elementary -->

A short self-introduction in Kannada: ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ, ನನ್ನ ಹೆಸರು ಆಶಾ (namaskaara, nanna hesaru Asha, hello, my name is Asha). ನನಗೆ ಇಪ್ಪತ್ತು ವರ್ಷ (nanage ippattu varsha, I am twenty). ನಾನು ಮೈಸೂರಿನವಳು (naanu maisoorinavalu, I'm from Mysuru; a man says ಮೈಸೂರಿನವನು, maisoorinavanu). ನಾನು ವಿದ್ಯಾರ್ಥಿ (naanu vidyaarthi, I'm a student). ನಾನು ಕನ್ನಡ ಕಲಿಯುತ್ತಿದ್ದೇನೆ (naanu kannada kaliyuttiddene, I'm learning Kannada). End with ನಿಮ್ಮನ್ನು ಭೇಟಿಯಾಗಿ ಸಂತೋಷವಾಯಿತು (nimmannu bhetiyaagi santoshavaayitu, nice to meet you). ನನ್ನ ಬಗ್ಗೆ (nanna bagge) means "about me".

Also asked as: how do I introduce myself in Kannada, self introduction, tell me about yourself, my name and age, introduce yourself

## You're welcome — ಪರವಾಗಿಲ್ಲ (paravaagilla)

<!-- topic: politeness; type: phrase -->

Kannada has no fixed reply to "thank you". The usual response is ಪರವಾಗಿಲ್ಲ (paravaagilla, it's all right), or ಅದಕ್ಕೇನು (adakkenu, "what's in that?" — no need to thank), or just a smile. ಏನೂ ತೊಂದರೆ ಇಲ್ಲ (enoo tondare illa, no trouble at all) also works. Do not use ಸ್ವಾಗತ (svaagata), which means "welcome" to a place. Among family and close friends, saying ಧನ್ಯವಾದ (dhanyavaada) can even sound too formal; a warm ಸರಿ (sari) or smile is enough.

Also asked as: how to say you're welcome in Kannada, reply to thank you, no problem, don't mention it
