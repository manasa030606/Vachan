# Asking questions in Hindi

## Where do question words go in Hindi?

<!-- level: beginner -->

In Hindi the question word does not jump to the front of the sentence as in English. It usually stays where the answer would go, which is normally just before the verb, and the verb stays at the end. Compare: आप कहाँ रहते हैं? (aap kahaan rahte hain?, where do you live?) — answer: मैं दिल्ली में रहता हूँ (main Dillee mein rahtaa hoon, I live in Delhi). यह क्या है? (yah kyaa hai?, what is this?) — यह किताब है (yah kitaab hai, this is a book). The question words are क्या (kyaa, what), कौन (kaun, who), कहाँ (kahaan, where), कब (kab, when), क्यों (kyon, why), कैसे (kaise, how), कितना (kitnaa, how much) and कौन-सा (kaun-saa, which). Your voice rises a little at the end.

## Yes / no questions — क्या (kyaa) at the start

To ask a yes / no question in Hindi, keep the normal sentence and either put क्या (kyaa) at the beginning or simply raise your voice at the end. Both are correct; क्या sounds a little more careful. Examples: क्या आप हिंदी बोलते हैं? (kyaa aap hindee bolte hain?, do you speak Hindi?), आप ठीक हैं? (aap theek hain?, are you okay?). Here क्या does NOT mean "what" — it only marks the question. Answers: हाँ (haan, yes) or नहीं (nahin, no); politely जी हाँ (jee haan) and जी नहीं (jee nahin). Hindi often answers by repeating the verb: हाँ, बोलता हूँ (haan, boltaa hoon, yes, I do speak).

## What? — क्या (kyaa)

<!-- type: phrase; level: beginner -->

"What" in Hindi is क्या (kyaa), placed before the verb. Examples: यह क्या है? (yah kyaa hai?, what is this?), आप क्या कर रहे हैं? (aap kyaa kar rahe hain?, what are you doing?), आपका नाम क्या है? (aapkaa naam kyaa hai?, what is your name?), क्या हुआ? (kyaa huaa?, what happened?). To ask "what did you say?" politely: आपने क्या कहा? (aapne kyaa kahaa?) or just जी? (jee?, pardon?). Remember that क्या at the very start of a sentence turns it into a yes / no question instead.

## Who? — कौन (kaun)

<!-- type: phrase; level: beginner -->

"Who" in Hindi is कौन (kaun). Examples: वह कौन है? (vah kaun hai?, who is that?), आप कौन हैं? (aap kaun hain?, who are you? — polite, but can sound blunt; on the phone say आप कौन बोल रहे हैं?, aap kaun bol rahe hain?, who is speaking?). About an adult, respectful Hindi uses the plural: ये कौन हैं? (ye kaun hain?, who is this person?). "Whose" is किसका / किसकी / किसके (kiskaa / kiskee / kiske), agreeing with the thing owned: यह किसकी किताब है? (yah kiskee kitaab hai?, whose book is this?). "To whom" is किसको / किसे (kisko / kise).

## Where? — कहाँ (kahaan)

<!-- type: phrase; level: beginner -->

"Where" in Hindi is कहाँ (kahaan). Put the place or person first and कहाँ before the verb: बाथरूम कहाँ है? (baathroom kahaan hai?, where is the bathroom?), आप कहाँ हैं? (aap kahaan hain?, where are you?), आप कहाँ जा रहे हैं? (aap kahaan jaa rahe hain?, where are you going?). "Where from" is कहाँ से (kahaan se): आप कहाँ से हैं? (aap kahaan se hain?, where are you from?). In speech किधर (kidhar, which way) is also common: टॉयलेट किधर है? (toilet kidhar hai?). Start with सुनिए (suniye, excuse me) when asking a stranger.

Also asked as: how to say where in Hindi, where is it, where are you from, asking where something is

## When? — कब (kab)

<!-- type: phrase; level: beginner -->

"When" in Hindi is कब (kab), placed before the verb. Examples: आप कब आएँगे? (aap kab aaenge?, when will you come?), बस कब आएगी? (bas kab aaegee?, when will the bus come?), परीक्षा कब है? (pareekshaa kab hai?, when is the exam?). Useful forms: कब से? (kab se?, since when? / for how long?), कब तक? (kab tak?, until when? / by when?). To ask the clock time use कितने बजे? (kitne baje?, at what time?): ट्रेन कितने बजे है? (tren kitne baje hai?, what time is the train?).

## Why? — क्यों (kyon)

<!-- type: phrase; level: beginner -->

"Why" in Hindi is क्यों (kyon), placed before the verb. Examples: आप उदास क्यों हैं? (aap udaas kyon hain?, why are you sad?), तुम लेट क्यों हो? (tum let kyon ho?, why are you late? — casual). The answer usually uses क्योंकि (kyonki, because): क्योंकि बस लेट थी (kyonki bas let thee, because the bus was late). क्यों नहीं? (kyon nahin?, why not?) also means "of course!". Asking क्यों? alone can sound challenging, so add a softer tone or say क्या हुआ? (kyaa huaa?, what happened?).

## How? — कैसे / कैसा (kaise / kaisaa)

<!-- type: phrase; level: beginner -->

In Hindi "how (in what way)" is कैसे (kaise): आप कॉलेज कैसे जाते हैं? (aap kolej kaise jaate hain?, how do you go to college?), इसे हिंदी में कैसे कहते हैं? (ise hindee mein kaise kahte hain?, how do you say this in Hindi?). "How is …? / what is … like?" uses कैसा / कैसी / कैसे (kaisaa / kaisee / kaise), which agrees with the thing asked about: मौसम कैसा है? (mausam kaisaa hai?, how is the weather?), फ़िल्म कैसी थी? (film kaisee thee?, how was the film?). For people: आप कैसे हैं? (aap kaise hain?, how are you? — to a man), आप कैसी हैं? (to a woman).

## How much? How many? — कितना / कितने (kitnaa / kitne)

<!-- type: phrase; level: beginner -->

"How much" in Hindi is कितना (kitnaa), and it agrees like an adjective: कितने (kitne) with masculine plural nouns, कितनी (kitnee) with feminine nouns. Examples: कितने लोग? (kitne log?, how many people?), कितनी बहनें? (kitnee bahnen?, how many sisters?), कितना पानी? (kitnaa paani?, how much water?). For prices say यह कितने का है? (yah kitne kaa hai?, how much is this?) or कितना हुआ? (kitnaa huaa?, how much is it altogether?). For time: कितनी देर लगेगी? (kitnee der lagegee?, how long will it take?). For distance: कितनी दूर है? (kitnee door hai?, how far is it?).

## Which? — कौन-सा (kaun-saa)

<!-- type: phrase -->

"Which" in Hindi is कौन-सा (kaun-saa), also written कौन सा. It agrees with the noun: कौन-सा (masculine), कौन-सी (kaun-see, feminine), कौन-से (kaun-se, masculine plural or before a postposition). Examples: कौन-सा रंग? (kaun-saa rang?, which colour?), कौन-सी बस? (kaun-see bas?, which bus?), ट्रेन कौन-से प्लेटफ़ॉर्म पर आएगी? (tren kaun-se pletform par aaegee?, which platform will the train come to?). For "which one": कौन-सा वाला? (kaun-saa vaalaa?) — feminine कौन-सी वाली? (kaun-see vaalee?).

## Tag questions — …, ना? / है ना? (naa? / hai naa?)

<!-- level: intermediate -->

Hindi adds ना (naa) or है ना (hai naa) at the end of a statement to ask for agreement, like English "…, right?" or "isn't it?". Examples: तुम आओगे, ना? (tum aaoge, naa?, you'll come, won't you?), मौसम अच्छा है, है ना? (mausam achchhaa hai, hai naa?, the weather is nice, isn't it?), आप हिंदी समझते हैं, ना? (aap hindee samajhte hain, naa?, you understand Hindi, right?). With a command ना makes it a gentle plea: बताओ ना (bataao naa, come on, tell me). Very common and friendly; it does not change for gender.

## Common question phrases for beginners

<!-- type: phrase; level: beginner -->

Useful ready-made Hindi questions: आपका नाम क्या है? (aapkaa naam kyaa hai?, what is your name?), आप कैसे हैं? (aap kaise hain?, how are you?), आप कहाँ से हैं? (aap kahaan se hain?, where are you from?), यह क्या है? (yah kyaa hai?, what is this?), यह कितने का है? (yah kitne kaa hai?, how much is this?), बाथरूम कहाँ है? (baathroom kahaan hai?, where is the bathroom?), कितने बजे हैं? (kitne baje hain?, what time is it?), इसका मतलब क्या है? (iskaa matlab kyaa hai?, what does this mean?), क्या आप अंग्रेज़ी बोलते हैं? (kyaa aap angrezee bolte hain?, do you speak English?). With friends swap आप for तुम and हैं for हो.

## What happened? Is everything okay? — क्या हुआ? (kyaa huaa?)

<!-- type: phrase -->

क्या हुआ? (kyaa huaa?, what happened? / what's wrong?) is one of the most common Hindi questions; it is the same for men and women. To check on someone: सब ठीक है? (sab theek hai?, is everything okay?), आप ठीक हैं? (aap theek hain?, are you okay?). Casual: तुम ठीक हो? (tum theek ho?). If someone looks upset: क्या बात है? (kyaa baat hai?, what's the matter?). If you didn't hear something: क्या कहा? (kyaa kahaa?, what did you say?). Typical answers: कुछ नहीं (kuchh nahin, nothing), सब ठीक है (sab theek hai, everything's fine).
