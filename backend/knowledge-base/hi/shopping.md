# Shopping and bargaining in Hindi

## How much is this? — यह कितने का है? (yah kitne kaa hai?)

To ask the price in Hindi, point and say यह कितने का है? (yah kitne kaa hai?), literally "this is of how much?". Spoken, यह sounds like ये (ye). For a feminine item you may hear कितने की है? (kitne kee hai?), e.g. for a साड़ी (saadee, sari). Other common ways: इसका दाम क्या है? (iskaa daam kyaa hai?, what is its price?), कितने का? (kitne kaa?, how much?), and for the total कितना हुआ? (kitnaa huaa?, how much does it come to?). For produce: आम कितने रुपये किलो हैं? (aam kitne rupaye kilo hain?, how much are mangoes per kilo?). These questions are the same for men and women.

Also asked as: how do I ask the price in Hindi, how much does this cost, what is the price, how much is it

## Prices and rupees — रुपये (rupaye)

<!-- topic: numbers -->

Prices in Hindi are given in रुपये (rupaye, rupees; singular रुपया, rupayaa): सौ रुपये (sau rupaye, 100 rupees), पचास रुपये (pachaas rupaye, 50 rupees). Sellers often answer in English numbers, especially for bigger amounts. "Per kilo" is किलो (kilo): अस्सी रुपये किलो (assee rupaye kilo, 80 rupees a kilo). "Each / per piece": एक का (ek kaa), e.g. दस रुपये का एक (das rupaye kaa ek, ten rupees each). Money in general is पैसे (paise). Small change is खुले पैसे (khule paise) or छुट्टे (chhutte): खुले पैसे हैं? (khule paise hain?, do you have change?).

## Too expensive — बहुत महँगा है (bahut mahangaa hai)

"It's too expensive" in Hindi is यह बहुत महँगा है (yah bahut mahangaa hai). महँगा (mahangaa, expensive; also written महंगा) agrees with the item: for a feminine thing say महँगी (mahangee): यह साड़ी बहुत महँगी है (yah saadee bahut mahangee hai). The opposite is सस्ता / सस्ती (sastaa / sastee, cheap). In a market people add a friendly tone: अरे भैया, बहुत महँगा है! (are bhaiyaa, bahut mahangaa hai!, oh brother, that's too expensive!). "Do you have something cheaper?": इससे सस्ता कुछ है? (isse sastaa kuchh hai?).

Also asked as: how to say too expensive in Hindi, that's a lot, cheap in Hindi, something cheaper

## Bargaining — थोड़ा कम कीजिए (thodaa kam keejiye)

Bargaining (मोल-भाव, mol-bhaav) is normal in Indian street markets but not in malls or fixed-price shops. The key phrase is थोड़ा कम कीजिए (thodaa kam keejiye, please reduce it a little) or भैया, थोड़ा कम कर दीजिए (bhaiyaa, thodaa kam kar deejiye). Other useful lines: सही दाम बताइए (sahee daam bataaiye, tell me the right price), आख़िरी दाम क्या है? (aakhiree daam kyaa hai?, what's your final price?), सौ रुपये में दे दीजिए (sau rupaye mein de deejiye, give it to me for 100). If the price is still too high, walking away politely — रहने दीजिए (rahne deejiye, never mind) — often brings a better offer.

## Discount — छूट (chhoot) / डिस्काउंट

In shops, a discount is छूट (chhoot) or, more often, the English डिस्काउंट (diskaaunt). Ask: कोई डिस्काउंट है? (koee diskaaunt hai?, is there any discount?) or कुछ छूट मिलेगी? (kuchh chhoot milegee?, will I get some discount?). Signs say "50% छूट" or सेल (sel, sale). For a fixed-price shop the answer may be फ़िक्स्ड रेट है (fiksd ret hai, the price is fixed). In markets the more natural approach is bargaining with थोड़ा कम कीजिए (thodaa kam keejiye).

## I'll take it — मैं यह ले लूँगा (main yah le loongaa)

To agree to buy in Hindi, a man says मैं यह ले लूँगा (main yah le loongaa, I'll take this) and a woman says मैं यह ले लूँगी (main yah le loongee). Very common and gender-neutral: ठीक है, दे दीजिए (theek hai, de deejiye, okay, give it to me) or पैक कर दीजिए (paik kar deejiye, please pack it). For "this one": यह वाला दीजिए (yah vaalaa deejiye); for a feminine item यह वाली (yah vaalee). If you decide not to buy: नहीं, रहने दीजिए (nahin, rahne deejiye, no, leave it) or बस देख रहा हूँ (bas dekh rahaa hoon, I'm just looking — a woman: देख रही हूँ).

## Do you have …? — … है? (… hai?)

To ask whether a shop has something in Hindi, say क्या आपके पास … है? (kyaa aapke paas … hai?, do you have …?) — or simply name it: भैया, आम हैं? (bhaiyaa, aam hain?, do you have mangoes?). "Do you have another colour?": कोई और रंग है? (koee aur rang hai?). "In red?": लाल रंग में है? (laal rang mein hai?). "Is there a red one?": लाल वाला है? (laal vaalaa hai?). "Show me that one": वह वाला दिखाइए (vah vaalaa dikhaaiye). Answers: हाँ, है (haan, hai, yes, we have) or नहीं है (nahin hai, we don't).

Also asked as: do you have this in Hindi, another colour, show me that one, is it available

## Sizes — छोटा, बड़ा, साइज़ (chhotaa, badaa, saaiz)

Clothes sizes are usually said in English in Indian shops: स्मॉल, मीडियम, लार्ज, or a number. Useful Hindi: क्या इसमें बड़ा साइज़ है? (kyaa ismein badaa saaiz hai?, is there a bigger size in this?), यह छोटा है (yah chhotaa hai, it's small), यह बड़ा है (yah badaa hai, it's big), यह टाइट है (yah taait hai, it's tight), यह ढीला है (yah dheelaa hai, it's loose). For feminine items (कुर्ती, साड़ी) the adjectives end in -ई: छोटी, बड़ी, ढीली. To try something on: क्या मैं पहनकर देख सकता हूँ? (kyaa main pahankar dekh saktaa hoon?, can I try it on?) — a woman says सकती हूँ (saktee hoon). Changing room: ट्रायल रूम (traayal room).

Also asked as: how to ask for a bigger size in Hindi, can I try it on, too small, clothes size

## Colours when shopping — रंग (rang)

<!-- type: vocabulary; skill: vocabulary -->

Hindi colour words: लाल (laal, red), नीला (neelaa, blue), हरा (haraa, green), पीला (peelaa, yellow), काला (kaalaa, black), सफ़ेद (safed, white), गुलाबी (gulaabee, pink), नारंगी (naarangee, orange), भूरा (bhooraa, brown). Colours ending in -आ agree with the item: नीली साड़ी (neelee saadee, blue sari), काले जूते (kaale joote, black shoes); लाल, सफ़ेद and गुलाबी never change. Shopping questions: कौन-सा रंग चाहिए? (kaun-saa rang chaahiye?, which colour do you want?), और किन रंगों में है? (aur kin rangon mein hai?, what other colours does it come in?).

## Paying: cash or UPI — कैश या यूपीआई (kaish yaa yoopeeaaee)

In India most shops and even street stalls accept phone payments (UPI) by scanning a QR code. Ask: यूपीआई चलेगा? (yoopeeaaee chalegaa?, does UPI work here?) or ऑनलाइन पेमेंट हो जाएगा? (onlaain pement ho jaaegaa?, can I pay online?). Cash is कैश (kaish) or नक़द (naqad): मैं कैश दूँगा (main kaish doongaa, I'll pay cash); a woman says दूँगी (doongee). Card: कार्ड चलेगा? (kaard chalegaa?, do you take cards?). The seller may say स्कैन कर दीजिए (skain kar deejiye, please scan) or पेमेंट आ गया (pement aa gayaa, the payment has come through).

## Bags, receipts and returns

When shopping in Hindi, a carry bag is थैली (thailee): एक थैली दे दीजिए (ek thailee de deejiye, please give me a bag). Many shops charge for bags, so a cloth bag (थैला, thailaa) is handy. A bill or receipt is बिल (bil): बिल दे दीजिए (bil de deejiye, please give me the bill). To exchange something: क्या इसे बदल सकते हैं? (kyaa ise badal sakte hain?, can this be exchanged?). Returns: वापस (vaapas, back) — क्या यह वापस हो सकता है? (kyaa yah vaapas ho saktaa hai?, can this be returned?). Many small shops exchange but don't refund.

Also asked as: can I get a bag in Hindi, receipt please, can I return this, exchange in Hindi

## At the vegetable market — सब्ज़ी मंडी (sabzee mandee)

At a Hindi-speaking vegetable market (सब्ज़ी मंडी, sabzee mandee) or cart, people buy by the kilo: एक किलो टमाटर दीजिए (ek kilo tamaatar deejiye, one kilo of tomatoes, please), आधा किलो प्याज़ (aadhaa kilo pyaaz, half a kilo of onions), पाव भर (paav bhar, a quarter kilo). Ask: आलू कैसे दिए? (aaloo kaise die?, how much are the potatoes? — a very common market phrase). Sellers are called भैया (bhaiyaa) or आंटी (aantee). Vendors may add a little extra धनिया (dhaniyaa, coriander) or मिर्च (mirch, chilli) for free if you ask nicely.

Also asked as: buying vegetables in Hindi, one kilo please, how much are the tomatoes, market phrases
