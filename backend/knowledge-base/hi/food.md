# Food and eating out in Hindi

## I am hungry — मुझे भूख लगी है (mujhe bhookh lagee hai)

"I am hungry" in Hindi is मुझे भूख लगी है (mujhe bhookh lagee hai), literally "hunger has struck me". It is the most natural way to say it and it is the same for men and women. भूख (bhookh) means hunger. "Very hungry": मुझे बहुत भूख लगी है (mujhe bahut bhookh lagee hai). You can also use the adjective: a man says मैं भूखा हूँ (main bhookhaa hoon) and a woman says मैं भूखी हूँ (main bhookhee hoon), but this sounds stronger, closer to "I'm starving / I haven't eaten". To ask a friend: भूख लगी है? (bhookh lagee hai?, are you hungry?); politely: आपको भूख लगी है? (aapko bhookh lagee hai?).

## I am thirsty — मुझे प्यास लगी है (mujhe pyaas lagee hai)

"I am thirsty" in Hindi is मुझे प्यास लगी है (mujhe pyaas lagee hai), literally "thirst has struck me", the same for men and women. प्यास (pyaas) means thirst. Then ask for water: मुझे पानी चाहिए (mujhe paani chaahiye, I want water) or politely थोड़ा पानी मिलेगा? (thodaa paani milegaa?, may I have some water?). In a restaurant: पानी दीजिए (paani deejiye, water, please). Cold water is ठंडा पानी (thandaa paani); bottled water is usually just called बोतल (botal) or "bottle": एक बोतल पानी दीजिए (ek botal paani deejiye).

Also asked as: how to say I am thirsty in Hindi, can I have water, I want water, thirsty in Hindi

## Ordering food — एक … दीजिए (ek … deejiye)

<!-- topic: restaurant -->

To order in a Hindi-speaking restaurant or dhaba, name the dish and add दीजिए (deejiye, please give): एक दाल मखनी और दो रोटी दीजिए (ek daal makhanee aur do rotee deejiye, one dal makhani and two rotis, please). Or use मुझे … चाहिए (mujhe … chaahiye, I want …). The waiter may ask आप क्या लेंगे? (aap kyaa lenge?, what will you have?) or और कुछ? (aur kuchh?, anything else?). Answer बस, इतना ही (bas, itnaa hee, that's all). To see the menu: मेन्यू दिखाइए (menyoo dikhaaiye, please show the menu). To ask what is good: यहाँ क्या अच्छा मिलता है? (yahaan kyaa achchhaa miltaa hai?, what's good here?).

## Is it spicy? Not spicy, please — तीखा (teekhaa)

<!-- topic: restaurant -->

To ask if food is spicy in Hindi: क्या यह तीखा है? (kyaa yah teekhaa hai?), or just ये तीखा है? with a rising voice. तीखा (teekhaa) means chilli-hot; a feminine dish takes तीखी (teekhee): दाल तीखी है? (daal teekhee hai?). To ask for less chilli: मिर्च कम डालिए (mirch kam daaliye, please put less chilli) or कम तीखा बनाइए (kam teekhaa banaaiye, please make it less spicy). No chilli at all: बिना मिर्च के (binaa mirch ke). If it's too hot for you: बहुत तीखा है! (bahut teekhaa hai!, it's very spicy!). मसालेदार (masaaledaar) means richly spiced, not necessarily hot.

Also asked as: how to say not spicy in Hindi, less spicy please, is it spicy, no chilli

## I am vegetarian — मैं शाकाहारी हूँ (main shaakaahaaree hoon)

"I am vegetarian" in Hindi is मैं शाकाहारी हूँ (main shaakaahaaree hoon), the same for men and women. In everyday speech people say वेज (vej) and नॉन-वेज (non-vej): मैं वेज खाता हूँ (main vej khaataa hoon); a woman says वेज खाती हूँ (vej khaatee hoon). "I don't eat meat": मैं मांस नहीं खाता (main maans nahin khaataa); a woman says नहीं खाती (nahin khaatee). Useful questions: क्या इसमें अंडा है? (kyaa ismein andaa hai?, does this have egg?), वेज खाना मिलेगा? (vej khaanaa milegaa?, can I get vegetarian food?). Many Indian restaurants are fully vegetarian, and menus mark dishes with a green (veg) or red/brown (non-veg) dot.

## Taste words — sweet, salty, sour, spicy, bitter

<!-- type: vocabulary; skill: vocabulary -->

Hindi taste words: मीठा (meethaa, sweet), नमकीन (namkeen, salty / savoury), खट्टा (khattaa, sour), तीखा (teekhaa, spicy-hot), कड़वा (kadvaa, bitter), फीका (pheekaa, bland / not enough salt or sugar). Words ending in -आ agree with the noun: मीठी चाय (meethee chaay, sweet tea — चाय is feminine), खट्टा दही (khattaa dahee, sour curd — दही is masculine in standard Hindi). Taste itself is स्वाद (svaad). "It needs more salt": नमक कम है (namak kam hai, there is too little salt). खट्टा-मीठा (khattaa-meethaa) means sweet and sour.

## It's delicious — बहुत बढ़िया है! (bahut badhiyaa hai!)

To praise food in Hindi, the most natural phrases are बहुत बढ़िया है! (bahut badhiyaa hai!, it's great!) and बहुत अच्छा बना है (bahut achchhaa banaa hai, it's very well made). स्वादिष्ट (svaadisht, delicious) is correct but sounds formal: यह बहुत स्वादिष्ट है (yah bahut svaadisht hai). After a great meal people say मज़ा आ गया! (mazaa aa gayaa!, I loved it!). Young people also say "tasty" (टेस्टी). Praising the cook's food is the best thank-you in a Hindi-speaking home. These phrases are the same for men and women.

## The bill, please — बिल लाइए (bil laaiye)

<!-- topic: restaurant -->

To ask for the bill in Hindi say बिल लाइए (bil laaiye, please bring the bill) or more softly ज़रा बिल ले आइए (zaraa bil le aaiye). At a dhaba or tea stall people ask भैया, कितना हुआ? (bhaiyaa, kitnaa huaa?, how much is it?). To pay by phone: यूपीआई चलेगा? (yoopeeaaee chalegaa?, is UPI okay?) — most places accept it. To take leftovers: बाक़ी पैक कर दीजिए (baaqee paik kar deejiye, please pack the rest). Tips are not compulsory; a small tip is appreciated in restaurants.

## Meals of the day — breakfast, lunch, dinner

<!-- type: vocabulary; skill: vocabulary -->

Hindi meal words: नाश्ता (naashtaa, breakfast; also a snack), दोपहर का खाना (dopahar kaa khaanaa, lunch, literally "afternoon's food"), रात का खाना (raat kaa khaanaa, dinner, "night's food"). In offices and colleges people simply say लंच (lanch) and डिनर (dinar). Verbs: नाश्ता करना (naashtaa karnaa, to have breakfast), खाना खाना (khaanaa khaanaa, to eat a meal). Examples: मैं नाश्ता कर रहा हूँ (main naashtaa kar rahaa hoon, I'm having breakfast — woman: कर रही हूँ), आज रात के खाने में क्या है? (aaj raat ke khaane mein kyaa hai?, what's for dinner tonight?).

## Eating at someone's home — और लीजिए (aur leejiye)

<!-- topic: hospitality; type: culture; skill: culture -->

At a Hindi-speaking home the host will serve you with लीजिए, खाइए (leejiye, khaaiye, please take, please eat) and keep insisting और लीजिए (aur leejiye, have some more). To accept: थोड़ा सा (thodaa saa, just a little). To refuse politely: बस, धन्यवाद (bas, dhanyavaad, that's enough, thank you) or मेरा पेट भर गया (meraa pet bhar gayaa, I'm full), which is the same for men and women. A first "no" is often politely ignored, so you may need to say it twice with a smile. Food is usually eaten with the right hand, and roti is torn with one hand.

## Common foods — roti, rice, dal, sabzi

<!-- type: vocabulary; skill: vocabulary; level: beginner -->

Everyday Hindi food words: रोटी (rotee, flatbread; also चपाती, chapaatee), चावल (chaaval, rice), दाल (daal, lentils / lentil curry), सब्ज़ी (sabzee, vegetables or a cooked vegetable dish), दही (dahee, curd / yoghurt), पराठा (paraathaa, layered or stuffed flatbread), पनीर (paneer, cottage cheese), अचार (achaar, pickle), मिठाई (mithaaee, sweets), नमक (namak, salt), चीनी (cheenee, sugar). A set plate with several dishes is a थाली (thaalee). Typical home meal: दाल, चावल, रोटी और सब्ज़ी (daal, chaaval, rotee aur sabzee).

## Drinks — tea, coffee, lassi

<!-- type: vocabulary; skill: vocabulary; level: beginner -->

Hindi drink words: चाय (chaay, tea — feminine), कॉफ़ी (kofee, coffee), दूध (doodh, milk), पानी (paani, water), जूस (joos, juice), लस्सी (lassee, a thick yoghurt drink, sweet or salty), छाछ (chhaachh, buttermilk), नारियल पानी (naariyal paani, coconut water). Ordering tea: एक चाय दीजिए (ek chaay deejiye). Less sugar: चीनी कम (cheenee kam); no sugar: बिना चीनी के (binaa cheenee ke). Strong tea is कड़क चाय (kadak chaay). Offering tea to a guest: चाय लेंगे? (chaay lenge?, will you have tea?).

## Food is ready, let's eat — खाना तैयार है (khaanaa taiyaar hai)

<!-- topic: daily-routine -->

At home in Hindi, someone calls out खाना तैयार है! (khaanaa taiyaar hai!, food is ready!) or खाना लग गया है (khaanaa lag gayaa hai, the food has been served). To invite people to the table: चलो, खाना खाते हैं (chalo, khaanaa khaate hain, let's eat) or politely आइए, खाना खाइए (aaiye, khaanaa khaaiye, please come and eat). A caring question from elders: खाना खाया? (khaanaa khaayaa?, have you eaten?) — answer हाँ, खा लिया (haan, khaa liyaa, yes, I've eaten), the same for men and women.
