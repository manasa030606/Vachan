# Hindi culture and context

## Showing respect: जी, भैया, दीदी and आप

In Hindi, respect is shown with words added to names. जी (jee) after a name or title is polite: शर्मा जी (Sharma jee), आशा जी (Asha jee), and it also appears in words like पिताजी (pitaajee, father). जी हाँ (jee haan) is a polite "yes". People also call strangers by family words: भैया (bhaiyaa, elder brother) for a shopkeeper or driver, दीदी (deedee, elder sister) for a woman, and children call adults अंकल (uncle) or आंटी (aunty). Use आप (aap), not तुम (tum), with anyone older or unfamiliar.

## Greetings: नमस्ते and folded hands

<!-- topic: greetings; skill: conversation -->

The common Hindi greeting is नमस्ते (namaste), said with the palms pressed together in front of the chest and often a small bow of the head. It is used both when meeting and when leaving. In some parts of North India, especially in villages, people also greet each other with राम राम (Ram Ram). In many Hindi-speaking families, younger people show respect to elders by touching their feet, called पैर छूना (pair chhoonaa), and the elder gives a blessing.

## Where Hindi is spoken

<!-- topic: about-language -->

Hindi is the official language of the Union government of India, together with English, and it is written in the Devanagari script. It is widely spoken across North and Central India, in states such as Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan and Delhi, and many people elsewhere in India understand it. Hindi is an Indo-Aryan language that grew from Sanskrit, and its everyday spoken form is very close to Urdu. Hindi Diwas (हिंदी दिवस, Hindi Day) is celebrated every year on 14 September.

## Festivals: Diwali and Holi

<!-- topic: festivals; level: elementary -->

दिवाली (Diwali), also called दीपावली (deepaavalee), is the festival of lights, held in October or November. Families light small clay lamps called दीये (diye), clean and decorate their homes, and share मिठाई (mithaai, sweets). A common Hindi greeting is दिवाली की शुभकामनाएँ (Diwali kee shubhkaamnaaen, "best wishes for Diwali"). होली (Holi) is the spring festival of colours, when people throw coloured powder (गुलाल, gulaal) and water at each other, often shouting बुरा न मानो, होली है (buraa na maano, Holi hai — "don't mind, it's Holi").

## Everyday etiquette

<!-- topic: etiquette -->

Some useful etiquette in Hindi-speaking homes: greet elders with नमस्ते (namaste) and folded hands; take your shoes off before entering a home or temple; offer and receive things with the right hand. Guests are treated with great care — a well-known Sanskrit phrase says अतिथि देवो भव (atithi devo bhava, "the guest is like God") — and are usually offered चाय-पानी (chaay-paani, "tea and water", meaning refreshments). When you have had enough food, say बस, धन्यवाद (bas, dhanyavaad, "that's enough, thank you").
