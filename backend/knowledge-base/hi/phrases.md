# Hindi greetings and common phrases

## Hello — नमस्ते (namaste)

The usual way to say hello in Hindi is नमस्ते (namaste), often said with the palms pressed together. It works at any time of day and with anyone, and the reply is simply नमस्ते again. A slightly more formal form is नमस्कार (namaskaar). With elders add जी: नमस्ते जी (namaste jee). Among friends people often just say "hi" or ask क्या हाल है? (kyaa haal hai?, "how are things?").

## Thank you — धन्यवाद (dhanyavaad)

To say thank you in Hindi, say धन्यवाद (dhanyavaad). It is polite and a little formal. In everyday speech शुक्रिया (shukriyaa) is also very common, and many people simply say "thank you" in English. To answer "thank you", say कोई बात नहीं (koee baat nahin, "it's nothing / no problem").

## How are you? — आप कैसे हैं? (aap kaise hain?)

<!-- topic: everyday-phrases -->

To ask "How are you?" politely in Hindi, say आप कैसे हैं? (aap kaise hain?). When speaking to a woman, कैसे changes to कैसी: आप कैसी हैं? (aap kaisee hain?). With a friend you can say तुम कैसे हो? (tum kaise ho?). The usual answer is मैं ठीक हूँ (main theek hoon, "I am fine"), and you can return the question with और आप? (aur aap?, "and you?").

## My name is… — मेरा नाम… है (meraa naam … hai)

<!-- topic: introductions -->

To introduce yourself in Hindi, say मेरा नाम आशा है (meraa naam Asha hai), "my name is Asha". Hindi needs the word है (hai, is) at the end of the sentence. To ask someone's name politely: आपका नाम क्या है? (aapkaa naam kyaa hai?, "what is your name?"). Informally: तुम्हारा नाम क्या है? (tumhaaraa naam kyaa hai?). You can add मैं दिल्ली से हूँ (main Dillee se hoon, "I am from Delhi").

## Yes and no — हाँ, नहीं (haan, nahin)

<!-- topic: everyday-phrases -->

"Yes" in Hindi is हाँ (haan) and "no" is नहीं (nahin). To sound polite, put जी (jee) in front: जी हाँ (jee haan, "yes, sir / madam") and जी नहीं (jee nahin, "no, sir / madam"). A short जी (jee) on its own also works as a polite "yes" or "I'm listening". In casual talk ना (naa) is often used for "no". नहीं also makes sentences negative: यह पानी नहीं है (yah paani nahin hai, "this is not water").

## Please and sorry — कृपया, माफ़ कीजिए

<!-- topic: politeness -->

"Please" in Hindi is कृपया (kripyaa), but it sounds formal and appears mostly in writing and announcements. In speech, politeness usually comes from the verb ending -इए (-iye): बैठिए (baithiye, "please sit"), आइए (aaiye, "please come"). "Sorry" is माफ़ कीजिए (maaf keejiye) or the more formal क्षमा कीजिए (kshamaa keejiye). To get someone's attention politely, say सुनिए (suniye, literally "please listen").

## Goodbye — फिर मिलेंगे (phir milenge)

<!-- topic: greetings -->

When leaving, Hindi speakers often say फिर मिलेंगे (phir milenge, "we will meet again") or simply नमस्ते (namaste) again. A man leaving can say चलता हूँ (chaltaa hoon, "I'll be off") and a woman चलती हूँ (chaltee hoon). अलविदा (alvidaa) also means goodbye, but it sounds final and formal, so it is less used in daily life. Among friends, "bye" in English is common.

## I don't understand — मुझे समझ नहीं आया (mujhe samajh nahin aayaa)

<!-- topic: everyday-phrases; level: elementary -->

If you do not understand something in Hindi, say मुझे समझ नहीं आया (mujhe samajh nahin aayaa, "I didn't understand"). To ask someone to speak slowly: थोड़ा धीरे बोलिए (thodaa dheere boliye, "please speak a little slowly"). To ask for a repeat: फिर से बोलिए (phir se boliye, "please say it again"). To ask what a word means: इसका मतलब क्या है? (iskaa matlab kyaa hai?, "what does this mean?").

## Shopping and directions — यह कितने का है? (yah kitne kaa hai?)

<!-- topic: shopping; level: elementary -->

At a shop or market in Hindi, ask यह कितने का है? (yah kitne kaa hai?, "how much is this?"). यह is often pronounced "yeh". To ask for something: मुझे पानी चाहिए (mujhe paani chaahiye, "I want water"). To ask for a place, say the place and add कहाँ है? (kahaan hai?, "where is it?"): बस स्टैंड कहाँ है? (bus stand kahaan hai?, "where is the bus stand?").

## Greeting an older person — नमस्ते जी (namaste jee)

<!-- topic: greetings; skill: culture -->

To greet an older person respectfully in Hindi, say नमस्ते जी (namaste jee) or नमस्कार (namaskaar) with your palms pressed together and a slight bow of the head. Add a title for relatives and elders: नमस्ते अंकल (namaste ankal), नमस्ते आंटी (namaste aantee), नमस्ते दादाजी (namaste daadaajee, to grandfather). Always use आप (aap): आप कैसे हैं? (aap kaise hain?, how are you?); to an older woman आप कैसी हैं? (aap kaisee hain?). In many families younger people also touch an elder's feet (पैर छूना, pair chhoonaa) and the elder blesses them with ख़ुश रहो (khush raho, stay happy). In villages of North India you may hear राम राम (raam raam).

## Introducing yourself — name, home town and age

<!-- topic: introductions -->

A simple self-introduction in Hindi: नमस्ते, मेरा नाम आशा है (namaste, meraa naam Asha hai, hello, my name is Asha). मैं जयपुर से हूँ (main Jaipur se hoon, I am from Jaipur). मैं बीस साल की हूँ (main bees saal kee hoon, I am twenty) — a man says बीस साल का हूँ (bees saal kaa hoon). मैं छात्रा हूँ (main chhaatraa hoon, I am a student — woman; a man says छात्र, chhaatr). मैं हिंदी सीख रही हूँ (main hindee seekh rahee hoon, I am learning Hindi — a man says सीख रहा हूँ). Name, place and "is / am" sentences are the same for everyone; only age, student and verb forms change with your gender. End with आपसे मिलकर अच्छा लगा (aapse milkar achchhaa lagaa, nice to meet you).

## Where are you from? — आप कहाँ से हैं? (aap kahaan se hain?)

<!-- topic: introductions -->

To ask where someone is from in Hindi: आप कहाँ से हैं? (aap kahaan se hain?), literally "you where from are?". Casual: तुम कहाँ से हो? (tum kahaan se ho?). Answer with place + से हूँ: मैं भारत से हूँ (main bhaarat se hoon, I am from India), मैं लखनऊ से हूँ (main Lakhnaoo se hoon, I am from Lucknow), मैं अमेरिका से हूँ (main amerikaa se hoon, I am from America). से (se, from) comes after the place, and these sentences are the same for men and women. "Where do you live?": आप कहाँ रहते हैं? (aap kahaan rahte hain?); to a woman रहती हैं (rahtee hain).

## Nice to meet you — आपसे मिलकर अच्छा लगा (aapse milkar achchhaa lagaa)

<!-- topic: introductions -->

"Nice to meet you" in Hindi is आपसे मिलकर अच्छा लगा (aapse milkar achchhaa lagaa), literally "having met you, it felt good". It is the same for men and women. A slightly warmer version is आपसे मिलकर ख़ुशी हुई (aapse milkar khushee huee, I'm happy to have met you). To a friend: तुमसे मिलकर अच्छा लगा (tumse milkar achchhaa lagaa). The reply is मुझे भी (mujhe bhee, me too) or मुझे भी आपसे मिलकर अच्छा लगा.

## Where are you going? — आप कहाँ जा रहे हैं? (aap kahaan jaa rahe hain?)

<!-- topic: everyday-conversation -->

"Where are you going?" in Hindi is आप कहाँ जा रहे हैं? (aap kahaan jaa rahe hain?) politely. The verb changes for the person you ask: to a woman आप कहाँ जा रही हैं? (aap kahaan jaa rahee hain?). To a friend: कहाँ जा रहे हो? (kahaan jaa rahe ho?) — to a female friend कहाँ जा रही हो? — or very casually कहाँ चले? (kahaan chale?). In India this is a friendly question, not nosy. Answers: मैं बाज़ार जा रहा हूँ (main baazaar jaa rahaa hoon, I'm going to the market); a woman says जा रही हूँ. Or just बस, घर (bas, ghar, just home).

## What are you doing? — आप क्या कर रहे हैं? (aap kyaa kar rahe hain?)

<!-- topic: everyday-conversation -->

"What are you doing?" in Hindi is आप क्या कर रहे हैं? (aap kyaa kar rahe hain?) politely; to a woman आप क्या कर रही हैं? (aap kyaa kar rahee hain?). To a friend: क्या कर रहे हो? (kyaa kar rahe ho?) — to a female friend क्या कर रही हो? (kyaa kar rahee ho?). Very casual: क्या चल रहा है? (kyaa chal rahaa hai?, what's going on?). Answers: मैं पढ़ रहा हूँ (main padh rahaa hoon, I'm studying), मैं खाना खा रही हूँ (main khaanaa khaa rahee hoon, I'm eating — woman), कुछ नहीं (kuchh nahin, nothing much). "What do you do (for work)?" is different: आप क्या करते हैं? (aap kyaa karte hain?).

## Good morning — सुप्रभात (suprabhaat)

<!-- topic: greetings -->

The Hindi word for "good morning" is सुप्रभात (suprabhaat), but it is formal: you see it in messages, school assemblies and announcements. In person most Hindi speakers simply say नमस्ते (namaste) or English "good morning", at any time before noon. In a religious household you may also hear जय श्री कृष्ण (jay shree krishn) or राम राम (raam raam) as a morning greeting. To ask about someone's night: अच्छी नींद आई? (achchhee neend aaee?, did you sleep well?).

## Good night — शुभ रात्रि (shubh raatri)

<!-- topic: greetings -->

"Good night" in Hindi is शुभ रात्रि (shubh raatri), literally "auspicious night". It is formal and mostly used in messages; in speech people usually say English "good night". At home before bed you may hear चलो, सो जाओ (chalo, so jaao, okay, go to sleep) from parents, or अच्छा, सोते हैं (achchhaa, sote hain, okay, let's sleep). When leaving someone's house at night, say नमस्ते (namaste) or फिर मिलेंगे (phir milenge, see you again), and a man can say चलता हूँ (chaltaa hoon, I'll be off), a woman चलती हूँ (chaltee hoon).

## You're welcome — कोई बात नहीं (koee baat nahin)

<!-- topic: politeness -->

The natural Hindi reply to "thank you" is कोई बात नहीं (koee baat nahin, it's nothing / no problem), often with जी: कोई बात नहीं जी. आपका स्वागत है (aapkaa svaagat hai) literally means "you are welcome (here)" — it is used to welcome guests and in customer service, and sounds English-style as a reply to thanks. Among friends: अरे, कोई बात नहीं (are, koee baat nahin) or just "welcome". The same कोई बात नहीं also answers "sorry", meaning "never mind".
