# College and work in Hindi

## I am a student — मैं छात्र हूँ (main chhaatr hoon)

<!-- level: beginner -->

"I am a student" in Hindi is मैं छात्र हूँ (main chhaatr hoon) for a man and मैं छात्रा हूँ (main chhaatraa hoon) for a woman. A gender-neutral word is विद्यार्थी (vidyaarthee): मैं विद्यार्थी हूँ. Many young people simply say मैं स्टूडेंट हूँ (main stoodent hoon). To say where: मैं दिल्ली विश्वविद्यालय में पढ़ता हूँ (main Dillee vishvavidyaalay mein padhtaa hoon, I study at Delhi University); a woman says पढ़ती हूँ (padhtee hoon). पढ़ना (padhnaa) means both "to read" and "to study".

Also asked as: how to say I am a student in Hindi, I study at, student in Hindi, female student

## What do you study? — आप क्या पढ़ते हैं? (aap kyaa padhte hain?)

To ask what someone studies in Hindi: आप क्या पढ़ते हैं? (aap kyaa padhte hain?); to a woman आप क्या पढ़ती हैं? (aap kyaa padhtee hain?); to a friend तुम क्या पढ़ रहे हो? (tum kyaa padh rahe ho?). Answer: मैं कंप्यूटर साइंस पढ़ता हूँ (main kampyootar saains padhtaa hoon, I study computer science) — a woman says पढ़ती हूँ. Subjects are usually said in English. Year of study: मैं दूसरे साल में हूँ (main doosre saal mein hoon, I'm in second year) or मैं सेकंड ईयर में हूँ (main sekand eeyar mein hoon), the same for men and women.

Also asked as: what is your subject in Hindi, what do you study, which year are you in, my major in Hindi

## Teacher — शिक्षक, टीचर, सर / मैडम

The Hindi word for teacher is शिक्षक (shikshak, male) / शिक्षिका (shikshikaa, female), also अध्यापक (adhyaapak). In everyday college life students say टीचर (teechar) or प्रोफ़ेसर (profesar), and they address teachers as सर (sar) or मैडम (maidam), sometimes with जी: सर जी. A classic school word is गुरुजी (gurujee). Always use आप with teachers: सर, आप कल आएँगे? (sar, aap kal aaenge?, sir, will you come tomorrow?). "Our teacher is very good": हमारे टीचर बहुत अच्छे हैं (hamaare teechar bahut achchhe hain) — plural for respect.

Also asked as: how to say teacher in Hindi, how to address a teacher, professor in Hindi, sir and madam

## Class and classroom — क्लास (klaas)

Students in Hindi say क्लास (klaas) for both a class and a classroom; the formal word is कक्षा (kakshaa). Useful sentences: क्लास कहाँ है? (klaas kahaan hai?, where is the class?), क्लास कितने बजे है? (klaas kitne baje hai?, what time is class?), आज क्लास नहीं है (aaj klaas nahin hai, there's no class today), मैं क्लास के लिए लेट हो गया (main klaas ke lie let ho gayaa, I'm late for class — a woman: लेट हो गई). A lecture is लेक्चर (lekchar) and a period is पीरियड (peeriyad). At the door: सर, क्या मैं अंदर आ सकता हूँ? (sar, kyaa main andar aa saktaa hoon?, may I come in?) — a woman says सकती हूँ.

Also asked as: classroom in Hindi, where is the class, I'm late for class, may I come in sir

## Exam — परीक्षा (pareekshaa) / एग्ज़ाम (egzaam)

"Exam" in Hindi is परीक्षा (pareekshaa, feminine), but students mostly say एग्ज़ाम (egzaam, masculine). Examples: कल मेरी परीक्षा है (kal meree pareekshaa hai) or कल मेरा एग्ज़ाम है (kal meraa egzaam hai), both "I have an exam tomorrow". परीक्षा कब है? (pareekshaa kab hai?, when is the exam?). Results: रिज़ल्ट (rizalt) or परिणाम (parinaam). "I passed": मैं पास हो गया (main paas ho gayaa); a woman says पास हो गई (paas ho gaee). Wishing luck: ऑल द बेस्ट or शुभकामनाएँ (shubhkaamnaaen, best wishes).

Also asked as: how to say exam in Hindi, I have an exam tomorrow, I passed, good luck for the exam

## Homework and assignments — होमवर्क (homvark)

In Hindi, students say होमवर्क (homvark) for homework; the textbook word is गृहकार्य (grihakaarya). In college people say असाइनमेंट (asaainment). Useful sentences: मुझे होमवर्क करना है (mujhe homvark karnaa hai, I have to do homework), असाइनमेंट कब जमा करना है? (asaainment kab jamaa karnaa hai?, when do we have to submit the assignment?) — जमा करना (jamaa karnaa) means to submit. "I haven't finished": मेरा काम पूरा नहीं हुआ (meraa kaam pooraa nahin huaa), the same for men and women. Notebook: कॉपी (kopee); book: किताब (kitaab).

Also asked as: homework in Hindi, assignment submission, when is it due, I haven't finished my homework

## What do you do? (job) — आप क्या करते हैं? (aap kyaa karte hain?)

To ask about someone's job in Hindi: आप क्या करते हैं? (aap kyaa karte hain?, what do you do?); to a woman आप क्या करती हैं? (aap kyaa kartee hain?). Also: आप क्या काम करते हैं? (aap kyaa kaam karte hain?). Answers: मैं इंजीनियर हूँ (main injeeniyar hoon, I'm an engineer), मैं एक कंपनी में काम करता हूँ (main ek kampanee mein kaam kartaa hoon, I work in a company) — a woman says करती हूँ (kartee hoon). Job titles are mostly English: डॉक्टर, टीचर, मैनेजर. A (salaried) job is नौकरी (naukaree); work in general is काम (kaam).

Also asked as: how to ask what someone does for work in Hindi, what is your job, I work as, my profession

## Office — दफ़्तर (daftar) / ऑफ़िस (ofis)

"Office" in Hindi is दफ़्तर (daftar) or, just as common, ऑफ़िस (ofis); government offices show कार्यालय (kaaryaalay) on signs. Examples: मैं ऑफ़िस जा रहा हूँ (main ofis jaa rahaa hoon, I'm going to the office) — a woman says जा रही हूँ, मैं दफ़्तर में काम करता हूँ (main daftar mein kaam kartaa hoon, I work in an office) — a woman says करती हूँ. "I'm at the office": मैं ऑफ़िस में हूँ (main ofis mein hoon), the same for everyone. "Working from home" is said in English: वर्क फ़्रॉम होम.

Also asked as: how to say office in Hindi, I am going to work, I work in an office, at the office

## Colleague and boss — सहकर्मी (sahkarmee)

The formal Hindi word for colleague is सहकर्मी (sahkarmee), but at work people usually say कलीग (kaleeg) or describe it: वे मेरे साथ काम करते हैं (ve mere saath kaam karte hain, he works with me — respectful plural; about a woman: करती हैं, kartee hain). The boss is बॉस (bos) or, more traditionally, साहब (saahab) / मैडम (maidam). Use आप and -जी with seniors: शर्मा जी, आप मीटिंग में आएँगे? (Sharmaa jee, aap meeting mein aaenge?, Mr Sharma, will you come to the meeting?). Close colleagues of the same age use तुम or first names.

## Meeting — मीटिंग (meeting)

Hindi speakers say मीटिंग (meeting) for a work meeting; it is feminine. Examples: मेरी मीटिंग है (meree meeting hai, I have a meeting), मीटिंग कितने बजे है? (meeting kitne baje hai?, what time is the meeting?), मीटिंग शुरू हो गई (meeting shuroo ho gaee, the meeting has started), मैं मीटिंग में हूँ, बाद में फ़ोन करता हूँ (main meeting mein hoon, baad mein fon kartaa hoon, I'm in a meeting, I'll call you later — a woman: करती हूँ). The formal Hindi word is बैठक (baithak), used in official and political contexts.

Also asked as: how to say meeting in Hindi, I have a meeting, I'm in a meeting, what time is the meeting

## Asking for leave — छुट्टी चाहिए (chhuttee chaahiye)

छुट्टी (chhuttee) means a holiday, leave or day off. To ask for leave in Hindi: मुझे कल छुट्टी चाहिए (mujhe kal chhuttee chaahiye, I need leave tomorrow), the same for men and women. Politely to a boss or teacher: सर, क्या मुझे कल छुट्टी मिल सकती है? (sar, kyaa mujhe kal chhuttee mil saktee hai?, sir, may I have tomorrow off?). "Today is a holiday": आज छुट्टी है (aaj chhuttee hai). "I'm on leave": मैं छुट्टी पर हूँ (main chhuttee par hoon). "I'm sick": मेरी तबीयत ठीक नहीं है (meree tabeeyat theek nahin hai).

Also asked as: how to ask for a day off in Hindi, I need leave, today is a holiday, sick leave in Hindi

## College places — library, canteen, hostel

Hindi speakers on campus use English names for most places: लाइब्रेरी (laaibreree, library; formal पुस्तकालय, pustakaalay), कैंटीन (kainteen, canteen), हॉस्टल (hostal, hostel; formal छात्रावास, chhaatraavaas), लैब (laib, lab). Questions: लाइब्रेरी कहाँ है? (laaibreree kahaan hai?, where is the library?), चलो, कैंटीन चलते हैं (chalo, kainteen chalte hain, let's go to the canteen). "I live in the hostel": मैं हॉस्टल में रहता हूँ (main hostal mein rahtaa hoon); a woman says रहती हूँ (rahtee hoon).

Also asked as: library in Hindi, campus words in Hindi, canteen, I live in the hostel

## Talking about classmates and seniors

In Hindi college life, classmates are usually दोस्त (dost, friends) or English क्लासमेट (klaasmet). Students often call seniors भैया (bhaiyaa) and दीदी (deedee) as a mark of respect, and they use आप with them at first. Asking someone's year: तुम किस साल में हो? (tum kis saal mein ho?, which year are you in?). Introducing a friend: यह मेरा दोस्त है (yah meraa dost hai, this is my friend); for a female friend यह मेरी दोस्त है (yah meree dost hai). New students hear क्या तुम यहाँ नए हो? (kyaa tum yahaan nae ho?, are you new here?) — to a girl नई हो (naee ho).
