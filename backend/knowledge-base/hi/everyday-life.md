# Everyday life in Hindi

## Daily routine — मेरी दिनचर्या (meree dinacharyaa)

To describe your day in Hindi, use the habitual present: verb stem + ता / ती + हूँ. A man says मैं सुबह छह बजे उठता हूँ (main subah chhah baje uthtaa hoon, I get up at six in the morning), नहाता हूँ (nahaataa hoon, I bathe), नाश्ता करता हूँ (naashtaa kartaa hoon, I have breakfast) and कॉलेज जाता हूँ (kolej jaataa hoon, I go to college). A woman uses -ती: उठती हूँ, नहाती हूँ, नाश्ता करती हूँ, जाती हूँ. "Every day" is रोज़ (roz) or हर दिन (har din): मैं रोज़ व्यायाम करता हूँ (main roz vyaayaam kartaa hoon, I exercise every day). दिनचर्या (dinacharyaa) is the formal word for daily routine.

## Getting ready and leaving home

<!-- type: phrase -->

Useful Hindi for the morning rush: मैं तैयार हो रहा हूँ (main taiyaar ho rahaa hoon, I'm getting ready) — a woman says तैयार हो रही हूँ (taiyaar ho rahee hoon). मैं निकल रहा हूँ (main nikal rahaa hoon, I'm heading out); a woman says निकल रही हूँ. "I'm late": मुझे देर हो रही है (mujhe der ho rahee hai, I'm getting late), the same for everyone. Hurrying someone: जल्दी करो! (jaldee karo!, hurry up!). Families say जल्दी आना (jaldee aanaa, come back early) and ध्यान से जाना (dhyaan se jaanaa, go carefully). Coming home: मैं घर आ गया (main ghar aa gayaa, I'm home); a woman says आ गई (aa gaee).

Also asked as: I'm getting ready in Hindi, I'm leaving, I'm late, I'm home

## Rooms in the house — कमरा, रसोई (kamraa, rasoee)

<!-- type: vocabulary; skill: vocabulary; topic: home -->

Hindi words for the home: घर (ghar, house / home), कमरा (kamraa, room), रसोई (rasoee, kitchen; also किचन), बाथरूम (baathroom, bathroom), बैठक (baithak, sitting room; also ड्रॉइंग रूम), छत (chhat, roof / terrace — many families sit on the roof in the evening), दरवाज़ा (darvaazaa, door), खिड़की (khidkee, window), बिस्तर (bistar, bed), कुर्सी (kursee, chair), मेज़ (mez, table). Examples: माँ रसोई में हैं (maa rasoee mein hain, Mum is in the kitchen — respectful plural), दरवाज़ा बंद कर दो (darvaazaa band kar do, close the door).

Also asked as: rooms of the house in Hindi, kitchen in Hindi, home vocabulary, door and window in Hindi

## Household chores — घर का काम (ghar kaa kaam)

<!-- topic: home -->

Housework in Hindi is घर का काम (ghar kaa kaam). Common chores: खाना बनाना (khaanaa banaanaa, to cook), बर्तन धोना (bartan dhonaa, to wash the dishes), कपड़े धोना (kapde dhonaa, to wash clothes), झाड़ू लगाना (jhaadoo lagaanaa, to sweep), पोंछा लगाना (ponchhaa lagaanaa, to mop), सामान लाना (saamaan laanaa, to bring groceries). Examples: मैं बर्तन धो रहा हूँ (main bartan dho rahaa hoon, I'm washing the dishes) — a woman says धो रही हूँ; आज खाना कौन बनाएगा? (aaj khaanaa kaun banaaegaa?, who will cook today?). Offering help: मैं मदद करूँ? (main madad karoon?, shall I help?), the same for men and women.

## What's the weather like? — मौसम कैसा है? (mausam kaisaa hai?)

<!-- topic: weather -->

To ask about the weather in Hindi: आज मौसम कैसा है? (aaj mausam kaisaa hai?, what's the weather like today?). Hindi describes weather mostly with nouns: आज गर्मी है (aaj garmee hai, it's hot today), आज ठंड है (aaj thand hai, it's cold today), बारिश हो रही है (baarish ho rahee hai, it's raining), धूप है (dhoop hai, it's sunny), मौसम अच्छा है (mausam achchhaa hai, the weather is nice). Feeling cold yourself: मुझे ठंड लग रही है (mujhe thand lag rahee hai, I'm feeling cold), the same for men and women. Seasons: गर्मी (garmee, summer), बरसात (barsaat, monsoon), सर्दी (sardee, winter).

## On the phone — फ़ोन पर (fon par)

<!-- topic: phone -->

Hindi phone phrases: हेलो? (helo?, hello?), कौन बोल रहा है? (kaun bol rahaa hai?, who's speaking?), मैं रवि बोल रहा हूँ (main Ravi bol rahaa hoon, it's Ravi speaking) — a woman says बोल रही हूँ. "Can you hear me?": आवाज़ आ रही है? (aavaaz aa rahee hai?). "I'll call you later": मैं बाद में फ़ोन करूँगा (main baad mein fon karoongaa); a woman says करूँगी. "Send me a message": मुझे मैसेज कर देना (mujhe maisej kar denaa). "My phone's battery is dead": मेरे फ़ोन की बैटरी ख़त्म हो गई (mere fon kee baitaree khatm ho gaee). "Do you have a charger?": चार्जर है? (chaarjar hai?).

Also asked as: phone conversation in Hindi, I'll call you back, my battery is dead, send me a message

## Spending time with friends — घूमने चलें? (ghoomne chalen?)

<!-- topic: friends -->

To suggest going out with friends in Hindi: चलो, घूमने चलते हैं (chalo, ghoomne chalte hain, let's go out / for a stroll). घूमना (ghoomnaa) means to wander, go out or travel for fun. Other ideas: फ़िल्म देखने चलें? (film dekhne chalen?, shall we go and watch a film?), चलो, चाय पीते हैं (chalo, chaay peete hain, let's have tea), कुछ खाने चलें? (kuchh khaane chalen?, shall we go and eat something?). Friends call each other यार (yaar, buddy): क्या प्लान है, यार? (kyaa plaan hai, yaar?, what's the plan?). "That was fun!": बहुत मज़ा आया! (bahut mazaa aayaa!).

Also asked as: let's hang out in Hindi, going out with friends, shall we watch a movie, what's the plan

## Inviting someone — आप ज़रूर आइए (aap zaroor aaiye)

<!-- topic: invitations -->

To invite someone in Hindi: शनिवार को मेरे घर आइए (shanivaar ko mere ghar aaiye, please come to my house on Saturday), or warmly आप ज़रूर आइए (aap zaroor aaiye, you must come). To a friend: कल मेरे घर आओ (kal mere ghar aao). For a party: मेरे जन्मदिन की पार्टी है, आना ज़रूर (mere janmadin kee paartee hai, aanaa zaroor, it's my birthday party, do come). Asking if someone is free: क्या आप कल फ़्री हैं? (kyaa aap kal free hain?). Invitations are often repeated with insistence — that's politeness, not pressure.

Also asked as: how to invite someone in Hindi, come to my house, please come to my party, are you free tomorrow

## Accepting or declining — ज़रूर / नहीं आ पाऊँगा

<!-- topic: invitations -->

To accept an invitation in Hindi: हाँ, ज़रूर आऊँगा (haan, zaroor aaoongaa, yes, I'll definitely come); a woman says आऊँगी (aaoongee). To decline politely: माफ़ कीजिए, मैं नहीं आ पाऊँगा (maaf keejiye, main nahin aa paaoongaa, sorry, I won't be able to come); a woman says आ पाऊँगी (aa paaoongee). पाना (paanaa) means "to manage to". Giving a reason softens it: मुझे कुछ काम है (mujhe kuchh kaam hai, I have some work). To a friend: सॉरी यार, अगली बार पक्का (sorry yaar, aglee baar pakkaa, sorry buddy, next time for sure).

Also asked as: how to accept an invitation in Hindi, I can't come, sorry I'm busy, decline politely

## I'm busy / I'm free — मैं बिज़ी हूँ (main bizee hoon)

<!-- type: phrase -->

"I'm busy" in Hindi is usually said with English: मैं बिज़ी हूँ (main bizee hoon); the Hindi word is व्यस्त (vyast): मैं थोड़ा व्यस्त हूँ (main thodaa vyast hoon, I'm a bit busy). "I'm free" is मैं फ़्री हूँ (main free hoon) or मैं ख़ाली हूँ (main khaalee hoon). All of these are the same for men and women. "I have work right now": अभी मुझे काम है (abhee mujhe kaam hai). "When are you free?": आप कब फ़्री हैं? (aap kab free hain?); casually तुम कब फ़्री हो? (tum kab free ho?).

Also asked as: how to say I'm busy in Hindi, I'm free now, when are you free, I have work

## Going to sleep — सोने जा रहा हूँ (sone jaa rahaa hoon)

<!-- topic: daily-routine -->

At night in Hindi: मैं सोने जा रहा हूँ (main sone jaa rahaa hoon, I'm going to sleep); a woman says सोने जा रही हूँ (sone jaa rahee hoon). "I'm sleepy": मुझे नींद आ रही है (mujhe neend aa rahee hai), the same for everyone. Parents tell children सो जाओ (so jaao, go to sleep). "Good night" is usually said in English, or शुभ रात्रि (shubh raatri) in messages. In the morning: अच्छी नींद आई? (achchhee neend aaee?, did you sleep well?) — हाँ, अच्छी नींद आई (haan, achchhee neend aaee, yes, I slept well).

Also asked as: I'm going to bed in Hindi, I'm sleepy, did you sleep well, go to sleep in Hindi

## Family time and visiting relatives

<!-- topic: family -->

Visiting relatives is a big part of everyday life in Hindi-speaking families. Useful phrases: हम नानी के घर जा रहे हैं (ham naanee ke ghar jaa rahe hain, we're going to grandma's house — mother's side), सब लोग कैसे हैं? (sab log kaise hain?, how is everyone?), घर पर सब ठीक है? (ghar par sab theek hai?, is everyone well at home?). Greeting elders: नमस्ते or touching their feet (पैर छूना, pair chhoonaa); they bless you with ख़ुश रहो (khush raho, stay happy) or जीते रहो (jeete raho, live long). Leaving: अच्छा, चलते हैं (achchhaa, chalte hain, okay, we'll be off).

Also asked as: visiting family in Hindi, how is everyone, greeting elders, family visits

## Running errands — बाज़ार, बैंक, दुकान

<!-- topic: errands -->

For everyday errands in Hindi: मैं बाज़ार जा रहा हूँ (main baazaar jaa rahaa hoon, I'm going to the market) — a woman says जा रही हूँ; कुछ चाहिए? (kuchh chaahiye?, do you need anything?); दूध ले आना (doodh le aanaa, bring milk, please — casual); बैंक कितने बजे खुलता है? (baink kitne baje khultaa hai?, what time does the bank open?); दुकान बंद है (dukaan band hai, the shop is closed). For the grocery shop people often say किराने की दुकान (kiraane kee dukaan). Bringing something back: मैं सब्ज़ी ले आया (main sabzee le aayaa, I've brought the vegetables); a woman says ले आई (le aaee).

Also asked as: going shopping in Hindi, do you need anything, bring milk please, what time does it open
