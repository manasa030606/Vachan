# Feelings and relationships in Hindi

## How feelings work in Hindi: मैं … हूँ or मुझे …

<!-- type: explanation; skill: grammar -->

Hindi expresses feelings in two ways. Some use मैं + adjective + हूँ (main … hoon): मैं ख़ुश हूँ (main khush hoon, I am happy), मैं उदास हूँ (main udaas hoon, I am sad); adjectives ending in -आ change for a woman, e.g. थका → थकी (thakaa → thakee). Others use the "to me" pattern with मुझे (mujhe), where the feeling "comes" or "strikes" you: मुझे डर लग रहा है (mujhe dar lag rahaa hai, I'm scared), मुझे ग़ुस्सा आ रहा है (mujhe gussaa aa rahaa hai, I'm angry), मुझे तुम्हारी याद आ रही है (I miss you). The मुझे sentences never change with the speaker's gender, so they are great for beginners.

## I am happy — मैं ख़ुश हूँ (main khush hoon)

"I am happy" in Hindi is मैं ख़ुश हूँ (main khush hoon). ख़ुश (khush, also written खुश) never changes, so men and women say the same thing. "Very happy": मैं बहुत ख़ुश हूँ (main bahut khush hoon). "I'm happy for you": मैं तुम्हारे लिए बहुत ख़ुश हूँ (main tumhaare lie bahut khush hoon); politely आपके लिए (aapke lie). For enjoying something: मज़ा आ गया! (mazaa aa gayaa!, that was great fun!). The formal word for happy is प्रसन्न (prasann), seen in writing.

## I am sad — मैं उदास हूँ (main udaas hoon)

"I am sad" in Hindi is मैं उदास हूँ (main udaas hoon); उदास (udaas) does not change for gender. Also common: मैं दुखी हूँ (main dukhee hoon, I am unhappy / hurting) and मेरा मन उदास है (meraa man udaas hai, my heart is heavy), both the same for men and women. "I feel bad (about it)" is मुझे बुरा लग रहा है (mujhe buraa lag rahaa hai). To ask why someone is sad: आप उदास क्यों हैं? (aap udaas kyon hain?) or casually तुम उदास क्यों हो? (tum udaas kyon ho?).

## I am tired — मैं थक गया हूँ (main thak gayaa hoon)

"I am tired" in Hindi is usually said as "I have got tired": a man says मैं थक गया हूँ (main thak gayaa hoon) and a woman says मैं थक गई हूँ (main thak gaee hoon). You can also use the adjective: मैं थका हुआ हूँ (main thakaa huaa hoon) / मैं थकी हुई हूँ (main thakee huee hoon). "Very tired": मैं बहुत थक गया हूँ (woman: थक गई हूँ). "I'm sleepy" is मुझे नींद आ रही है (mujhe neend aa rahee hai), the same for everyone. "I need rest": मुझे आराम चाहिए (mujhe aaraam chaahiye).

## I am angry — मुझे ग़ुस्सा आ रहा है (mujhe gussaa aa rahaa hai)

"I'm angry" in Hindi is मुझे ग़ुस्सा आ रहा है (mujhe gussaa aa rahaa hai), literally "anger is coming to me"; it is the same for men and women. ग़ुस्सा (gussaa, anger; also written गुस्सा). If you are upset WITH someone, use नाराज़ (naaraaz): मैं तुमसे नाराज़ हूँ (main tumse naaraaz hoon, I'm upset with you), also the same for men and women. To ask: आप नाराज़ हैं? (aap naaraaz hain?, are you upset?). To calm someone: ग़ुस्सा मत करो (gussaa mat karo, don't be angry) or शांत हो जाओ (shaant ho jaao, calm down).

Also asked as: how to say I am angry in Hindi, I'm upset with you, calm down, don't be angry

## I am bored — मैं बोर हो रहा हूँ (main bor ho rahaa hoon)

Hindi speakers use the English word "bore" for boredom: a man says मैं बोर हो रहा हूँ (main bor ho rahaa hoon, I'm getting bored) and a woman says मैं बोर हो रही हूँ (main bor ho rahee hoon). Already bored: मैं बोर हो गया (woman: बोर हो गई, bor ho gaee). "This is boring": यह बोरिंग है (yah boring hai) or बहुत बोर है (bahut bor hai). A more Hindi way to say "I'm not enjoying it" is मज़ा नहीं आ रहा (mazaa nahin aa rahaa), the same for everyone. Note: "बोर करना" means to bore someone: मुझे बोर मत करो (don't bore me).

## I am scared — मुझे डर लग रहा है (mujhe dar lag rahaa hai)

"I'm scared" in Hindi is मुझे डर लग रहा है (mujhe dar lag rahaa hai), literally "fear is striking me" — the same for men and women. डर (dar) means fear. "I'm afraid of dogs": मुझे कुत्तों से डर लगता है (mujhe kutton se dar lagtaa hai). After a sudden fright: मैं डर गया (main dar gayaa); a woman says मैं डर गई (main dar gaee). To reassure someone: डरो मत (daro mat, don't be afraid) or politely डरिए मत (dariye mat).

## I am excited — मैं बहुत एक्साइटेड हूँ (main bahut eksaaited hoon)

Young Hindi speakers mostly use English: मैं बहुत एक्साइटेड हूँ (main bahut eksaaited hoon, I'm very excited), the same for men and women. The formal Hindi word is उत्साहित (utsaahit): मैं बहुत उत्साहित हूँ (main bahut utsaahit hoon), used in writing and speeches. A natural everyday way to say you can't wait: मैं इंतज़ार नहीं कर सकता (main intazaar nahin kar saktaa, I can't wait); a woman says नहीं कर सकती (nahin kar saktee). To say something was thrilling or fun: बहुत मज़ा आया! (bahut mazaa aayaa!, it was so much fun!).

## I am worried — मुझे चिंता हो रही है (mujhe chintaa ho rahee hai)

"I'm worried" in Hindi is मुझे चिंता हो रही है (mujhe chintaa ho rahee hai), literally "worry is happening to me"; it is the same for men and women. Also very common: मैं परेशान हूँ (main pareshaan hoon, I'm worried / troubled), which also does not change. "I'm worried about you": मुझे तुम्हारी चिंता हो रही है (mujhe tumhaaree chintaa ho rahee hai). Young people say मुझे टेंशन है (mujhe tenshan hai, I'm stressed). The reply is चिंता मत कीजिए (chintaa mat keejiye, please don't worry), casually चिंता मत करो or टेंशन मत लो (tenshan mat lo).

Also asked as: how to say I am worried in Hindi, I'm stressed, don't worry, I'm worried about you

## I love you — मैं तुमसे प्यार करता हूँ (main tumse pyaar kartaa hoon)

"I love you" in Hindi is मैं तुमसे प्यार करता हूँ (main tumse pyaar kartaa hoon) when a man says it; a woman says मैं तुमसे प्यार करती हूँ (main tumse pyaar kartee hoon). प्यार (pyaar) means love and तुमसे (tumse) is "with you / to you". With the respectful आप: मैं आपसे प्यार करता हूँ / करती हूँ (main aapse pyaar kartaa / kartee hoon). It is strong and romantic, said to a partner; families rarely say it aloud. "I love you too": मैं भी तुमसे प्यार करता हूँ / करती हूँ. Many couples simply say "I love you" in English. In poems and songs you will hear मोहब्बत (mohabbat) and इश्क़ (ishq) for love.

## I miss you — मुझे तुम्हारी याद आ रही है (mujhe tumhaaree yaad aa rahee hai)

"I miss you" in Hindi is मुझे तुम्हारी याद आ रही है (mujhe tumhaaree yaad aa rahee hai), literally "your memory is coming to me". It is the same for men and women, and fine for friends and family as well as partners. याद (yaad) means memory, and याद आना (yaad aanaa) means "to miss". Respectful (to a parent or elder): मुझे आपकी याद आ रही है (mujhe aapkee yaad aa rahee hai). "I always miss you": मुझे तुम्हारी बहुत याद आती है (mujhe tumhaaree bahut yaad aatee hai). "Miss you" in English is common in messages. Reply: मुझे भी (mujhe bhee, me too).

## I like you — तुम मुझे अच्छे लगते हो (tum mujhe achchhe lagte ho)

A gentle way to say "I like you" in Hindi is तुम मुझे अच्छे लगते हो (tum mujhe achchhe lagte ho), literally "you seem good to me". The words change for the person you are talking to, not for you: to a woman say तुम मुझे अच्छी लगती हो (tum mujhe achchhee lagtee ho). Another common form is मुझे तुम पसंद हो (mujhe tum pasand ho, I like you), the same for everyone. Both can mean friendly or romantic liking depending on the tone. For a stronger romantic feeling: मैं तुम्हें पसंद करता हूँ (main tumhen pasand kartaa hoon); a woman says पसंद करती हूँ.

## I'm sorry — माफ़ कीजिए / मुझे माफ़ कर दो

<!-- topic: apologies -->

For a small mistake in Hindi say माफ़ कीजिए (maaf keejiye, please forgive me / sorry) or simply "sorry". For a real apology to someone close: मुझे माफ़ कर दो (mujhe maaf kar do, please forgive me); politely मुझे माफ़ कर दीजिए (mujhe maaf kar deejiye). These are the same for men and women. A formal apology is मैं माफ़ी चाहता हूँ (main maafee chaahtaa hoon, I apologise); a woman says मैं माफ़ी चाहती हूँ (main maafee chaahtee hoon). The warm reply is कोई बात नहीं (koee baat nahin, it's okay / never mind).

## Congratulations — बधाई हो (badhaaee ho)

"Congratulations" in Hindi is बधाई हो! (badhaaee ho!) or मुबारक हो! (mubaarak ho!); both are used for good news like a new job, a wedding or a baby. "Many congratulations": बहुत-बहुत बधाई (bahut-bahut badhaaee). "Happy birthday": जन्मदिन मुबारक हो (janmadin mubaarak ho), जन्मदिन की शुभकामनाएँ (janmadin kee shubhkaamnaaen, birthday wishes), or "happy birthday" in English. For festivals: दिवाली की शुभकामनाएँ (divaalee kee shubhkaamnaaen), ईद मुबारक (eed mubaarak). Reply with धन्यवाद (dhanyavaad) or शुक्रिया (shukriyaa).

## Take care, get well soon — अपना ख़याल रखिए (apnaa khayaal rakhiye)

<!-- topic: health -->

"Take care" in Hindi is अपना ख़याल रखिए (apnaa khayaal rakhiye) politely, or अपना ख़याल रखना (apnaa khayaal rakhnaa) to a friend; English "take care" is also common. "Get well soon": जल्दी ठीक हो जाइए (jaldee theek ho jaaiye) politely, or जल्दी ठीक हो जाओ (jaldee theek ho jaao) to a friend. Asking about health: अब तबीयत कैसी है? (ab tabeeyat kaisee hai?, how are you feeling now?). Elders often add a blessing: भगवान आपको जल्दी ठीक करे (bhagvaan aapko jaldee theek kare, may God make you well soon).

## Comforting someone — चिंता मत करो, सब ठीक हो जाएगा

<!-- topic: comfort -->

To comfort someone in Hindi, the most common words are चिंता मत करो, सब ठीक हो जाएगा (chintaa mat karo, sab theek ho jaaegaa, don't worry, everything will be fine). Politely: चिंता मत कीजिए (chintaa mat keejiye). Other kind phrases: मैं तुम्हारे साथ हूँ (main tumhaare saath hoon, I am with you), हिम्मत रखो (himmat rakho, stay strong / be brave), कोई बात नहीं (koee baat nahin, it's okay). For sad news such as a death: सुनकर बहुत दुख हुआ (sunkar bahut dukh huaa, I am very sorry to hear this), the same for men and women.

## Talking about relationships — friend, partner, family

<!-- topic: relationships -->

Useful Hindi words for relationships: दोस्त (dost, friend), सहेली (sahelee, a woman's female friend), सबसे अच्छा दोस्त (sabse achchhaa dost, best friend) — young people say बेस्ट फ्रेंड. A partner is often called by English words: बॉयफ्रेंड, गर्लफ्रेंड. Married: पति (pati, husband), पत्नी (patnee, wife). "We are friends": हम दोस्त हैं (ham dost hain). "Are you married?": क्या आपकी शादी हो गई है? (kyaa aapkee shaadee ho gaee hai?), the same for men and women. "I'm single" is usually said in English, or मेरी शादी नहीं हुई है (meree shaadee nahin huee hai, I'm not married).

## Are you okay? — आप ठीक हैं? (aap theek hain?)

"Are you okay?" in Hindi is आप ठीक हैं? (aap theek hain?) politely, or तुम ठीक हो? (tum theek ho?) to a friend. ठीक (theek, fine) does not change for gender, so these work for anyone. Other caring questions: क्या हुआ? (kyaa huaa?, what happened?), सब ठीक है? (sab theek hai?, is everything okay?), तुम्हें क्या परेशानी है? (tumhen kyaa pareshaanee hai?, what's troubling you?). Answers: मैं ठीक हूँ (main theek hoon, I'm fine), कुछ नहीं, बस थोड़ा थका हुआ हूँ (kuchh nahin, bas thodaa thakaa huaa hoon, nothing, just a bit tired — a woman says थकी हुई हूँ).
