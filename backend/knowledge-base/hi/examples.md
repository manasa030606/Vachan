# Hindi example sentences

## "My name is Asha" — मेरा नाम आशा है

Hindi: मेरा नाम आशा है (meraa naam Asha hai). Word by word: मेरा (meraa) = my, नाम (naam) = name, आशा (Asha) = Asha, है (hai) = is. Hindi puts "is" at the end of the sentence. Question form: आपका नाम क्या है? (aapkaa naam kyaa hai?) = your name what is? = "What is your name?" मेरा stays the same whether a man or a woman is speaking, because it agrees with नाम, a masculine noun.

## "How are you? I am fine."

<!-- topic: everyday-phrases -->

Hindi: आप कैसे हैं? (aap kaise hain?) — आप (aap) = you (respectful), कैसे (kaise) = how, हैं (hain) = are. To a woman: आप कैसी हैं? (aap kaisee hain?). Answer: मैं ठीक हूँ (main theek hoon) — मैं (main) = I, ठीक (theek) = fine / all right, हूँ (hoon) = am. The answer is the same for men and women, because ठीक does not change.

## "I want water" — मुझे पानी चाहिए

<!-- topic: dative; level: elementary -->

Hindi: मुझे पानी चाहिए (mujhe paani chaahiye). Word by word: मुझे (mujhe) = to me (मैं + को), पानी (paani) = water, चाहिए (chaahiye) = is needed / wanted. Literally "to me water is needed". Swap the noun to ask for other things: मुझे चाय चाहिए (mujhe chaay chaahiye, I want tea), मुझे खाना चाहिए (mujhe khaanaa chaahiye, I want food).

## "I am going home" — मैं घर जा रहा हूँ

<!-- topic: continuous; level: elementary -->

Hindi: मैं घर जा रहा हूँ (main ghar jaa rahaa hoon). Word by word: मैं (main) = I, घर (ghar) = home, जा (jaa) = go, रहा (rahaa) = -ing (masculine), हूँ (hoon) = am. A woman says मैं घर जा रही हूँ (main ghar jaa rahee hoon). The verb comes last, and with घर and a verb of motion Hindi usually needs no word for "to".

## "I don't know Hindi well" — मुझे हिंदी ठीक से नहीं आती

<!-- topic: negation; level: elementary -->

Hindi: मुझे हिंदी ठीक से नहीं आती (mujhe hindee theek se nahin aatee). Word by word: मुझे (mujhe) = to me, हिंदी (hindee) = Hindi, ठीक से (theek se) = properly / well, नहीं (nahin) = not, आती (aatee) = comes. Hindi says a language "comes" to you: मुझे हिंदी आती है (mujhe hindee aatee hai) = I know Hindi. The verb is आती (feminine) because हिंदी is a feminine noun.

## "Ram's book is on the table" — राम की किताब मेज़ पर है

<!-- topic: postpositions; level: elementary -->

Hindi: राम की किताब मेज़ पर है (Ram kee kitaab mez par hai). Word by word: राम (Ram) = Ram, की (kee) = 's / of, किताब (kitaab) = book, मेज़ (mez) = table, पर (par) = on, है (hai) = is. की is used because किताब is feminine; with a masculine noun it would be का: राम का घर (Ram kaa ghar, Ram's house). "On the table" is "table on" — the postposition follows the noun.
