# Hindi sentence patterns

## I want … — मुझे … चाहिए (mujhe … chaahiye)

<!-- topic: wanting; type: phrase; level: beginner -->

The easiest way to say "I want" or "I need" a thing in Hindi is मुझे … चाहिए (mujhe … chaahiye), literally "to me … is needed". मुझे (mujhe) means "to me" and चाहिए (chaahiye) means "is wanted / needed". It never changes for the speaker's gender, so men and women say exactly the same thing. Examples: मुझे पानी चाहिए (mujhe paani chaahiye, I want water), मुझे एक टिकट चाहिए (mujhe ek tikat chaahiye, I need one ticket), मुझे मदद चाहिए (mujhe madad chaahiye, I need help). To ask someone politely: आपको क्या चाहिए? (aapko kyaa chaahiye?, what do you want?). Casual: तुम्हें क्या चाहिए? (tumhen kyaa chaahiye?).

## I want to … — मैं … चाहता हूँ (main … chaahtaa hoon)

<!-- topic: wanting; type: phrase -->

To say you want to DO something in Hindi, use the infinitive (the -ना form) with चाहना (chaahnaa, to want): मैं घर जाना चाहता हूँ (main ghar jaanaa chaahtaa hoon, I want to go home). A woman says चाहती हूँ (chaahtee hoon): मैं हिंदी सीखना चाहती हूँ (main hindee seekhnaa chaahtee hoon, I want to learn Hindi). In everyday speech people very often use मुझे … है (mujhe … hai) instead, which is the same for men and women: मुझे घर जाना है (mujhe ghar jaanaa hai, I want to / need to go home), मुझे कुछ कहना है (mujhe kuchh kahnaa hai, I want to say something).

## I have … — मेरे पास … है (mere paas … hai)

<!-- topic: to-have; type: phrase -->

Hindi has no verb "to have". For things you own or carry, say मेरे पास … है (mere paas … hai), literally "near me … is": मेरे पास पेन है (mere paas pen hai, I have a pen), मेरे पास पैसे नहीं हैं (mere paas paise nahin hain, I don't have money). For family and body parts use मेरा / मेरी / मेरे … है: मेरे दो भाई हैं (mere do bhaaee hain, I have two brothers). For illnesses and feelings use मुझे: मुझे बुख़ार है (mujhe bukhaar hai, I have a fever). All three patterns are the same for men and women. Question: क्या आपके पास … है? (kyaa aapke paas … hai?, do you have …?).

## I like … — मुझे … पसंद है (mujhe … pasand hai)

<!-- topic: liking; type: phrase; level: beginner -->

In Hindi "I like" is मुझे … पसंद है (mujhe … pasand hai), literally "to me … is liked". It is the same for men and women. Examples: मुझे चाय पसंद है (mujhe chaay pasand hai, I like tea), मुझे क्रिकेट पसंद है (mujhe kriket pasand hai, I like cricket). For several things use हैं: मुझे आम पसंद हैं (I like mangoes). For something you just tried, say मुझे यह अच्छा लगा (mujhe yah achchhaa lagaa, I liked this). For activities use the -ना form: मुझे फ़िल्में देखना पसंद है (mujhe filmen dekhnaa pasand hai, I like watching films). Negative: मुझे यह पसंद नहीं है (mujhe yah pasand nahin hai, I don't like this).

Also asked as: how do I say I like in Hindi, I like tea in Hindi, saying what you like, do you like

## I need … — मुझे … की ज़रूरत है (mujhe … kee zaroorat hai)

<!-- topic: wanting; type: phrase -->

In Hindi you can say "I need" in two ways. The everyday one is मुझे … चाहिए (mujhe … chaahiye): मुझे डॉक्टर चाहिए (mujhe doktar chaahiye, I need a doctor). A slightly stronger, more serious form is मुझे … की ज़रूरत है (mujhe … kee zaroorat hai), literally "to me there is need of …": मुझे डॉक्टर की ज़रूरत है (mujhe doktar kee zaroorat hai, I need a doctor). ज़रूरत (zaroorat, need) is feminine, so it always takes की. "I need to do something" is मुझे … है or मुझे … पड़ेगा: मुझे जाना पड़ेगा (mujhe jaanaa padegaa, I will have to go). These are the same for men and women.

Also asked as: how to say I need in Hindi, I need help, I have to go, saying necessity

## I am going to … — future plans

<!-- topic: future-tense; type: phrase -->

For "I am going to (a place)" Hindi uses the continuous: मैं बाज़ार जा रहा हूँ (main baazaar jaa rahaa hoon, I'm going to the market); a woman says जा रही हूँ (jaa rahee hoon). For "I am going to do something", use the oblique infinitive (-ने) + जा रहा हूँ: मैं खाना खाने जा रहा हूँ (main khaanaa khaane jaa rahaa hoon, I'm going to eat). For plans in general, use the future: मैं कल फ़िल्म देखूँगा (main kal film dekhoongaa, I will watch a film tomorrow); a woman says देखूँगी (dekhoongee). Hindi needs no word for "to" before a place like घर or बाज़ार.

## There is / there are — है / हैं (hai / hain)

<!-- topic: existence; type: phrase -->

Hindi says "there is" simply with है (hai) and "there are" with हैं (hain) at the end; the place usually comes first. Examples: यहाँ एक दुकान है (yahaan ek dukaan hai, there is a shop here), कमरे में दो लोग हैं (kamre mein do log hain, there are two people in the room). The question is the same with a rising voice or with क्या: क्या यहाँ बाथरूम है? (kyaa yahaan baathroom hai?, is there a bathroom here?). "There isn't" is नहीं है: यहाँ कोई नहीं है (yahaan koee nahin hai, there is nobody here). Is there any …? कोई … है? (koee … hai?).

## Can I …? — क्या मैं … सकता हूँ? (kyaa main … saktaa hoon?)

<!-- topic: modal-verbs; type: phrase -->

To ask permission in Hindi, say क्या मैं + verb stem + सकता हूँ? (kyaa main … saktaa hoon?). A woman says सकती हूँ (saktee hoon). Examples: क्या मैं अंदर आ सकता हूँ? (kyaa main andar aa saktaa hoon?, may I come in?), क्या मैं यहाँ बैठ सकती हूँ? (kyaa main yahaan baith saktee hoon?, can I sit here? — woman). A shorter everyday way is the "shall I" form: मैं यहाँ बैठ जाऊँ? (main yahaan baith jaaoon?, shall I sit here?), the same for men and women. To ask whether something is allowed: क्या यहाँ फ़ोटो ले सकते हैं? (kyaa yahaan foto le sakte hain?, can one take photos here?).

Also asked as: may I in Hindi, asking permission, can I come in, is it allowed

## Let's … — चलो, … (chalo, …)

<!-- topic: suggestions; type: phrase -->

In Hindi "let's" is usually चलो (chalo), literally "come on / let's go", followed by the plan in the -ते हैं form or the -एँ form. Examples: चलो, चाय पीते हैं (chalo, chaay peete hain, let's have tea), चलो, चलें (chalo, chalen, let's go), चलो, खाना खाते हैं (chalo, khaanaa khaate hain, let's eat). With elders or in polite speech use चलिए (chaliye): चलिए, शुरू करते हैं (chaliye, shuroo karte hain, let's begin). A suggestion as a question: फ़िल्म देखने चलें? (film dekhne chalen?, shall we go and see a film?). These forms are the same for men and women.

## X is Y — the sentence with है (hai)

<!-- topic: word-order; level: beginner -->

A Hindi "X is Y" sentence puts है (hai, is) at the very end: यह मेरा घर है (yah meraa ghar hai, this is my house), दिल्ली बड़ा शहर है (Dillee badaa shahar hai, Delhi is a big city). The verb changes with the subject: मैं … हूँ (main … hoon, I am), तुम … हो (tum … ho, you are — casual), आप … हैं (aap … hain, you are — polite), वह … है (vah … hai, he / she / it is), हम / वे … हैं (ham / ve … hain, we / they are). Unlike in some languages, है cannot be dropped in a normal statement: say मैं छात्र हूँ (main chhaatr hoon, I am a student), not just "मैं छात्र".

## Negative sentences — नहीं (nahin)

<!-- topic: negation -->

To make a Hindi sentence negative, put नहीं (nahin, not) just before the verb: मैं मांस नहीं खाता (main maans nahin khaataa, I don't eat meat); a woman says नहीं खाती (nahin khaatee). In the present tense the final हूँ / है / हैं is often dropped after नहीं, as in that example. With "to be" it stays: यह महँगा नहीं है (yah mahangaa nahin hai, this is not expensive). In commands use मत (mat): मत जाओ (mat jaao, don't go), चिंता मत कीजिए (chintaa mat keejiye, please don't worry). "Nothing" is कुछ नहीं (kuchh nahin) and "nobody" is कोई नहीं (koee nahin).

## I think … — मुझे लगता है कि … (mujhe lagtaa hai ki …)

<!-- topic: opinions; type: phrase; level: intermediate -->

To give an opinion in Hindi, say मुझे लगता है कि … (mujhe lagtaa hai ki …), literally "it seems to me that …". It is the same for men and women. Examples: मुझे लगता है कि बारिश होगी (mujhe lagtaa hai ki baarish hogee, I think it will rain), मुझे लगता है कि वह घर पर है (mujhe lagtaa hai ki vah ghar par hai, I think he / she is at home). कि (ki, that) can be left out in quick speech. To disagree gently: मुझे ऐसा नहीं लगता (mujhe aisaa nahin lagtaa, I don't think so). To ask: आपको क्या लगता है? (aapko kyaa lagtaa hai?, what do you think?).

Also asked as: how to say I think in Hindi, giving an opinion, what do you think, I don't think so

## I know / I don't know — मुझे पता है (mujhe pataa hai)

<!-- topic: knowing; type: phrase; level: beginner -->

In Hindi "I know (a fact)" is मुझे पता है (mujhe pataa hai), literally "to me it is known", and "I don't know" is मुझे नहीं पता (mujhe nahin pataa). Both are the same for men and women and are the most natural everyday forms. To know a person or a language you can also use जानना (jaannaa, to know): मैं उसे जानता हूँ (main use jaantaa hoon, I know him / her); a woman says जानती हूँ (jaantee hoon). For languages Hindi says the language "comes" to you: मुझे हिंदी थोड़ी आती है (mujhe hindee thodee aatee hai, I know a little Hindi). Question: क्या आपको पता है? (kyaa aapko pataa hai?, do you know?).

Also asked as: how to say I don't know in Hindi, I know, do you know, I know a little Hindi

## I don't like … — मुझे … पसंद नहीं है (mujhe … pasand nahin hai)

<!-- topic: liking; type: phrase; level: beginner -->

To say "I don't like …" in Hindi, put नहीं (nahin, not) before है in the liking pattern: मुझे … पसंद नहीं है (mujhe … pasand nahin hai), literally "to me … is not liked". It is the same for men and women, and the final है is often dropped in speech. Examples: मुझे यह पसंद नहीं है (mujhe yah pasand nahin hai, I don't like this), मुझे कॉफ़ी पसंद नहीं (mujhe kofee pasand nahin, I don't like coffee), मुझे तीखा खाना पसंद नहीं है (mujhe teekhaa khaanaa pasand nahin hai, I don't like spicy food), मुझे सुबह जल्दी उठना पसंद नहीं (mujhe subah jaldee uthnaa pasand nahin, I don't like getting up early). Softer: मुझे यह ज़्यादा पसंद नहीं (mujhe yah zyaadaa pasand nahin, I don't like it much).

Also asked as: how to say I don't like in Hindi, I don't like this, I hate, saying you dislike something

## I am coming from … — मैं … से आ रहा हूँ (main … se aa rahaa hoon)

<!-- topic: directions; type: phrase -->

"I am coming from …" in Hindi is मैं … से आ रहा हूँ (main … se aa rahaa hoon); a woman says आ रही हूँ (aa rahee hoon). से (se, from) comes AFTER the place. Examples: मैं घर से आ रहा हूँ (main ghar se aa rahaa hoon, I'm coming from home), मैं कॉलेज से आ रही हूँ (main kolej se aa rahee hoon, I'm coming from college — woman), मैं ऑफ़िस से आ रहा हूँ (main ofis se aa rahaa hoon, I'm coming from the office). For where you are from (your home town), use हूँ instead: मैं जयपुर से हूँ (main Jaipur se hoon, I am from Jaipur), the same for men and women. Question: आप कहाँ से आ रहे हैं? (aap kahaan se aa rahe hain?, where are you coming from?).

Also asked as: how to say I am coming from in Hindi, coming from home, where are you coming from, from in Hindi

## I don't have … — मेरे पास … नहीं है (mere paas … nahin hai)

<!-- topic: to-have; type: phrase -->

To say "I don't have …" in Hindi, add नहीं (nahin) to the "have" pattern: मेरे पास … नहीं है (mere paas … nahin hai), the same for men and women. Use हैं for plural things. Examples: मेरे पास पैसे नहीं हैं (mere paas paise nahin hain, I don't have money), मेरे पास खुले पैसे नहीं हैं (mere paas khule paise nahin hain, I don't have change), मेरे पास टिकट नहीं है (mere paas tikat nahin hai, I don't have a ticket). For time and illnesses use मुझे or मेरे पास: मेरे पास टाइम नहीं है (mere paas taaim nahin hai, I don't have time). For family: मेरा कोई भाई नहीं है (meraa koee bhaaee nahin hai, I don't have a brother).

Also asked as: how to say I don't have in Hindi, I have no money, I don't have time, no change

## Where is …? — … कहाँ है? (… kahaan hai?)

<!-- topic: directions; type: phrase; level: beginner -->

To ask "Where is …?" in Hindi, say the place or thing first, then कहाँ है? (kahaan hai?). Start politely with सुनिए (suniye, excuse me). Examples: बाथरूम कहाँ है? (baathroom kahaan hai?, where is the bathroom?), स्टेशन कहाँ है? (steshan kahaan hai?, where is the station?), मेरा फ़ोन कहाँ है? (meraa fon kahaan hai?, where is my phone?), आप कहाँ हैं? (aap kahaan hain?, where are you? — हैं for a person you respect). For several things use हैं: मेरी चाबियाँ कहाँ हैं? (meree chaabiyaan kahaan hain?, where are my keys?). In speech किधर (kidhar, which way) also works: टॉयलेट किधर है? The question is the same for men and women.

Also asked as: how to say where is in Hindi, where is the station, asking where something is, where are my keys

## What is …? — … क्या है? (… kyaa hai?)

<!-- topic: questions; type: phrase; level: beginner -->

To ask "What is …?" in Hindi, name the thing first and put क्या है? (kyaa hai?) at the end. Examples: यह क्या है? (yah kyaa hai?, what is this?), आपका नाम क्या है? (aapkaa naam kyaa hai?, what is your name?), इसका मतलब क्या है? (iskaa matlab kyaa hai?, what does this mean?), आपका फ़ोन नंबर क्या है? (aapkaa fon nambar kyaa hai?, what is your phone number?). For "what is X called in Hindi": इसे हिंदी में क्या कहते हैं? (ise hindee mein kyaa kahte hain?). Remember that क्या at the START of a sentence makes a yes / no question instead (क्या यह पानी है?, is this water?).

Also asked as: how to say what is in Hindi, what is this, what does it mean, what is it called

## Can you …? — क्या आप … सकते हैं? (kyaa aap … sakte hain?)

<!-- topic: requests; type: phrase -->

To ask "Can you …?" in Hindi, say क्या आप + verb stem + सकते हैं? (kyaa aap … sakte hain?). To a woman, use सकती हैं (saktee hain). Examples: क्या आप मेरी मदद कर सकते हैं? (kyaa aap meree madad kar sakte hain?, can you help me?), क्या आप धीरे बोल सकते हैं? (kyaa aap dheere bol sakte hain?, can you speak slowly?), क्या आप यह लिख सकती हैं? (kyaa aap yah likh saktee hain?, can you write this? — to a woman), क्या आप मेरी फ़ोटो खींच सकते हैं? (kyaa aap meree foto kheench sakte hain?, can you take my photo?). To a friend: तुम … सकते हो? (tum … sakte ho?) or the simple future: मेरी मदद करोगे? (meree madad karoge?, will you help me?).

Also asked as: how to say can you in Hindi, could you help, can you speak slowly, asking someone to do something

## Please … — … दीजिए / … -इए (polite requests)

<!-- topic: requests; type: phrase; level: beginner -->

To say "Please (do something)" in Hindi, use the polite verb ending -इए (-iye); it already means "please", so कृपया (kripyaa) is optional and formal. Examples: पानी दीजिए (paani deejiye, water, please), बैठिए (baithiye, please sit), थोड़ा धीरे बोलिए (thodaa dheere boliye, please speak a little slowly), यहाँ रुकिए (yahaan rukiye, please stop / wait here). Irregular polite forms: दीजिए (give), लीजिए (take), कीजिए (do). ज़रा (zaraa, just) softens a request: ज़रा सुनिए (zaraa suniye, excuse me). With friends use the -ओ form: बैठो (baitho), बोलो (bolo). On signs: कृपया जूते बाहर उतारें (kripyaa joote baahar utaaren, please remove shoes outside).

Also asked as: how to say please in Hindi, polite requests, please sit, please give me

## Do you have …? — क्या आपके पास … है? (kyaa aapke paas … hai?)

<!-- topic: to-have; type: phrase -->

To ask "Do you have …?" in Hindi, say क्या आपके पास … है? (kyaa aapke paas … hai?), literally "near you is there …?"; use हैं for plural things. It is the same whoever you ask. Examples: क्या आपके पास पेन है? (kyaa aapke paas pen hai?, do you have a pen?), क्या आपके पास खुले पैसे हैं? (kyaa aapke paas khule paise hain?, do you have change?), क्या आपके पास आम हैं? (kyaa aapke paas aam hain?, do you have mangoes?). In shops people just name the item: भैया, आम हैं? (bhaiyaa, aam hain?). Casual: तुम्हारे पास चार्जर है? (tumhaare paas chaarjar hai?, do you have a charger?). For time: आपके पास टाइम है? (aapke paas taaim hai?).

Also asked as: how to ask do you have in Hindi, do you have change, do you have a pen, is it available

## How much is …? — … कितने का है? (… kitne kaa hai?)

<!-- topic: shopping; type: phrase; level: beginner -->

To ask "How much is …?" in Hindi, name the item and add कितने का है? (kitne kaa hai?). Examples: यह कितने का है? (yah kitne kaa hai?, how much is this?), यह शर्ट कितने की है? (yah shart kitne kee hai?, how much is this shirt? — शर्ट is feminine), टिकट कितने का है? (tikat kitne kaa hai?, how much is the ticket?), आम कितने रुपये किलो हैं? (aam kitne rupaye kilo hain?, how much are mangoes a kilo?). For the total: कितना हुआ? (kitnaa huaa?, how much altogether?). Other ways: इसका दाम क्या है? (iskaa daam kyaa hai?, what is its price?). These are the same for men and women.

Also asked as: how to say how much is it in Hindi, how much does this cost, price of, how much altogether
