# Common Hindi idioms and sayings

## नाच न जाने आँगन टेढ़ा (naach na jaane aangan tedhaa)

Literal meaning: "(someone) does not know how to dance, (and says) the courtyard is crooked". Hindi speakers use this saying when someone blames the circumstances or the tools for their own lack of skill, much like the English "a bad workman blames his tools". Example: he lost the match and now says the bat was bad — नाच न जाने आँगन टेढ़ा!

## अधजल गगरी छलकत जाय (adhjal gagree chhalkat jaay)

Literal meaning: "a half-filled water pot keeps splashing". This Hindi saying means that people with only a little knowledge or wealth show off the most, while those who truly have it stay quiet. It is close to the English "empty vessels make the most noise". The verb forms छलकत and जाय are old-fashioned, as is common in proverbs.

## दाल में कुछ काला है (daal mein kuchh kaalaa hai)

Literal meaning: "there is something black in the lentils". This Hindi expression means that something is suspicious or not quite right, like the English "something fishy is going on". Example: they stopped talking when I came in — दाल में कुछ काला है (daal mein kuchh kaalaa hai).

## ऊँट के मुँह में जीरा (oont ke munh mein jeeraa)

Literal meaning: "a cumin seed in a camel's mouth". This Hindi saying describes something far too small for what is needed, like the English "a drop in the ocean". Example: for such a big family, one kilo of rice is ऊँट के मुँह में जीरा.
