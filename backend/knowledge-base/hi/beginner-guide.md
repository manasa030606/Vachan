# Getting started with Hindi

## How to start learning Hindi

A good order for a beginner in Hindi: first learn a few vowels and consonants of the Devanagari script (अ आ इ ई उ ऊ, then क म न प र ल) and how the vowel signs (मात्रा, maatraa) attach to consonants; then learn greetings such as नमस्ते (namaste) and धन्यवाद (dhanyavaad); then simple sentences like मेरा नाम … है (meraa naam … hai, my name is …) and मुझे पानी चाहिए (mujhe paani chaahiye, I want water). A few minutes every day works better than one long session. Reading the letters aloud helps you remember both the shape and the sound.

## Reading the romanization

<!-- topic: romanization -->

Vachan writes Hindi in English letters (romanization) so beginners can read it before they know the script. Doubled vowels mean a long sound: aa as in "father", ee as in "see", oo as in "moon". Nasal vowels are usually shown with n: हाँ (haan), मैं (main), हूँ (hoon). The silent "a" is left out, as it is in speech: कमरा is "kamraa". "ph" and "bh" are aspirated p and b, not English f; f is फ़. Romanization does not separate dental and retroflex t and d, so always check the Hindi letter too: the script is the real spelling.

## Formal and informal "you"

<!-- topic: politeness; skill: conversation -->

The most important beginner rule in Hindi conversation: use आप (aap) for "you" with anyone older, a stranger, a teacher or a shopkeeper; use तुम (tum) with friends and younger people; keep तू (too) for very close relationships only. Verbs change with it: आप कैसे हैं? (aap kaise hain?) is polite, तुम कैसे हो? (tum kaise ho?) is informal. Polite requests end in -इए: बैठिए (baithiye, please sit). When unsure, choose आप.

## Learn every noun with its gender

<!-- topic: gender; skill: grammar -->

Every Hindi noun is masculine or feminine, and the gender changes the adjectives, possessives and verbs around it, so learn it together with the word. A simple trick is to learn a noun inside a short phrase: मेरा घर (meraa ghar, my house) shows that घर is masculine, and मेरी किताब (meree kitaab, my book) shows that किताब is feminine. Also remember that your own gender changes your verbs: a man says मैं जाता हूँ (main jaataa hoon) and a woman says मैं जाती हूँ (main jaatee hoon), both meaning "I go".
