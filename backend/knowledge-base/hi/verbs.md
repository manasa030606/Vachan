# Hindi verb forms

## To be — होना (honaa)

<!-- topic: to-be -->

The Hindi verb होना (honaa) means "to be". Present forms: मैं हूँ (main hoon, I am), तू है (too hai, you are — intimate), तुम हो (tum ho, you are — informal), आप हैं (aap hain, you are — respectful), यह / वह है (yah / vah hai, he / she / it is), हम हैं (ham hain, we are), ये / वे हैं (ye / ve hain, they are). Example: मैं ठीक हूँ (main theek hoon, I am fine). The past forms agree with gender: था (thaa, masculine), थी (thee, feminine), थे (the, masculine plural), थीं (theen, feminine plural): वह घर पर था (vah ghar par thaa, he was at home).

## Present tense — खाना (khaanaa, to eat)

Hindi verbs are listed with the ending -ना (-naa); remove it to get the stem: खाना → खा (khaa). The simple present (habitual) is stem + -ता / -ती / -ते + a form of होना. For खाना: मैं खाता हूँ / खाती हूँ (main khaataa hoon / khaatee hoon, I eat — man / woman), तुम खाते हो / खाती हो (tum khaate ho / khaatee ho), आप खाते हैं / खाती हैं (aap khaate hain / khaatee hain), वह खाता है / खाती है (vah khaataa hai / khaatee hai, he / she eats), हम खाते हैं (ham khaate hain, we eat), वे खाते हैं (ve khaate hain, they eat).

## Present continuous — "I am eating"

<!-- topic: continuous -->

To say an action is happening right now, Hindi uses the stem + रहा / रही / रहे + a form of होना. रहा (rahaa) is masculine singular, रही (rahee) feminine, रहे (rahe) masculine plural or respectful. Examples: मैं खा रहा हूँ (main khaa rahaa hoon, I am eating — man), मैं खा रही हूँ (main khaa rahee hoon, I am eating — woman), वह पढ़ रही है (vah padh rahee hai, she is reading), वे आ रहे हैं (ve aa rahe hain, they are coming).

## Future tense — जाना (jaanaa, to go)

<!-- topic: future-tense -->

The Hindi future adds endings to the verb stem that show person and gender, ending in -गा (-gaa, masculine), -गी (-gee, feminine) or -गे (-ge, plural / respectful). For जाना (to go): मैं जाऊँगा / जाऊँगी (main jaaoongaa / jaaoongee, I will go — man / woman), तुम जाओगे / जाओगी (tum jaaoge / jaaogee), आप जाएँगे (aap jaaenge), वह जाएगा / जाएगी (vah jaaegaa / jaaegee, he / she will go), हम जाएँगे (ham jaaenge, we will go), वे जाएँगे (ve jaaenge, they will go).

## Past tense — जाना (jaanaa, to go)

<!-- topic: past-tense; level: intermediate -->

The Hindi simple past agrees with gender and number. जाना (to go) has an irregular past stem: मैं गया (main gayaa, I went — man), मैं गई (main gaee, I went — woman), वह गया / गई (vah gayaa / gaee, he / she went), हम गए (ham gae, we went), वे गए (ve gae, they went), आप गए (aap gae, you went — respectful). Regular verbs add -आ (-aa): आना → मैं आया / आई (main aayaa / aaee, I came). Negative: मैं नहीं गया (main nahin gayaa, I did not go).

## Past tense with ने (ne) — the ergative

<!-- topic: past-tense; level: intermediate -->

In Hindi, when a verb takes an object (eat, see, drink, write), the simple past works differently: the subject gets ने (ne) and the verb agrees with the object, not the subject. राम ने खाना खाया (Ram ne khaanaa khaayaa, Ram ate food — खाना is masculine). राम ने रोटी खाई (Ram ne rotee khaaee, Ram ate a roti — रोटी is feminine). Pronouns join with ने: मैंने (mainne), तुमने (tumne), आपने (aapne), उसने (usne), हमने (hamne), उन्होंने (unhonne). If the object has को, the verb stays masculine singular: मैंने माँ को देखा (mainne maa ko dekhaa, I saw mother). Verbs without an object, like जाना and आना, never take ने.

## Polite requests and commands

<!-- topic: imperative -->

Hindi commands have three levels that match the three words for "you". With तू: आ (aa, come), बैठ (baith, sit). With तुम: आओ (aao, come), बैठो (baitho, sit), खाओ (khaao, eat). With आप (polite, add -इए / -iye): आइए (aaiye, please come), बैठिए (baithiye, please sit), खाइए (khaaiye, please eat), बताइए (bataaiye, please tell). Some polite forms are irregular: कीजिए (keejiye, please do), लीजिए (leejiye, please take), दीजिए (deejiye, please give). Always use the -इए form with elders, strangers and teachers.

## Can — सकना (saknaa)

<!-- topic: modal-verbs -->

To say "can" in Hindi, put the stem of the main verb before सकना (saknaa, to be able), which then takes the normal tense endings. मैं हिंदी बोल सकता हूँ (main hindee bol saktaa hoon, I can speak Hindi — man), मैं हिंदी बोल सकती हूँ (woman). Question: क्या आप मेरी मदद कर सकते हैं? (kyaa aap meree madad kar sakte hain?, can you help me?). Negative: मैं नहीं आ सकता (main nahin aa saktaa, I can't come).

## Was and were — था, थी, थे (thaa, thee, the)

<!-- topic: past-tense -->

The past of "to be" in Hindi agrees with gender and number, not with person: था (thaa, masculine singular), थी (thee, feminine singular), थे (the, masculine plural or respectful), थीं (theen, feminine plural or respectful). Examples: मैं घर पर था (main ghar par thaa, I was at home — man), मैं घर पर थी (main ghar par thee, I was at home — woman), वह बीमार थी (vah beemaar thee, she was ill), हम कल वहाँ थे (ham kal vahaan the, we were there yesterday), आप कहाँ थे? (aap kahaan the?, where were you?). Adding था to a verb puts it further in the past: मैं गया था (main gayaa thaa, I had gone / I went).

## Common verb in all tenses — करना (karnaa, to do)

<!-- topic: verb-forms -->

करना (karnaa, to do) is the most useful Hindi verb, because it builds many others: काम करना (kaam karnaa, to work), फ़ोन करना (fon karnaa, to call), मदद करना (madad karnaa, to help). Its forms for "I" (man / woman): present मैं करता / करती हूँ (main kartaa / kartee hoon, I do); continuous मैं कर रहा / रही हूँ (main kar rahaa / rahee hoon, I am doing); past with ने मैंने किया (mainne kiyaa, I did — same for both); future मैं करूँगा / करूँगी (main karoongaa / karoongee, I will do). Commands: करो (karo, casual), कीजिए (keejiye, polite), कर (kar, intimate). Example: मैं कल फ़ोन करूँगा (main kal fon karoongaa, I'll call tomorrow).

## Common verb in all tenses — आना (aanaa, to come)

<!-- topic: verb-forms -->

आना (aanaa, to come) in Hindi, for "I" (man / woman): present मैं आता / आती हूँ (main aataa / aatee hoon, I come); continuous मैं आ रहा / रही हूँ (main aa rahaa / rahee hoon, I'm coming); past मैं आया / आई (main aayaa / aaee, I came) — no ने, because आना has no object; future मैं आऊँगा / आऊँगी (main aaoongaa / aaoongee, I will come). Commands: आओ (aao, come — casual), आइए (aaiye, please come), आ (aa, intimate). Useful: अंदर आइए (andar aaiye, please come in), मैं अभी आया (main abhee aayaa, I'll be right back — literally "I came just now"; a woman: अभी आई).

## Want to — चाहना (chaahnaa)

<!-- topic: modal-verbs -->

To say "want to do" in Hindi, use the infinitive (-ना) + चाहना (chaahnaa, to want) in the present: मैं हिंदी सीखना चाहता हूँ (main hindee seekhnaa chaahtaa hoon, I want to learn Hindi); a woman says चाहती हूँ (chaahtee hoon). Question: आप क्या खाना चाहते हैं? (aap kyaa khaanaa chaahte hain?, what do you want to eat?). For things (not actions) use मुझे … चाहिए (mujhe … chaahiye) instead. In everyday speech मुझे … है is often used for wanting / needing to do something: मुझे बाज़ार जाना है (mujhe baazaar jaanaa hai, I want to / have to go to the market), the same for men and women.

## Have to — पड़ना (padnaa)

<!-- topic: modal-verbs; level: intermediate -->

"Have to / must" in Hindi uses मुझे + infinitive + a form of पड़ना (padnaa): मुझे जाना पड़ेगा (mujhe jaanaa padegaa, I will have to go), मुझे रोज़ काम करना पड़ता है (mujhe roz kaam karnaa padtaa hai, I have to work every day), मुझे इंतज़ार करना पड़ा (mujhe intazaar karnaa padaa, I had to wait). The person takes मुझे, so it is the same for men and women. A lighter "need to / am supposed to" is मुझे … है: मुझे पढ़ना है (mujhe padhnaa hai, I have to study). चाहिए after an infinitive means "should": आपको आराम करना चाहिए (aapko aaraam karnaa chaahiye, you should rest).

## Finished actions — गया, लिया, दिया (compound verbs)

<!-- topic: compound-verbs; level: intermediate -->

Hindi often adds a second verb to show that an action is complete. जाना (to go) after a stem means "got / became": मैं थक गया (main thak gayaa, I got tired), मैं समझ गई (main samajh gaee, I understood — woman), बस आ गई (bas aa gaee, the bus has come). लेना (to take) shows something done for yourself: मैंने खा लिया (mainne khaa liyaa, I've eaten). देना (to give) shows something done for someone else or a polite request: बता दीजिए (bataa deejiye, please tell me), दे दीजिए (de deejiye, please give it). These forms sound very natural in conversation; the meaning comes from the first verb.
