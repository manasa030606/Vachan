# Hindi verb forms

## To be — होना (honaa)

<!-- topic: to-be -->

The Hindi verb होना (honaa) means "to be". Present forms: मैं हूँ (main hoon, I am), तू है (too hai, you are — intimate), तुम हो (tum ho, you are — informal), आप हैं (aap hain, you are — respectful), यह / वह है (yah / vah hai, he / she / it is), हम हैं (ham hain, we are), ये / वे हैं (ye / ve hain, they are). Example: मैं ठीक हूँ (main theek hoon, I am fine). The past forms agree with gender: था (thaa, masculine), थी (thee, feminine), थे (the, masculine plural), थीं (theen, feminine plural): वह घर पर था (vah ghar par thaa, he was at home).

## Present tense — खाना (khaanaa, to eat)

Hindi verbs are listed with the ending -ना (-naa); remove it to get the stem: खाना → खा (khaa). The simple present (habitual) is stem + -ता / -ती / -ते + a form of होना. For खाना: मैं खाता हूँ / खाती हूँ (main khaataa hoon / khaatee hoon, I eat — man / woman), तुम खाते हो / खाती हो (tum khaate ho / khaatee ho), आप खाते हैं / खाती हैं (aap khaate hain / khaatee hain), वह खाता है / खाती है (vah khaataa hai / khaatee hai, he / she eats), हम खाते हैं (ham khaate hain, we eat), वे खाते हैं (ve khaate hain, they eat).

## Present continuous — "I am eating"

<!-- topic: continuous -->

To say an action is happening right now, Hindi uses the stem + रहा / रही / रहे + a form of होना. रहा (rahaa) is masculine singular, रही (rahee) feminine, रहे (rahe) masculine plural or respectful. Examples: मैं खा रहा हूँ (main khaa rahaa hoon, I am eating — man), मैं खा रही हूँ (main khaa rahee hoon, I am eating — woman), वह पढ़ रही है (vah padh rahee hai, she is reading), वे आ रहे हैं (ve aa rahe hain, they are coming).

## Future tense — जाना (jaanaa, to go)

<!-- topic: future-tense -->

The Hindi future adds endings to the verb stem that show person and gender, ending in -गा (-gaa, masculine), -गी (-gee, feminine) or -गे (-ge, plural / respectful). For जाना (to go): मैं जाऊँगा / जाऊँगी (main jaaoongaa / jaaoongee, I will go — man / woman), तुम जाओगे / जाओगी (tum jaaoge / jaaogee), आप जाएँगे (aap jaaenge), वह जाएगा / जाएगी (vah jaaegaa / jaaegee, he / she will go), हम जाएँगे (ham jaaenge, we will go), वे जाएँगे (ve jaaenge, they will go).

## Past tense — जाना (jaanaa, to go)

<!-- topic: past-tense; level: intermediate -->

The Hindi simple past agrees with gender and number. जाना (to go) has an irregular past stem: मैं गया (main gayaa, I went — man), मैं गई (main gayee, I went — woman), वह गया / गई (vah gayaa / gayee, he / she went), हम गए (ham gaye, we went), वे गए (ve gaye, they went), आप गए (aap gaye, you went — respectful). Regular verbs add -आ (-aa): आना → मैं आया / आई (main aayaa / aayee, I came). Negative: मैं नहीं गया (main nahin gayaa, I did not go).

## Past tense with ने (ne) — the ergative

<!-- topic: past-tense; level: intermediate -->

In Hindi, when a verb takes an object (eat, see, drink, write), the simple past works differently: the subject gets ने (ne) and the verb agrees with the object, not the subject. राम ने खाना खाया (Ram ne khaanaa khaayaa, Ram ate food — खाना is masculine). राम ने रोटी खाई (Ram ne rotee khaayee, Ram ate a roti — रोटी is feminine). Pronouns join with ने: मैंने (mainne), तुमने (tumne), आपने (aapne), उसने (usne), हमने (hamne), उन्होंने (unhonne). If the object has को, the verb stays masculine singular: मैंने माँ को देखा (mainne maa ko dekhaa, I saw mother). Verbs without an object, like जाना and आना, never take ने.

## Polite requests and commands

<!-- topic: imperative -->

Hindi commands have three levels that match the three words for "you". With तू: आ (aa, come), बैठ (baith, sit). With तुम: आओ (aao, come), बैठो (baitho, sit), खाओ (khaao, eat). With आप (polite, add -इए / -iye): आइए (aaiye, please come), बैठिए (baithiye, please sit), खाइए (khaaiye, please eat), बताइए (bataaiye, please tell). Some polite forms are irregular: कीजिए (keejiye, please do), लीजिए (leejiye, please take), दीजिए (deejiye, please give). Always use the -इए form with elders, strangers and teachers.

## Can — सकना (saknaa)

<!-- topic: modal-verbs -->

To say "can" in Hindi, put the stem of the main verb before सकना (saknaa, to be able), which then takes the normal tense endings. मैं हिंदी बोल सकता हूँ (main Hindi bol saktaa hoon, I can speak Hindi — man), मैं हिंदी बोल सकती हूँ (woman). Question: क्या आप मेरी मदद कर सकते हैं? (kyaa aap meree madad kar sakte hain?, can you help me?). Negative: मैं नहीं आ सकता (main nahin aa saktaa, I can't come).
