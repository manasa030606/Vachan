# Hindi pronunciation for beginners

## Short and long vowels change the meaning

In Hindi, vowel length matters: a long vowel is held longer than a short one, and swapping them can change the word. कम (kam) means "less", but काम (kaam) means "work". In romanization Vachan writes long vowels doubled: aa, ee, oo, as in नाम (naam, name), तीन (teen, three) and दूध (doodh, milk). Short अ (a) is a light sound like the "u" in English "but", while आ (aa) is like the "a" in "father".

## Dental and retroflex sounds

<!-- topic: retroflex -->

Hindi has two sets of t and d sounds. Dental sounds त (ta), थ (tha), द (da), ध (dha) are made with the tongue touching the back of the upper teeth, as in तीन (teen, three) and दो (do, two). Retroflex sounds ट (ta), ठ (tha), ड (da), ढ (dha) are made with the tip of the tongue curled back to the roof of the mouth, as in ठीक (theek, fine). English "t" and "d" sit between the two. Hindi also has the flapped sounds ड़ and ढ़, made by flicking the curled tongue forward, as in लड़का (ladkaa, boy) and पढ़ना (padhnaa, to read / study).

## Aspirated sounds

<!-- topic: aspiration -->

Hindi letters such as ख (kha), घ (gha), थ (tha), ध (dha), फ (pha) and भ (bha) are aspirated: they are said with a clear puff of air. The plain letters क (ka) and प (pa) have no puff, like the k in "skin". Aspiration changes meaning: पल (pal) is "moment", but फल (phal) is "fruit". Note that in Hindi "ph" means an aspirated p, not English "f". Common words with aspirated sounds: खाना (khaanaa, food), धन्यवाद (dhanyavaad, thank you), भाई (bhaai, brother), दूध (doodh, milk).

## Silent "a" — schwa deletion

<!-- topic: schwa-deletion; level: elementary -->

Every Hindi consonant letter carries a short "a", but in speech this "a" is often dropped. At the end of a word it is almost always silent: नाम is "naam", not "naama"; दोस्त is "dost"; धन्यवाद is "dhanyavaad". It is also often dropped in the middle of a word: कमरा (room) is said "kamraa", not "kamaraa", and समझना (to understand) is "samajhnaa". Vachan's romanization shows the spoken form, so follow the romanization when you are unsure.

## Nukta sounds — ज़ and फ़

<!-- topic: nukta; level: elementary -->

A dot written under a Hindi letter is called a नुक़्ता (nuqtaa). It marks sounds that came into Hindi through Persian, Arabic and English words. ज़ is "z", as in ज़रूर (zaroor, certainly), and फ़ is "f", as in फ़िल्म (film). Without the dot, ज is "j" and फ is an aspirated "ph". Other nukta letters are क़, ख़ and ग़. In everyday writing the dot is often left out, and many speakers say ज़ as ज; both are understood.

## Nasal vowels — anusvara and chandrabindu

<!-- topic: nasal-vowels -->

Hindi has nasal vowels, where air passes through the nose. They are shown with the chandrabindu ँ (a moon with a dot) or the anusvara ं (a dot). हाँ (haan, yes) and माँ (maa, mother) use the chandrabindu. When a vowel sign already sits above the head line, only the dot is written: मैं (main, I), हैं (hain, are), नहीं (nahin, no). The dot also stands for a nasal consonant before another consonant: हिंदी (Hindi). Vachan's romanization usually shows nasal sounds with n (haan, main, hoon); माँ is simply written maa.
