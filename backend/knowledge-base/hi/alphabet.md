# The Hindi script (Devanagari)

## How Hindi writing works

Hindi is written in the Devanagari script (देवनागरी, devanaagaree), which is also used for Sanskrit, Marathi and Nepali. Devanagari is an abugida: every consonant letter already carries a short "a" sound, so क is read "ka", not just "k". To write another vowel after a consonant you add a vowel sign (मात्रा, maatraa) to it. Hindi is written from left to right, and the letters of a word hang from a horizontal line drawn across the top. Hindi spelling is quite regular: a word is mostly read the way it is written.

## Vowels — स्वर (svar)

<!-- topic: vowels -->

Hindi vowels are called स्वर (svar). The vowel letters are: अ (a), आ (aa), इ (i), ई (ee), उ (u), ऊ (oo), ऋ (ri), ए (e), ऐ (ai), ओ (o), औ (au). Two signs are added to the list: अं (an / am, the anusvara, a nasal sound) and अः (ah, the visarga, found mostly in Sanskrit words). Short and long vowels are different letters: इ and ई, उ and ऊ. Unlike Telugu, Hindi has no separate short e and short o. Beginners usually start with अ आ इ ई उ ऊ.

## Consonants — व्यंजन (vyanjan)

<!-- topic: consonants -->

Hindi consonants are called व्यंजन (vyanjan). They are arranged by where the sound is made in the mouth: क ख ग घ ङ (throat), च छ ज झ ञ (palate), ट ठ ड ढ ण (retroflex, tongue curled back), त थ द ध न (dental, tongue at the teeth), प फ ब भ म (lips), then य र ल व श ष स ह. Each of the first five rows goes plain, aspirated, voiced, voiced-aspirated, nasal. Hindi also uses ड़ (ra) and ढ़ (rha), two flapped sounds, and the combined letters क्ष (ksha), त्र (tra) and ज्ञ (gya).

## Vowel signs — मात्रा (maatraa)

<!-- topic: vowel-signs -->

When a vowel follows a consonant, Hindi writes a vowel sign called मात्रा (maatraa) instead of the full vowel letter. With क: क (ka), का (kaa), कि (ki), की (kee), कु (ku), कू (koo), कृ (kri), के (ke), कै (kai), को (ko), कौ (kau), कं (kan). Note that the sign for short i (ि) is written before the consonant but pronounced after it: कि is "ki". The signs for u and oo hang below the letter, while e and ai sit on top. Learning this table, called बारहखड़ी (baarahkhadee), for each consonant is the usual way Hindi learners practise reading.

## The head line — शिरोरेखा (shirorekhaa)

<!-- topic: script-overview -->

The horizontal line across the top of Hindi letters is called शिरोरेखा (shirorekhaa), "head line". In a word, the head lines of all the letters join into one continuous line: नमस्ते (namaste), धन्यवाद (dhanyavaad). When writing by hand, many people write the letters first and draw the line over the whole word at the end. In a few letters, such as ध (dha) and भ (bha), the line is broken; this gap is what tells ध apart from घ (gha) and भ apart from म (ma).

## Conjunct letters and the halant

<!-- topic: conjuncts; level: elementary -->

When two consonants come together with no vowel between them, Hindi joins them into a conjunct letter (संयुक्त अक्षर, sanyukt akshar). Usually the first consonant loses its vertical stroke and attaches to the next: स + त = स्त in नमस्ते (namaste), न + य = न्य in धन्यवाद (dhanyavaad), क + य = क्य in क्या (kyaa). र changes shape: before a consonant it becomes a small hook on top (धर्म, dharm), after one it becomes a slanted stroke (प्रेम, prem). A consonant with no vowel at all is shown with the हलंत (halant) mark ्, as in क् (k).
