# Making requests in Hindi

## Please — how politeness works in Hindi

<!-- type: explanation -->

Hindi does have a word for "please", कृपया (kripyaa), but it is formal and used mostly on signs, in announcements and in careful requests: कृपया बैठिए (kripyaa baithiye, please be seated). In everyday speech politeness comes from the verb ending -इए (-iye), which already means "please": बैठिए (baithiye, please sit), बताइए (bataaiye, please tell), दीजिए (deejiye, please give). Adding ज़रा (zaraa, just / a little) makes it softer still: ज़रा सुनिए (zaraa suniye, excuse me, listen a moment). With friends people use the तुम form (बैठो, baitho) and often English "please" (प्लीज़, pleez).

## Excuse me — सुनिए (suniye)

To get a stranger's attention in Hindi say सुनिए (suniye), literally "please listen", or ज़रा सुनिए (zaraa suniye). Adding भैया (bhaiyaa, brother) or दीदी (deedee, sister) is friendly and normal: भैया, सुनिए (bhaiyaa, suniye). To squeeze past people, say ज़रा रास्ता दीजिए (zaraa raastaa deejiye, please make way a little) or just "excuse me" in English. To apologise for a small bump: माफ़ कीजिए (maaf keejiye, sorry / excuse me) or "sorry". These are the same for men and women.

## Can you help me? — क्या आप मेरी मदद कर सकते हैं? (kyaa aap meree madad kar sakte hain?)

To ask for help politely in Hindi say क्या आप मेरी मदद कर सकते हैं? (kyaa aap meree madad kar sakte hain?, can you help me?). Speaking to a woman, use सकती (saktee): क्या आप मेरी मदद कर सकती हैं? Note that Hindi says "my help" (मेरी मदद, meree madad). Shorter and still polite: मेरी मदद कीजिए (meree madad keejiye, please help me). To a friend: मेरी मदद करोगे? (meree madad karoge?, will you help me?). "I need help" is मुझे मदद चाहिए (mujhe madad chaahiye). In an emergency people shout बचाओ! (bachaao!, help! / save me!).

## Give me … please — … दीजिए (… deejiye)

To ask for something in Hindi, name the thing and add दीजिए (deejiye, please give): एक चाय दीजिए (ek chaay deejiye, one tea, please), थोड़ा पानी दीजिए (thodaa paani deejiye, some water, please), यह वाला दीजिए (yah vaalaa deejiye, this one, please). दे दीजिए (de deejiye) sounds a little warmer and more natural in shops: एक थैली दे दीजिए (ek thailee de deejiye, please give me a bag). With friends: दो (do) or देना (denaa): पानी देना (paani denaa, pass the water). Asking as a question is very polite: थोड़ा पानी मिलेगा? (thodaa paani milegaa?, could I get some water?).

## Wait please — रुकिए (rukiye)

"Please wait" in Hindi is रुकिए (rukiye) or the softer ज़रा रुकिए (zaraa rukiye, wait a moment). Very common: एक मिनट (ek minat, one minute) or बस एक मिनट (bas ek minat, just a minute). For a longer wait: थोड़ा इंतज़ार कीजिए (thodaa intazaar keejiye, please wait a while). Casual: रुको (ruko), with तू: रुक (ruk). To ask someone to wait for you: मेरा इंतज़ार कीजिए (meraa intazaar keejiye, please wait for me). These forms are the same for men and women.

## Say it again, please — फिर से बोलिए (phir se boliye)

<!-- topic: understanding -->

If you didn't catch something in Hindi, say फिर से बोलिए (phir se boliye, please say it again) or एक बार फिर से बोलिए (ek baar phir se boliye, please say it once more). A quick polite "pardon?" is जी? (jee?) or क्या कहा? (kyaa kahaa?, what did you say?). Casual: फिर से बोलो (phir se bolo). Very useful together with मुझे समझ नहीं आया (mujhe samajh nahin aayaa, I didn't understand), which is the same for men and women. You can also ask someone to write it: लिखकर दिखाइए (likhkar dikhaaiye, please write it down and show me).

## Please speak slowly — थोड़ा धीरे बोलिए (thodaa dheere boliye)

<!-- topic: understanding -->

To ask someone to slow down in Hindi, say थोड़ा धीरे बोलिए (thodaa dheere boliye, please speak a little slowly). Explaining helps: मैं हिंदी सीख रहा हूँ (main hindee seekh rahaa hoon, I am learning Hindi); a woman says सीख रही हूँ (seekh rahee hoon). Casual: थोड़ा धीरे बोलो (thodaa dheere bolo). Other useful lines: मुझे समझ नहीं आया (mujhe samajh nahin aayaa, I don't understand), इसका मतलब क्या है? (iskaa matlab kyaa hai?, what does this mean?), क्या आप अंग्रेज़ी बोलते हैं? (kyaa aap angrezee bolte hain?, do you speak English?).

## Polite, casual and intimate commands

<!-- type: grammar; skill: grammar; topic: imperative -->

Every Hindi command has three forms that match the three words for "you". With आप (aap, polite) add -इए: आइए (aaiye, please come), बैठिए (baithiye, please sit), बोलिए (boliye, please say). With तुम (tum, casual) add -ओ: आओ (aao), बैठो (baitho), बोलो (bolo). With तू (too, very close or to a child) use the bare stem: आ (aa), बैठ (baith), बोल (bol). A few polite forms are irregular: कीजिए (keejiye, do), दीजिए (deejiye, give), लीजिए (leejiye, take), पीजिए (peejiye, drink). Use the -इए form with strangers, elders, shopkeepers and anyone you are unsure about.

## Don't … please — मत / न (mat / na)

<!-- type: grammar; topic: negation -->

To tell someone not to do something in Hindi, put मत (mat) before the verb: मत जाओ (mat jaao, don't go), चिंता मत कीजिए (chintaa mat keejiye, please don't worry), यहाँ फ़ोटो मत लीजिए (yahaan foto mat leejiye, please don't take photos here). A softer, more polite "don't" uses न (na): आप न जाइए (aap na jaaiye, please don't go). On signs you will see कृपया … न करें (kripyaa … na karen): कृपया धूम्रपान न करें (kripyaa dhoomrapaan na karen, please do not smoke). नहीं is not used in commands.

## Could you …? — making a request softer

To make a Hindi request extra polite, ask it as a question about the future: क्या आप दरवाज़ा खोलेंगे? (kyaa aap darvaazaa kholenge?, would you open the door?) — to a woman खोलेंगी (kholengee). Or use सकना (can): क्या आप थोड़ा धीरे चला सकते हैं? (kyaa aap thodaa dheere chalaa sakte hain?, could you drive a little slower?). Adding ज़रा (zaraa) and दीजिए also softens: ज़रा खिड़की बंद कर दीजिए (zaraa khidkee band kar deejiye, could you please close the window). End with धन्यवाद (dhanyavaad) or शुक्रिया (shukriyaa, thanks).

## Please come in, please sit — आइए, बैठिए (aaiye, baithiye)

<!-- topic: hospitality -->

When welcoming someone in Hindi, say आइए (aaiye, please come in); hosts often double it: आइए, आइए! Then बैठिए (baithiye, please sit down). A refined, extra-polite Urdu-style form is तशरीफ़ रखिए (tashreef rakhiye, please be seated). Offering food or drink: लीजिए (leejiye, please take / here you are), चाय लीजिए (chaay leejiye, please have some tea). To friends: आओ, बैठो (aao, baitho). Guests usually reply with धन्यवाद (dhanyavaad) or just a smile and जी (jee).

## May I …? — asking permission politely

To ask permission in Hindi, use क्या मैं … सकता हूँ? (kyaa main … saktaa hoon?); a woman says सकती हूँ (saktee hoon). Examples: क्या मैं अंदर आ सकता हूँ? (kyaa main andar aa saktaa hoon?, may I come in?), क्या मैं आपका फ़ोन इस्तेमाल कर सकती हूँ? (kyaa main aapkaa fon istemaal kar saktee hoon?, may I use your phone? — woman). A quick, gender-neutral alternative: मैं यहाँ बैठूँ? (main yahaan baithoon?, shall I sit here?). Polite answers: हाँ, ज़रूर (haan, zaroor, yes, of course) or जी, बिल्कुल (jee, bilkul, yes, certainly).
