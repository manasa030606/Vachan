# Travel and getting around in Hindi

## Where is the bathroom? — बाथरूम कहाँ है? (baathroom kahaan hai?)

<!-- topic: directions -->

To ask where the bathroom or toilet is in Hindi, say बाथरूम कहाँ है? (baathroom kahaan hai?). Start politely with सुनिए (suniye, excuse me): सुनिए, बाथरूम कहाँ है? Equally common: टॉयलेट किधर है? (toilet kidhar hai?, which way is the toilet?) or वॉशरूम कहाँ है? (washroom kahaan hai?). On public signs you will see शौचालय (shauchaalay, toilet), with पुरुष (purush, men) and महिला (mahilaa, women). To ask if there is one: क्या यहाँ बाथरूम है? (kyaa yahaan baathroom hai?, is there a bathroom here?). These questions are the same for men and women.

## A ticket, please — एक टिकट दीजिए (ek tikat deejiye)

To buy a ticket in Hindi, say where to and add टिकट दीजिए (tikat deejiye): लखनऊ का एक टिकट दीजिए (Lakhnaoo kaa ek tikat deejiye, one ticket to Lucknow, please) — Hindi says "Lucknow's ticket". Also correct: लखनऊ के लिए एक टिकट (Lakhnaoo ke lie ek tikat, a ticket for Lucknow). Two tickets: दो टिकट (do tikat). To ask the price: टिकट कितने का है? (tikat kitne kaa hai?, how much is the ticket?). The fare is किराया (kiraayaa): किराया कितना है? (kiraayaa kitnaa hai?). Ticket counter: टिकट खिड़की (tikat khidkee) or काउंटर (kaauntar).

## Bus, train, auto, taxi — vehicles in Hindi

<!-- type: vocabulary; skill: vocabulary -->

Hindi words for transport are mostly everyday English loanwords: बस (bas, bus), ट्रेन (tren, train; older Hindi रेलगाड़ी, relgaadee), ऑटो (oto, auto-rickshaw), टैक्सी (taiksee, taxi), कैब (kaib, app taxi), मेट्रो (metro), रिक्शा (rikshaa, cycle rickshaw), गाड़ी (gaadee, car / any vehicle). बस and ट्रेन are feminine: बस आ गई (bas aa gaee, the bus has come), ट्रेन लेट है (tren let hai, the train is late). "By bus" is बस से (bas se): मैं बस से जाऊँगा (main bas se jaaoongaa, I'll go by bus); a woman says जाऊँगी (jaaoongee). On foot: पैदल (paidal).

## Taking an auto-rickshaw — स्टेशन चलोगे? (steshan chaloge?)

In North India you agree on the auto fare first. The natural way to start is भैया, स्टेशन चलोगे? (bhaiyaa, steshan chaloge?, brother, will you go to the station?). Then ask the price: कितना लोगे? (kitnaa loge?, how much will you take?) or स्टेशन तक कितना लगेगा? (steshan tak kitnaa lagegaa?, how much to the station?). If it is too high: बहुत ज़्यादा है, थोड़ा कम कीजिए (bahut zyaadaa hai, thodaa kam keejiye, that's too much, please lower it a little). In cities with meters: मीटर से चलिए (meetar se chaliye, please go by the meter). Drivers are addressed as भैया (bhaiyaa); casual forms like चलोगे are normal with them, and the politer version is स्टेशन चलेंगे? (steshan chalenge?).

## Stop here, please — यहाँ रोकिए (yahaan rokiye)

To ask a driver to stop in Hindi, say यहाँ रोकिए (yahaan rokiye, please stop here) or, very commonly, भैया, यहीं रोक दो (bhaiyaa, yaheen rok do, stop right here). "A little further ahead": थोड़ा आगे (thodaa aage). "Stop at the next corner": अगले मोड़ पर रोकिए (agle mod par rokiye). On a bus, to say you want to get off: मुझे यहाँ उतरना है (mujhe yahaan utarnaa hai, I need to get off here), the same for men and women. Asking the conductor to tell you: स्टेशन आए तो बता दीजिए (steshan aae to bataa deejiye, please tell me when the station comes).

## Left, right, straight — बाएँ, दाएँ, सीधे (baaen, daaen, seedhe)

<!-- topic: directions -->

Hindi direction words: बाएँ (baaen, to the left), दाएँ (daaen, to the right), सीधे (seedhe, straight), पीछे (peechhe, back / behind), आगे (aage, ahead), सामने (saamne, in front / opposite). Polite commands: सीधे जाइए (seedhe jaaiye, go straight), बाएँ मुड़िए (baaen mudiye, turn left), दाएँ मुड़िए (daaen mudiye, turn right). People also mix in English: लेफ़्ट ले लीजिए (left le leejiye, take a left), राइट (right). Directions are usually given by landmarks: चौराहे से बाएँ (chauraahe se baaen, left at the crossing), मंदिर के पास (mandir ke paas, near the temple).

## Asking the way — … कहाँ है? (… kahaan hai?)

<!-- topic: directions -->

To ask the way in Hindi, say the place and add कहाँ है? (kahaan hai?, where is it?): सुनिए, रेलवे स्टेशन कहाँ है? (suniye, relve steshan kahaan hai?, excuse me, where is the railway station?). Or ask for directions: ... का रास्ता बताइए (... kaa raastaa bataaiye, please tell me the way to ...): अस्पताल का रास्ता बताइए (aspataal kaa raastaa bataaiye). "Is it this way?": इधर है? (idhar hai?). If you are lost: मैं रास्ता भूल गया हूँ (main raastaa bhool gayaa hoon, I'm lost); a woman says भूल गई हूँ (bhool gaee hoon). Say धन्यवाद (dhanyavaad) at the end.

## How far is it? — कितनी दूर है? (kitnee door hai?)

"How far is it?" in Hindi is कितनी दूर है? (kitnee door hai?). Answers usually come in minutes or landmarks rather than kilometres: पास में ही है (paas mein hee hai, it's right nearby), बहुत दूर है (bahut door hai, it's very far), पैदल दस मिनट (paidal das minat, ten minutes on foot). "Is it near?": क्या पास में है? (kyaa paas mein hai?). "Can I walk there?": क्या पैदल जा सकते हैं? (kyaa paidal jaa sakte hain?, can one go on foot?). दूर (door, far) is pronounced with a long oo, not like English "door".

## How long does it take? — कितना समय लगेगा? (kitnaa samay lagegaa?)

"How long will it take?" in Hindi is कितना समय लगेगा? (kitnaa samay lagegaa?) or, more casually, कितनी देर लगेगी? (kitnee der lagegee?) and कितना टाइम लगेगा? (kitnaa taaim lagegaa?). Answers: आधा घंटा (aadhaa ghantaa, half an hour), एक घंटा (ek ghantaa, one hour), दो घंटे (do ghante, two hours), दस मिनट (das minat, ten minutes). "When does the bus come?": बस कब आएगी? (bas kab aaegee?). "What time does the train leave?": ट्रेन कितने बजे चलेगी? (tren kitne baje chalegee?).

## At the station — platform and train questions

At an Indian railway station, useful Hindi questions: ट्रेन किस प्लेटफ़ॉर्म पर आएगी? (tren kis pletform par aaegee?, which platform will the train come to?), ट्रेन लेट है? (tren let hai?, is the train late?), क्या यह ट्रेन दिल्ली जाती है? (kyaa yah tren Dillee jaatee hai?, does this train go to Delhi?). ट्रेन is feminine, so its verbs end in -ई / -ती. Announcements use formal Hindi: कृपया ध्यान दें (kripyaa dhyaan den, attention please), यात्री (yaatree, passenger). A berth is बर्थ (barth) and a coach is डिब्बा (dibbaa).

## At the hotel — कमरा (kamraa)

At a hotel in Hindi: क्या कोई कमरा ख़ाली है? (kyaa koee kamraa khaalee hai?, is any room available?), एक रात का किराया कितना है? (ek raat kaa kiraayaa kitnaa hai?, how much for one night?), मुझे दो रात के लिए कमरा चाहिए (mujhe do raat ke lie kamraa chaahiye, I need a room for two nights). Note that in India होटल (hotal) can also mean a simple eating place; lodging is often called होटल, लॉज (loj) or गेस्ट हाउस. Other words: चाबी (chaabee, key), चेक-आउट (chek-aaut), गरम पानी (garam paani, hot water). Staff will use आप with you.

## Luggage and taking a train journey — सामान (saamaan)

सामान (saamaan) is the Hindi word for luggage and belongings. Useful sentences: मेरा सामान कहाँ है? (meraa saamaan kahaan hai?, where is my luggage?), यह सामान ऊपर रख दीजिए (yah saamaan oopar rakh deejiye, please put this luggage up there), मेरा बैग खो गया (meraa baig kho gayaa, my bag is lost). A porter at a station is a कुली (kulee) or porter. "Have a good journey": आपकी यात्रा शुभ हो (aapkee yaatraa shubh ho, formal) or "happy journey" in English. यात्रा (yaatraa) and सफ़र (safar) both mean journey.
