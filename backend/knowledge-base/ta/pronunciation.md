# Tamil pronunciation for beginners

## Short and long vowels change the meaning

In Tamil, vowel length matters: a long vowel is held about twice as long as a short one, and swapping them can change the word. பல் (pal) means "tooth", but பால் (paal) means "milk". கல் (kal) means "stone", but கால் (kaal) means "leg". In romanization Vachan writes the long vowels ஆ, ஈ and ஊ doubled: aa, ee, oo. Tamil also has short எ and ஒ next to long ஏ and ஓ, which English speakers often merge, so check the Tamil letter.

## One letter, several sounds

<!-- topic: aspiration -->

Tamil has no separate letters for aspirated sounds (no kh, gh, bh), and one letter can sound different depending on where it stands. க is "k" at the start of a word or when doubled: கடை (kadai, shop), அக்கா (akkaa, elder sister). Between vowels it softens to "g": மகன் (magan, son), and some speakers soften it further towards "h". After a nasal it is "g": தங்கை (thangai, younger sister). In the same way ச is "s" at the start (சாப்பாடு, saappaadu) and "ch" when doubled (பச்சை, pachchai, green); ட and த become "d" and "dh" between vowels (படம், padam, picture; அது, adhu, it).

## The special sound ழ (zha)

<!-- topic: retroflex -->

ழ is the most famous Tamil sound and appears in the name of the language itself: தமிழ் (thamizh). It is written "zh" in romanization but is not a "z". To say it, curl the tip of the tongue far back towards the roof of the mouth without touching it, and let the sound flow, a little like an American "r". Common words with ழ: பழம் (pazham, fruit), மழை (mazhai, rain), ஏழு (ezhu, seven), வாழைப்பழம் (vaazhaippazham, banana). In casual speech in some regions it is pronounced like ள.

## Three l sounds: ல, ள, ழ

<!-- topic: retroflex -->

Tamil has three different l-like sounds. ல (la) is an ordinary "l" with the tongue behind the upper teeth: பால் (paal, milk). ள (la) is retroflex: the tongue tip curls back to the roof of the mouth: பள்ளி (palli, school), அவள் (aval, she). ழ (zha) is the curled sound without touching: வழி (vazhi, way). Mixing them can change the meaning: வலி (vali) with ல means "pain", while வழி (vazhi) with ழ means "way, path". Romanization often writes ல and ள the same, so check the Tamil letter.

## Three n sounds and two r sounds

<!-- topic: retroflex; level: elementary -->

Tamil writes three "n" letters. ந is a dental n, used mostly at the start of words: நான் (naan, I), நன்றி (nandri). ன is the n used inside and at the end of words: நான் ends in ன். ண is retroflex, with the tongue curled back: தண்ணீர் (thanneer, water), கண் (kan, eye). There are also two r letters: ர is a light tap, as in பெயர் (peyar, name), and ற is a stronger, trilled r, as in அறை (arai, room). Together, ன்ற sounds "ndr" (நன்றி, nandri; ஒன்று, ondru) and doubled ற்ற sounds "ttr" (வெற்றி, vetri, victory, said roughly "vet-tri").

## Double consonants are held longer

<!-- topic: double-consonants; level: elementary -->

When a Tamil word has a doubled consonant, hold it for a moment before releasing it. அம்மா (ammaa, mother), அப்பா (appaa, father), அக்கா (akkaa, elder sister), வணக்கம் (vanakkam, hello) and சாப்பாடு (saappaadu, food) all have a held middle consonant. A doubled letter also keeps its hard sound: க்க is always "kk", never "gg". Saying the double sound as a single one can make a word unclear, so practise a short pause: am-maa, ap-paa.
