# Tamil culture and context

## Showing respect: அண்ணா, அக்கா, -ங்க and சார்

In Tamil, respect is shown through words of address and polite endings. People call strangers by family words: அண்ணா (annaa, elder brother) for a man a little older than you, and அக்கா (akkaa, elder sister) for a woman. In spoken Tamil, adding -ங்க (-nga) makes a word polite: ஆமாங்க (aamaanga, "yes (polite)"), சரிங்க (saringa, "okay (polite)"). Teachers, officials and customers are often called சார் (saar, "sir") or மேடம் (medam). Use நீங்கள் (neengal), not நீ (nee), with anyone older or unfamiliar, and the respectful அவர் (avar) when talking about them.

## Where Tamil is spoken

<!-- topic: about-language -->

Tamil is the official language of the Indian state of Tamil Nadu and of the union territory of Puducherry. Outside India it is an official language of Sri Lanka and of Singapore, and Tamil communities live in many other countries. Tamil is a Dravidian language, related to Telugu, Kannada and Malayalam, and it has one of the oldest continuous literary traditions in the world, going back to the classical Sangam poems. In 2004 Tamil became the first language to be declared a classical language by the Government of India. Speakers call their language தமிழ் (thamizh).

## Festivals: Pongal and Puthandu

<!-- topic: festivals; level: elementary -->

பொங்கல் (Pongal) is the Tamil harvest festival, celebrated in mid-January at the start of the Tamil month தை (Thai). Its main dish, also called pongal, is rice cooked with milk and jaggery in a new pot until it boils over, a sign of plenty; people shout பொங்கலோ பொங்கல்! (pongalo pongal!). Homes are decorated with கோலம் (kolam, patterns drawn at the doorstep). புத்தாண்டு (Puthandu), the Tamil New Year, falls in mid-April, on the first day of the month சித்திரை (Chithirai). The greeting is புத்தாண்டு வாழ்த்துகள் (puththaandu vaazhththugal, "New Year wishes").

## Everyday etiquette

<!-- topic: etiquette -->

Some useful Tamil etiquette: greet elders with வணக்கம் (vanakkam) and folded hands; take your shoes off before entering a home or temple; offer and receive things with the right hand. Hosts often ask சாப்பிட்டீங்களா? (saappitteengalaa?, "have you eaten?") as a friendly greeting, and guests are usually offered coffee or tea. When you have had enough food, a polite "enough" is போதும் (podhum).
