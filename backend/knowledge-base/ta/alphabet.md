# The Tamil script

## How Tamil writing works

Tamil is written in the Tamil script, which, like the other Indian scripts, grew out of the ancient Brahmi script. It is written from left to right. Every consonant letter such as க is read with a built-in short "a" (ka). Tamil letters are called எழுத்துகள் (ezhuththugal). The traditional Tamil alphabet has 247 letters: 12 vowels (உயிர் எழுத்து, uyir ezhuththu, "life letters"), 18 consonants (மெய் எழுத்து, mei ezhuththu, "body letters"), 216 combined consonant-vowel letters (உயிர்மெய், uyirmei) and one special letter, the ஆய்தம் ஃ. Compared with many Indian scripts, Tamil has few consonants, because it has no separate letters for aspirated sounds.

## Vowels — உயிர் எழுத்து (uyir ezhuththu)

<!-- topic: vowels -->

Tamil has 12 vowels, called உயிர் எழுத்து (uyir ezhuththu, "life letters"): அ (a), ஆ (aa), இ (i), ஈ (ee), உ (u), ஊ (oo), எ (e, short), ஏ (e, long), ஐ (ai), ஒ (o, short), ஓ (o, long), ஔ (au). Short and long vowels are different letters, so எ and ஏ, ஒ and ஓ must not be mixed up. In Vachan's romanization ee means ஈ and oo means ஊ, as in தண்ணீர் (thanneer) and மூன்று (moondru). Beginners usually start with அ ஆ இ ஈ உ ஊ.

## Consonants — மெய் எழுத்து (mei ezhuththu)

<!-- topic: consonants -->

Tamil has 18 consonants, called மெய் எழுத்து (mei ezhuththu, "body letters"). Written as pure consonants they are: க் ங் ச் ஞ் ட் ண் த் ந் ப் ம் ய் ர் ல் வ் ழ் ள் ற் ன். Tamil grammar sorts them into three groups of six: வல்லினம் (vallinam, "hard"): க ச ட த ப ற; மெல்லினம் (mellinam, "soft", the nasals): ங ஞ ண ந ம ன; இடையினம் (idaiyinam, "medium"): ய ர ல வ ழ ள. Tamil has no separate letters for aspirated sounds like kh or bh, and one letter can stand for more than one sound depending on its position.

## The pulli and the aaytham — புள்ளி, ஃ

<!-- topic: consonants -->

In Tamil a dot written on top of a consonant, called the புள்ளி (pulli, "dot"), removes the built-in "a" and leaves a pure consonant: க (ka) → க் (k), ம (ma) → ம் (m). That is why so many Tamil words end in a dotted letter: வணக்கம் (vanakkam), பால் (paal), நான் (naan). The ஆய்தம் (aaytham) ஃ is a special Tamil letter of three dots. It is rare and appears mostly in older or literary words, such as எஃகு (ehku, steel) and அஃது (ahdhu, "that", literary).

## Combined letters — உயிர்மெய் (uyirmei)

<!-- topic: vowel-signs -->

When a Tamil consonant is followed by a vowel, the two combine into one உயிர்மெய் (uyirmei, "life-body") letter: 18 consonants × 12 vowels = 216 letters. For க: க (ka), கா (kaa), கி (ki), கீ (kee), கு (ku), கூ (koo), கெ (ke), கே (ke, long), கை (kai), கொ (ko), கோ (ko, long), கௌ (kau). Note that the signs for e, ee and ai are written before the consonant (கெ, கே, கை), and o, oo and au wrap around it (கொ, கோ, கௌ). The u and oo forms change shape from consonant to consonant (கு, சு, டு, மு, ரு), so they are best learnt one by one.

## Grantha letters for borrowed sounds — ஜ ஷ ஸ ஹ க்ஷ

<!-- topic: consonants; level: elementary -->

Tamil uses a few extra letters, called Grantha letters (கிரந்த எழுத்துகள், kirandha ezhuththugal), to write sounds found in words borrowed from Sanskrit, English and other languages: ஜ (ja), ஷ (sha), ஸ (sa), ஹ (ha) and க்ஷ (ksha), plus the combination ஸ்ரீ (sree). Examples: ஜன்னல் (jannal, window), விஷயம் (vishayam, matter), ஸ்ரீ (sree, a respectful title). They are not part of the traditional 247 Tamil letters, and very formal "pure Tamil" writing tries to avoid them.
