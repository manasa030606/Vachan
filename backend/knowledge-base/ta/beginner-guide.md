# Getting started with Tamil

## How to start learning Tamil

A good order for a beginner in Tamil: first learn the 12 vowels (start with அ ஆ இ ஈ உ ஊ) and a few consonants (க ம ன ப ர ல), the dot ் (pulli) and how vowel signs attach to consonants; then learn greetings such as வணக்கம் (vanakkam) and நன்றி (nandri); then simple sentences like என் பெயர் … (en peyar …, my name is …) and எனக்கு தண்ணீர் வேண்டும் (enakku thanneer vendum, I want water). A few minutes every day works better than one long session. Reading the letters aloud helps you remember both the shape and the sound.

## Reading the romanization

<!-- topic: romanization -->

Vachan writes Tamil in English letters (romanization) so beginners can read it before they know the script. Doubled vowels mean a long sound: aa as in "father" (ஆ), ee as in "see" (ஈ), oo as in "moon" (ஊ). Long ஏ and ஓ are written simply e and o, as in வேண்டும் (vendum) and போ (po). "zh" stands for the special Tamil sound ழ, as in தமிழ் (thamizh); "th" and "dh" are soft dental sounds, not the English "th"; "ndr" is how ன்ற sounds, as in நன்றி (nandri). A doubled consonant (ammaa, akkaa) is held longer. Romanization is a helper: the script is the real spelling.

## Formal and informal "you"

<!-- topic: politeness; skill: conversation -->

The most important beginner rule in Tamil conversation: use நீங்கள் (neengal) for "you" with anyone older, a stranger, a teacher or a shopkeeper, and use நீ (nee) only with close friends, younger family members and children. Verbs change with it: நீங்கள் எப்படி இருக்கிறீர்கள்? (neengal eppadi irukkireergal?) is polite, நீ எப்படி இருக்கிறாய்? (nee eppadi irukkiraai?) is informal. When unsure, choose நீங்கள் — or in speech நீங்க (neenga).

## Spoken and written Tamil

<!-- topic: diglossia; level: elementary; skill: conversation -->

Tamil has two clearly different styles, called diglossia. Written or formal Tamil (used in books, news and speeches) keeps full forms: நான் நன்றாக இருக்கிறேன் (naan nandraaga irukkiren, I am fine). Spoken Tamil, used at home and on the street, shortens words: நான் நல்லா இருக்கேன் (naan nallaa irukken). Other pairs: இல்லை / இல்ல (illai / illa, no), வேண்டும் / வேணும் (vendum / venum, want), வருகிறேன் / வரேன் (varugiren / varen, I come), வீட்டில் / வீட்டுல (veettil / veettula, at home). Both are correct; learn the written form to read, and the spoken form to talk with people.
