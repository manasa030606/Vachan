# Getting started with Malayalam

## How to start learning Malayalam

A good order for a beginner in Malayalam: first learn a few vowels and consonants (അ ആ ഇ ഈ ഉ ഊ, then ക മ ന പ ര ല), how vowel signs attach to consonants, and the five chillu letters (ൻ ർ ൽ ൾ ൺ); then learn greetings such as നമസ്കാരം (namaskaaram) and നന്ദി (nandi); then simple sentences like എന്റെ പേര് … (ente peru …, my name is …) and എനിക്ക് വെള്ളം വേണം (enikku vellam venam, I want water). A few minutes every day works better than one long session.

## Reading the romanization

<!-- topic: romanization -->

Vachan writes Malayalam in English letters (romanization) so beginners can read it before they know the script. Doubled vowels mean a long sound: aa as in "father", ee as in "see", oo as in "moon". A doubled consonant (amma, vellam) is held a little longer. "th" is the soft dental t of ത, "zh" is the curled sound ഴ, and "nj" is ഞ. A final "u" written with ് (as in peru, randu) is the short half-u. Romanization is only a helper: ര and റ, or ല and ള, look the same in English letters, so always check the Malayalam script.

## Formal and informal "you"

<!-- topic: politeness; skill: conversation -->

An important beginner rule in Malayalam conversation: use നിങ്ങൾ (ningal) for "you" with anyone older, a stranger, a teacher or a shopkeeper, and use നീ (nee) only with close friends, younger family members and children. താങ്കൾ (thaankal) is a very formal "you" heard in speeches and official situations. With elders, many Malayalam speakers avoid "you" altogether and use a title such as ചേട്ടൻ (chettan) or സാർ (saar). When unsure, choose നിങ്ങൾ: നിങ്ങൾക്ക് സുഖമാണോ? (ningalkku sukhamaano?).

## Good news for beginners: one verb form for everyone

<!-- topic: verb-agreement; skill: grammar -->

Malayalam is easier than many Indian languages in one way: verbs do not change for the person, number or gender of the subject. Once you know പോകുന്നു (pokunnu, go / goes), പോയി (poyi, went) and പോകും (pokum, will go), you can use them with ഞാൻ (njaan, I), നീ (nee, you), അവൻ (avan, he), അവൾ (aval, she) or അവർ (avar, they) without any change. Focus your effort instead on the case endings, such as -ഇൽ (in) and -ക്ക് (to / for), and on the special sounds ഴ, ള and റ.
