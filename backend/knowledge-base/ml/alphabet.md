# The Malayalam script

## How Malayalam writing works

Malayalam is written in the Malayalam script, an abugida that grew out of the ancient Brahmi script through the Grantha script. In an abugida every consonant letter already carries a short "a" sound: ക is read "ka", not just "k". To write another vowel after a consonant you add a vowel sign to it, and to remove the vowel you add the chandrakkala ് or use a chillu letter. Malayalam is written from left to right and its letters are known for their round, curly shapes. A letter is called അക്ഷരം (aksharam).

## Vowels — സ്വരങ്ങൾ (swarangal)

<!-- topic: vowels -->

Malayalam vowels are called സ്വരങ്ങൾ (swarangal). The main ones are: അ (a), ആ (aa), ഇ (i), ഈ (ee), ഉ (u), ഊ (oo), ഋ (ru), എ (e), ഏ (long e), ഐ (ai), ഒ (o), ഓ (long o), ഔ (au). Two extra signs follow them: അം (am, the anusvara, a nasal "m" sound, as at the end of വെള്ളം, vellam, water) and അഃ (ah, the visarga). Short and long vowels are different letters in Malayalam, so എ (short e) and ഏ (long e), ഒ (short o) and ഓ (long o) must not be mixed up. Vachan's romanization writes both e-sounds as "e" and both o-sounds as "o" (പേര്, peru; ഓണം, onam), so check the script for the length. Beginners usually start with അ ആ ഇ ഈ ഉ ഊ.

## Consonants — വ്യഞ്ജനങ്ങൾ (vyanjanangal)

<!-- topic: consonants -->

Malayalam consonants are called വ്യഞ്ജനങ്ങൾ (vyanjanangal). They are arranged by where the sound is made in the mouth: ക ഖ ഗ ഘ ങ (ka group, throat), ച ഛ ജ ഝ ഞ (cha group, palate), ട ഠ ഡ ഢ ണ (retroflex group, tongue curled back), ത ഥ ദ ധ ന (dental group, tongue at the teeth), പ ഫ ബ ഭ മ (lips), then യ ര ല വ ശ ഷ സ ഹ ള ഴ റ. Each row goes plain, aspirated (with a puff of air), voiced, voiced-aspirated, nasal. The letters ഴ (zha) and റ (rra) are special Malayalam sounds that beginners need to practise.

## Vowel signs

<!-- topic: vowel-signs -->

When a vowel follows a consonant, Malayalam writes a vowel sign instead of the full vowel: ക (ka), കാ (kaa), കി (ki), കീ (kee), കു (ku), കൂ (koo), കൃ (kru), കെ (ke), കേ (ke, long), കൈ (kai), കൊ (ko), കോ (ko, long), കൗ (kau), കം (kam). Note that some Malayalam vowel signs are written before the consonant even though they are said after it: in കെ (ke) and കേ (long ke) the sign െ / േ stands on the left, and in കൊ (ko) and കോ (long ko) the sign wraps around the letter on both sides.

## The chandrakkala ് and the half-u

<!-- topic: chandrakkala -->

The Malayalam mark ് is called ചന്ദ്രക്കല (chandrakkala). Inside a word it removes the built-in "a" from a consonant, so that it can join the next consonant. At the end of a word it usually stands for a very short, half-swallowed "u" sound called സംവൃതോകാരം (samvruthokaaram, the "closed u"). That is why പേര് (name) is read peru, രണ്ട് (two) is read randu, ഒന്ന് (one) is read onnu and സുഹൃത്ത് (friend) is read suhruthu. Vachan's romanization writes this half-u as "u", but say it lightly and without rounding the lips.

## Chillu letters — ചില്ലക്ഷരങ്ങൾ (chillaksharangal)

<!-- topic: chillu -->

Malayalam has special letters called chillu (ചില്ല്) for a consonant that has no vowel at all, mostly at the end of a word. There are five common ones: ൻ (n), ർ (r), ൽ (l), ൾ (retroflex l) and ൺ (retroflex n). Examples: അച്ഛൻ (achchhan, father), ഞാൻ (njaan, I), അവർ (avar, they), പാൽ (paal, milk), അവൾ (aval, she), നിങ്ങൾ (ningal, you). Unlike the chandrakkala ്, a chillu letter adds no half-u sound: പാൽ is just "paal", while പേര് is "peru".

## Conjunct letters — കൂട്ടക്ഷരങ്ങൾ (koottaksharangal)

<!-- topic: conjuncts; level: elementary -->

When two consonants come together with no vowel between them, Malayalam often joins them into one combined shape called a കൂട്ടക്ഷരം (koottaksharam, conjunct). Examples: മ്മ in അമ്മ (amma, mother), ച്ഛ in അച്ഛൻ (achchhan, father), ള്ള in വെള്ളം (vellam, water), ക്ഷ in ഭക്ഷണം (bhakshanam, food) and ന്റ in എന്റെ (ente, my), which is read "nt". Some combinations can also be written as two separate letters joined by the chandrakkala ്, so the same Malayalam word may look slightly different in different fonts or books.
