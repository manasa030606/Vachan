# Malayalam pronunciation for beginners

## Short and long vowels change the meaning

In Malayalam, vowel length matters: a long vowel is held about twice as long as a short one, and swapping them can change the word. മല (mala) means "mountain", but മാല (maala) means "garland / necklace". In romanization Vachan writes long vowels doubled: aa, ee, oo. Malayalam also has short e and o (എ, ഒ) as well as long ee and oo (ഏ, ഓ), which English speakers often merge, for example എന്റെ (ente, my) and പേര് (peru, name).

## The zha sound — ഴ

<!-- topic: zha -->

ഴ (zha) is a famous Malayalam sound. Curl the tip of your tongue far back towards the roof of your mouth, without touching it, and let the sound flow out — somewhere between an English "r" and "l", with no buzz like "z". Examples: മഴ (mazha, rain), വഴി (vazhi, road / way), പഴം (pazham, fruit / banana), ഏഴ് (ezhu, seven). It is usually romanized "zh", but it is not said like the letters z and h. Note that the word മലയാളം (malayaalam) itself is spelt with ള, not ഴ.

## Dental and retroflex sounds: ല and ള

<!-- topic: retroflex -->

Malayalam has two sets of t, d, n and l sounds. Dental sounds ത (tha), ദ (da), ന (na) and ല (la) are made with the tongue at the upper teeth or just behind them, as in തല (thala, head) and നന്ദി (nandi, thank you). Retroflex sounds ട (ta), ഡ (da), ണ (na) and ള (la) are made with the tip of the tongue curled back, as in പട്ടി (patti, dog) and വെള്ളം (vellam, water). The word മലയാളം (malayaalam) has both: plain ല in the middle and retroflex ള near the end.

## Two r sounds: ര and റ, and the "tt" of റ്റ

<!-- topic: r-sounds -->

Malayalam has two r letters. ര (ra) is a soft, light tap, as in രണ്ട് (randu, two). റ (rra) is a slightly harder r made a little further forward, as in ആറ് (aaru, six). Both are romanized "r", so ആര് (aaru, who) and ആറ് (aaru, six) look the same in English letters. When റ is doubled, written റ്റ, it is not said as "rr" but as a sharp "tt": മുറ്റം (muttam, courtyard), ഒറ്റ (otta, single). After ന, the combination ന്റ is said "nt": എന്റെ (ente, my).

## Aspirated sounds

<!-- topic: aspiration -->

Malayalam letters such as ഖ (kha), ഘ (gha), ഛ (chha), ധ (dha) and ഭ (bha) are aspirated: they are said with a clear puff of air. The plain letters ക (ka) and പ (pa) have no puff — like the k in "skin", not the k in "kin". Aspirated letters mostly appear in words borrowed from Sanskrit, such as ഭക്ഷണം (bhakshanam, food), സുഖം (sukham, well-being) and അച്ഛൻ (achchhan, father). Hold your hand in front of your mouth: you should feel air on ഖ but not on ക.

## The nj sound — ഞ

<!-- topic: nasals -->

Malayalam uses the nasal letter ഞ (nja) much more than most Indian languages, including at the start of everyday words. It sounds like the "ny" in "canyon", and Vachan writes it "nj". Examples: ഞാൻ (njaan, I), ഞങ്ങൾ (njangal, we — not including you) and മഞ്ഞ (manja, yellow). Do not say a hard "j" as in "jam": the sound is a soft nasal. The related letter ങ (nga), as in ഞങ്ങൾ, is the "ng" of "singing".

## Double consonants are held longer

<!-- topic: double-consonants; level: elementary -->

Malayalam has many doubled consonants, and they must be held for a moment before release. അമ്മ (amma, mother), അച്ഛൻ (achchhan, father), വെള്ളം (vellam, water), ചേട്ടൻ (chettan, elder brother), ഒന്ന് (onnu, one) and മൂന്ന് (moonnu, three) all have a held middle consonant. Saying the double sound as a single one can make a word unclear or change it, so practise a short pause: am-ma, vel-lam, chet-tan.
