# Malayalam greetings and common phrases

## Hello — നമസ്കാരം (namaskaaram)

The usual way to say hello in Malayalam is നമസ്കാരം (namaskaaram), often said with the palms pressed together. It works at any time of day and with anyone, and it is the polite choice with elders and strangers. Among friends people often just say "hi", or ask എന്തൊക്കെയുണ്ട്? (enthokkeyundu?, "how are things?") or എന്താ വിശേഷം? (enthaa vishesham?, "what's new?").

## Thank you — നന്ദി (nandi)

To say thank you in Malayalam, say നന്ദി (nandi). To make it stronger, say വളരെ നന്ദി (valare nandi, "thank you very much"). In everyday speech many Malayalam speakers simply say "thanks" in English, and between close friends and family a spoken thank-you is often left out altogether, because it can sound too formal.

## How are you? — നിങ്ങൾക്ക് സുഖമാണോ? (ningalkku sukhamaano?)

<!-- topic: everyday-phrases -->

To ask "How are you?" in Malayalam, say നിങ്ങൾക്ക് സുഖമാണോ? (ningalkku sukhamaano?), literally "to you, is it well-being?". In casual speech just സുഖമാണോ? (sukhamaano?) is enough. The usual answer is എനിക്ക് സുഖമാണ് (enikku sukhamaanu, "I am fine"), or simply സുഖം (sukham). You can return the question with നിങ്ങൾക്കോ? (ningalkko?, "and you?").

## My name is… — എന്റെ പേര്… (ente peru…)

<!-- topic: introductions -->

To introduce yourself in Malayalam, say എന്റെ പേര് ആശ (ente peru Asha), literally "my name Asha"; you can also add ആണ് (aanu, is) at the end. To ask someone's name politely: നിങ്ങളുടെ പേര് എന്താണ്? (ningalude peru enthaanu?, "what is your name?"). Informally, to a child or a close friend: നിന്റെ പേര് എന്താ? (ninte peru enthaa?). You can add ഞാൻ കൊച്ചിയിൽ നിന്നാണ് (njaan Kochiyil ninnaanu, "I am from Kochi").

## Yes and no — അതെ, ഇല്ല, അല്ല

<!-- topic: everyday-phrases -->

"Yes" in Malayalam is അതെ (athe), meaning "that's right". There are two words for "no". ഇല്ല (illa) means "there isn't / did not / will not": വെള്ളം ഇല്ല (vellam illa, "there is no water"). അല്ല (alla) means "it is not (so)", used to deny what something is: ഇത് പാൽ അല്ല (ithu paal alla, "this is not milk"). To answer "do you have…?" with yes, Malayalam speakers often say ഉണ്ട് (undu, "there is / I have").

## Please and sorry — ദയവായി, ക്ഷമിക്കണം

<!-- topic: politeness -->

"Please" in Malayalam is ദയവായി (dayavaayi), but it is quite formal and used mostly in writing and announcements. In speech, politeness usually comes from the verb ending -ഊ (-oo) instead: വരൂ (varoo, "please come"), ഇരിക്കൂ (irikkoo, "please sit"). A gentle request can also end in -ആമോ? (-aamo?): പറയാമോ? (parayaamo?, "could you tell me?"). "Sorry" or "excuse me" is ക്ഷമിക്കണം (kshamikkanam); "sorry" in English is also very common.

## Goodbye — പോയിട്ട് വരാം (poyittu varaam)

<!-- topic: greetings -->

Malayalam speakers rarely say a direct "goodbye". Instead they say പോയിട്ട് വരാം (poyittu varaam), meaning "I'll go and come (back)", which sounds warmer than a final farewell. A conversation is also often closed with ശരി (shari, "okay / alright"). Among friends, "bye" in English is common.

## I don't understand — എനിക്ക് മനസ്സിലായില്ല (enikku manassilaayilla)

<!-- topic: everyday-phrases; level: elementary -->

If you do not understand something in Malayalam, say എനിക്ക് മനസ്സിലായില്ല (enikku manassilaayilla, "I didn't understand"). To ask someone to speak slowly: പതുക്കെ പറയാമോ? (pathukke parayaamo?, "could you say it slowly?"). To ask what a word means: ഇതിന്റെ അർത്ഥം എന്താണ്? (ithinte arththam enthaanu?, "what does this mean?"). If you did understand, say മനസ്സിലായി (manassilaayi, "I understood").

## Shopping and directions — ഇതിന് എത്രയാണ്? (ithinu ethrayaanu?)

<!-- topic: shopping; level: elementary -->

At a shop or market in Malayalam, ask ഇതിന് എത്രയാണ്? (ithinu ethrayaanu?, "how much is this?"). To ask for something: എനിക്ക് വെള്ളം വേണം (enikku vellam venam, "I want water"); to refuse: വേണ്ട (venda, "I don't want it"). To ask for a place, put the place first and add എവിടെയാണ്? (evideyaanu?, "where is it?"): ബസ് സ്റ്റാൻഡ് എവിടെയാണ്? (bus stand evideyaanu?, "where is the bus stand?").
