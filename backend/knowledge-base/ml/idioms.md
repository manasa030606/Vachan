# Common Malayalam idioms and sayings

## മുറ്റത്തെ മുല്ലയ്ക്ക് മണമില്ല (muttathe mullaykku manamilla)

Literal meaning: "the jasmine in your own courtyard has no fragrance". Malayalam speakers use this saying when people fail to value something good simply because it is close at hand, at home or familiar, while admiring the same thing elsewhere. It is similar to the English idea that familiarity breeds contempt. Example: a family ignores the talented cook at home and praises restaurant food — മുറ്റത്തെ മുല്ലയ്ക്ക് മണമില്ല!

## അണ്ണാൻ കുഞ്ഞും തന്നാലായത് (annaan kunjum thannaalaayathu)

Literal meaning: "even the little squirrel (does) what it can". This Malayalam saying comes from the Ramayana story of the squirrel that helped build the bridge to Lanka by carrying grains of sand. It means that everyone, however small, can contribute in their own way. People say it modestly about their own small help to a big effort.

## ആന കൊടുത്താലും ആശ കൊടുക്കരുത് (aana koduthaalum aasha kodukkaruthu)

Literal meaning: "even if you give (someone) an elephant, don't give (them) hope". This Malayalam saying warns against raising false hopes or making promises you will not keep: it is better to be generous or to say no plainly than to leave someone waiting. Note that ആശ (aasha) here is the common word for "hope / desire" — the same word as the name Asha used in Vachan's examples.
