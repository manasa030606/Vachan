# Telugu culture and context

## Showing respect: గారు, అండీ and family words

In Telugu, respect is shown with words added to names. గారు (gaaru) after a name or title is polite: రవి గారు (Ravi gaaru), టీచర్ గారు (teacher gaaru). Adding -అండీ (-andi) to a word softens it: అవునండీ (avunandi, "yes (polite)"). People also call strangers by family words — అన్న (anna, elder brother), అక్క (akka, elder sister) — and children call adults ఆంటీ (aunty) or అంకుల్ (uncle). Use మీరు (meeru), not నువ్వు (nuvvu), with anyone older or unfamiliar.

## Where Telugu is spoken

<!-- topic: about-language -->

Telugu is the official language of Andhra Pradesh and Telangana and one of the most widely spoken languages in India, with large communities around the world as well. It is a Dravidian language, related to Kannada, Tamil and Malayalam, and it borrows many words from Sanskrit. The Government of India declared Telugu a classical language in 2008. Because its words end in vowels it is sometimes called "the Italian of the East".

## Festivals: Ugadi and Sankranti

<!-- topic: festivals; level: elementary -->

ఉగాది (Ugadi) is the Telugu New Year, celebrated in spring (March or April). Families prepare ఉగాది పచ్చడి (ugaadi pachchadi), a dish that mixes six tastes — sweet, sour, salty, bitter, spicy and tangy — as a reminder that the year will bring all kinds of experiences. సంక్రాంతి (Sankranti) in January is the harvest festival, when people draw ముగ్గులు (muggulu, rangoli patterns) in front of their homes.

## Everyday etiquette

<!-- topic: etiquette -->

Some useful Telugu etiquette: greet elders with నమస్కారం (namaskaaram) and folded hands; take your shoes off before entering a home or temple; offer and receive things with the right hand. When you are offered food, a polite "enough" is చాలు (chaalu). Guests are often offered coffee or tea first, and it is polite to accept something small.
