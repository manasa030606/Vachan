# Getting started with Telugu

## How to start learning Telugu

A good order for a beginner in Telugu: first learn a few vowels and consonants (అ ఆ ఇ ఈ ఉ ఊ, then క మ న ప ర ల) and how vowel signs attach to consonants; then learn greetings such as నమస్కారం (namaskaaram) and ధన్యవాదాలు (dhanyavaadaalu); then simple sentences like నా పేరు … (naa peru …, my name is …). A few minutes every day works better than one long session. Reading the letters aloud helps you remember both the shape and the sound.

## Reading the romanization

<!-- topic: romanization -->

Vachan writes Telugu in English letters (romanization) so beginners can read it before they know the script. Doubled vowels mean a long sound: aa as in "father", ee as in "see", oo as in "moon". A doubled consonant (amma, akka) is held a little longer. Capital letters are not used for retroflex sounds, so always check the Telugu letter too: romanization is a helper, the script is the real spelling.

## Formal and informal "you"

<!-- topic: politeness; skill: conversation -->

The most important beginner rule in Telugu conversation: use మీరు (meeru) for "you" with anyone older, a stranger, a teacher or a shopkeeper, and use నువ్వు (nuvvu) only with close friends, younger family members and children. Verbs change with it: మీరు ఎలా ఉన్నారు? (meeru elaa unnaaru?) is polite, ఎలా ఉన్నావు? (elaa unnaavu?) is informal. When unsure, choose మీరు.
