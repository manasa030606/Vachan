# Telugu pronunciation for beginners

## Short and long vowels change the meaning

In Telugu, vowel length matters: a long vowel is held about twice as long as a short one, and swapping them can change the word. కలం (kalam) means "pen", but కాలం (kaalam) means "time". In romanization Vachan writes long vowels doubled: aa, ee, oo. Telugu also has short e and o (ఎ, ఒ) as well as long ee and oo (ఏ, ఓ), which English speakers often merge, for example పెట్టు (pettu, to put) and పేరు (peru, name).

## Dental and retroflex sounds

<!-- topic: retroflex -->

Telugu has two sets of t, d, n and l sounds. Dental sounds త (ta), ద (da), న (na), ల (la) are made with the tongue touching the back of the upper teeth, as in తల (tala, head). Retroflex sounds ట (ta), డ (da), ణ (na), ళ (la) are made with the tip of the tongue curled back to the roof of the mouth, as in టమాటా (tamaataa, tomato) and నీళ్ళు (neellu, water). English "t" and "d" sit between the two, so practise feeling where your tongue touches.

## Aspirated sounds

<!-- topic: aspiration -->

Letters such as ఖ (kha), ఘ (gha), ధ (dha) and భ (bha) are aspirated: they are said with a clear puff of air. The plain letters క (ka) and ప (pa) have no puff — like the k in "skin", not the k in "kin". Aspirated letters mostly appear in words borrowed from Sanskrit, such as ధన్యవాదాలు (dhanyavaadaalu, thank you) and భోజనం (bhojanam, meal). Hold your hand in front of your mouth: you should feel air on ఖ but not on క.

## Words end in vowels

<!-- topic: word-endings -->

Almost every native Telugu word ends in a vowel, which gives the language its smooth, musical sound. Many nouns end in -u, such as పాలు (paalu, milk), నీళ్ళు (neellu, water) and పేరు (peru, name), and many end in -am, such as భోజనం (bhojanam, meal). Do not drop the final vowel: say "paalu", not "paal". Words borrowed from English often get an extra -u too, for example బస్సు (bassu, bus).

## Double consonants are held longer

<!-- topic: double-consonants; level: elementary -->

When a Telugu word has a doubled consonant, hold it for a moment before releasing it. అమ్మ (amma, mother), నాన్న (naanna, father), అక్క (akka, elder sister) and పిల్లి (pilli, cat) all have a held middle consonant. Saying the double sound as a single one can make a word unclear or change it, so practise a short pause: am-ma, ak-ka.
