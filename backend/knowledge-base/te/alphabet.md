# The Telugu script

## How Telugu writing works

Telugu is written in the Telugu script, an abugida that grew out of the ancient Brahmi script and is a close cousin of the Kannada script. In an abugida every consonant letter already carries a short "a" sound: క is read "ka", not just "k". To write another vowel after a consonant you add a vowel sign to it. Telugu is written from left to right and letters are known for their round shapes. A letter is called అక్షరం (aksharam); the set of letters is అక్షరాలు (aksharaalu).

## Vowels — అచ్చులు (achchulu)

<!-- topic: vowels -->

Telugu vowels are called అచ్చులు (achchulu). The main ones are: అ (a), ఆ (aa), ఇ (i), ఈ (ii / ee), ఉ (u), ఊ (uu / oo), ఋ (ru), ఎ (e), ఏ (ee), ఐ (ai), ఒ (o), ఓ (oo), ఔ (au). Two extra signs follow them: అం (am, the anusvara, a nasal "m/n" sound) and అః (ah, the visarga). Short and long vowels are different letters, so ఎ (short e) and ఏ (long e), ఒ (short o) and ఓ (long o) must not be mixed up. Note: Vachan's romanization writes both ఎ and ఏ as "e" and both ఒ and ఓ as "o" (as in నేను, nenu and పేరు, peru), and keeps "ee" for ఈ and "oo" for ఊ — so check the Telugu letter for e/o length. Beginners usually start with అ ఆ ఇ ఈ ఉ ఊ.

## Consonants — హల్లులు (hallulu)

<!-- topic: consonants -->

Telugu consonants are called హల్లులు (hallulu). They are arranged by where the sound is made in the mouth: క ఖ గ ఘ ఙ (ka group, throat), చ ఛ జ ఝ ఞ (cha group, palate), ట ఠ డ ఢ ణ (retroflex group, tongue curled back), త థ ద ధ న (dental group, tongue at the teeth), ప ఫ బ భ మ (lips), then య ర ల వ శ ష స హ ళ క్ష ఱ. Each row goes plain, aspirated (with a puff of air), voiced, voiced-aspirated, nasal. Every consonant is read with "a" until a vowel sign changes it.

## Vowel signs and the talakattu — గుణింతాలు (guninthaalu)

<!-- topic: vowel-signs -->

When a vowel follows a consonant, Telugu writes a vowel sign (మాత్ర) instead of the full vowel. The table of a consonant with every vowel sign is called గుణింతం (gunintham): క (ka), కా (kaa), కి (ki), కీ (kii), కు (ku), కూ (kuu), కె (ke), కే (kee), కై (kai), కొ (ko), కో (koo), కౌ (kau), కం (kam). Many Telugu consonants carry a small tick on top called the తలకట్టు (talakattu); it shows the built-in "a". When a sign such as ి (i) is added, the tick is replaced: క → కి.

## Conjunct letters — వత్తులు (vattulu)

<!-- topic: conjuncts; level: elementary -->

When two consonants come together with no vowel between them, Telugu writes the second consonant as a small form under (or beside) the first. This small form is called a వత్తు (vattu). Examples: అమ్మ (amma, mother) has మ under మ; నాన్న (naanna, father) has న under న; క్ష (ksha) in క్షమించండి (kshaminchandi, sorry). A consonant with no vowel at all is shown with the పొల్లు (pollu) mark ్, as in క్ (k).
