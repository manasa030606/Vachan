# Bengali pronunciation for beginners

## The inherent vowel অ is read "o"

In Bengali the built-in vowel of every consonant, and the letter অ itself, is pronounced "o", not "a" as in Hindi. So ক is "ko", ন is "no", and মন (mon) means "mind". This is why নমস্কার is said "nomoshkar". At the end of many words the inherent vowel is not pronounced at all: নাম is "naam" (name), not "naamo", and দুধ is "dudh" (milk). Romanization in Vachan writes this sound as "o" and আ (আ-কার) as "aa", as in বাবা (baabaa, father).

## শ, ষ and স mostly sound "sh"

<!-- topic: sibilants -->

Bengali has three letters for s-sounds — শ, ষ and স — but in everyday speech all three are usually pronounced "sh". সাত (shaat, seven) and ষাট (shaat, sixty) sound the same; শাক (shaak) means leafy greens. This is why নমস্কার is said "nomoshkar". In some conjunct clusters, especially before ত, থ, ন or র, the letter স keeps a real "s" sound, as in আস্তে (aaste, slowly). When you learn a new Bengali word, learn its spelling separately, because the sound alone does not tell you which of the three letters to write.

## ই/ঈ and উ/ঊ sound almost the same

<!-- topic: vowel-length -->

Bengali writes a "short" ই and a "long" ঈ, and a "short" উ and a "long" ঊ, but in normal speech each pair is pronounced almost the same. Vowel length does not usually change the meaning of a spoken word in Bengali, so Vachan writes both as "i" or "u". The spelling still matters in writing: কি (ki) is the yes/no question word, while কী (ki) means "what", as in আপনার নাম কী? (aapnaar naam ki?, what is your name?). Similarly, দিন (din, day) and নীল (nil, blue) both have an "i" sound.

## ব and ভ: "b" and "bh"

<!-- topic: aspiration -->

In Bengali the letter ব is pronounced "b", as in বাবা (baabaa, father) and বন্ধু (bondhu, friend); native Bengali words do not have a "v" sound. The letter ভ is the aspirated "bh", said with a puff of air, as in ভালো (bhaalo, good / well) and ভাত (bhaat, cooked rice). Do not say "v" for either letter. The name of the language itself, বাংলা, is said "baanglaa".

## য-ফলা: ব্যাগ is "byag"

<!-- topic: conjuncts; level: elementary -->

The য-ফলা (jo-pholaa) is a small sign ্য attached to a consonant. At the start of a Bengali word, together with আ-কার it usually gives a "ya / æ" sound: ব্যাগ (byag, bag), হ্যাঁ (hyaan, yes). In the middle of a word the য-ফলা usually doubles the consonant before it instead of adding a "y": ধন্যবাদ is pronounced "dhonnobad" (thank you), বিদ্যা is "biddaa" (learning). So the spelling and the sound can look quite different; learn such words by ear.

## Aspirated sounds

<!-- topic: aspiration -->

Bengali letters such as খ (kho), ঘ (gho), ছ (chho), ঝ (jho), থ (tho), ধ (dho), ফ (pho) and ভ (bho) are aspirated: they are said with a clear puff of air. The plain letters ক (ko), চ (cho), ত (to), দ (do) and প (po) have no puff. Aspiration changes words, so practise it: দুধ (dudh, milk), আছি (aachhi, I am), ভালো (bhaalo, good), খাবার (khaabaar, food). In everyday speech many Bengali speakers say ফ close to an English "f".

## Dental and retroflex sounds

<!-- topic: retroflex -->

Bengali has two sets of t and d sounds. Dental ত (to), থ (tho), দ (do), ধ (dho) are made with the tongue touching the back of the upper teeth, as in তুমি (tumi, you) and দুধ (dudh, milk). Retroflex ট (to), ঠ (tho), ড (do), ঢ (dho) are made with the tip of the tongue curled back, as in টাকা (taakaa, money) and ডাল (daal, lentils). The letters ন and ণ are both simply pronounced "n" in Bengali. ড় is a quick flap "r", as in বাড়ি (baari, house).
