# Asking questions in Bengali

## Yes/no questions with কি (ki)

To make a yes/no question in Bengali, add কি (ki) after the subject and keep the normal word order: আপনি কি চা খান? (aapni ki chaa khaan?, "do you drink tea?"), তুমি কি কাল আসবে? (tumi ki kaal aashbe?, "will you come tomorrow?"). This কি has no meaning of its own; it just marks the question. In casual speech কি is often dropped and a rising voice is enough: চা খাবে? (chaa khaabe?, "want some tea?"). In writing, the question marker is spelled কি with a short i, while "what" is কী with a long ī; both are said "ki". Answer with হ্যাঁ (hyaan, yes) or না (naa, no).

## What? — কী (ki)

<!-- type: phrase -->

"What" in Bengali is কী (ki). It usually comes just before the verb, or at the end when there is no verb: এটা কী? (etaa ki?, "what is this?"), আপনার নাম কী? (aapnaar naam ki?, "what is your name?"), তুমি কী করছ? (tumi ki korchho?, "what are you doing?"), আপনি কী খাবেন? (aapni ki khaaben?, "what will you eat?"). Doubling it asks for a list: কী কী লাগবে? (ki ki laagbe?, "what all do you need?"). "What happened?" is কী হল? (ki holo?) or কী হয়েছে? (ki hoyechhe?). "What does this mean?" is এর মানে কী? (er maane ki?).

## Who? — কে (ke)

<!-- type: phrase -->

"Who" in Bengali is কে (ke): উনি কে? (uni ke?, "who is he / she?", respectful), ও কে? (o ke?, "who is that?", casual), কে এসেছে? (ke eshechhe?, "who has come?"). On the phone: কে বলছেন? (ke bolchhen?, "who is speaking?"). The other forms: কাকে (kaake, whom / to whom): কাকে চাই? (kaake chaai?, "whom do you want?"); কার (kaar, whose): এটা কার বই? (etaa kaar boi?, "whose book is this?"); কার সঙ্গে (kaar shonge, with whom). Plural "who all": কে কে (ke ke): কে কে আসবে? (ke ke aashbe?, "who all are coming?").

## Where? — কোথায় (kothaay)

<!-- type: phrase -->

"Where" in Bengali is কোথায় (kothaay). Put the place or person first and কোথায় after it: বাথরুমটা কোথায়? (baathrumtaa kothaay?, "where is the bathroom?"), আপনি কোথায় থাকেন? (aapni kothaay thaaken?, "where do you live?"), তুমি কোথায়? (tumi kothaay?, "where are you?"). "From where" is কোথা থেকে (kothaa theke): আপনি কোথা থেকে এসেছেন? (aapni kothaa theke eshechhen?, "where are you from?"). "Which way" is কোন দিকে (kon dike). On the phone, কোথায় আছ? (kothaay aachho?, "where are you right now?").

## When? — কখন and কবে (kokhon, kobe)

<!-- type: phrase -->

Bengali has two words for "when". কখন (kokhon) asks about a time of day: বাস কখন আসবে? (baas kokhon aashbe?, "when will the bus come?"), তুমি কখন ফিরবে? (tumi kokhon phirbe?, "when will you be back?"). কবে (kobe) asks which day or date: পরীক্ষা কবে? (porikkhaa kobe?, "when is the exam?"), আপনি কবে যাচ্ছেন? (aapni kobe jaachchhen?, "when are you leaving (which day)?"). For clock time use কটায় (kotaay, "at what time"): ক্লাস কটায়? (klaas kotaay?, "what time is the class?"). Using the wrong one is a common learner mistake.

## Why? — কেন (keno)

<!-- type: phrase -->

"Why" in Bengali is কেন (keno). It usually goes before the verb or at the end: তুমি কেন এলে না? (tumi keno ele naa?, "why didn't you come?"), আপনার দেরি হল কেন? (aapnaar deri holo keno?, "why were you late?"), কেন? (keno?, "why?"). A softer, more curious form is কেন বলুন তো? (keno bolun to?, "why, may I ask?"). The answer often uses কারণ (kaaron, because): কারণ বৃষ্টি হচ্ছিল (kaaron brishti hochchhilo, "because it was raining"). Casual "because" can also be expressed with বলে (bole) at the end: শরীর খারাপ বলে আসিনি (shorir khaaraap bole aashini, "I didn't come because I was unwell").

## How? — কীভাবে and কেমন (kibhaabe, kemon)

<!-- type: phrase -->

Bengali has two words for "how". কীভাবে (kibhaabe) means "in what way": এটা কীভাবে করব? (etaa kibhaabe korbo?, "how do I do this?"), স্টেশনে কীভাবে যাব? (steshone kibhaabe jaabo?, "how do I get to the station?"). কেমন (kemon) asks about condition or quality: আপনি কেমন আছেন? (aapni kemon aachhen?, "how are you?"), খাবারটা কেমন? (khaabaartaa kemon?, "how is the food?"), সিনেমাটা কেমন লাগল? (sinemaataa kemon laaglo?, "how did you like the film?"). Short alternative for "how" in speech: কী করে (ki kore): কী করে জানলে? (ki kore jaanle?, "how did you know?").

## How much and how many? — কত, কটা, কজন (koto, kotaa, kojon)

<!-- type: phrase -->

"How much" in Bengali is কত (koto): দাম কত? (daam koto?, "how much is the price?"), বয়স কত? (boyosh koto?, "how old?"), কত দূর? (koto dur?, "how far?"). For counting things use কটা (kotaa, how many): কটা চাই? (kotaa chaai?, "how many do you want?"). For people use কজন (kojon): কজন আসবে? (kojon aashbe?, "how many people are coming?"). For time on a clock, কটা also appears: কটা বাজে? (kotaa baaje?, "what time is it?"). For an amount of stuff: কতটা (kototaa): কতটা দেব? (kototaa debo?, "how much shall I give?").

## Which? — কোন and কোনটা (kon, kontaa)

<!-- type: phrase -->

"Which" in Bengali is কোন (kon) before a noun: কোন বাস? (kon baas?, "which bus?"), তুমি কোন কলেজে পড়ো? (tumi kon koleje poro?, "which college do you study at?"), কোন দিকে? (kon dike?, "which way?"). On its own, "which one" is কোনটা (kontaa): কোনটা ভালো? (kontaa bhaalo?, "which one is better?"), আপনার কোনটা পছন্দ? (aapnaar kontaa pochhondo?, "which one do you like?"). Note: কোনো (kono) is a different word meaning "any": কোনো অসুবিধা নেই (kono oshubidhaa nei, "no problem at all").

## Where question words go — word order in questions

<!-- level: elementary -->

In Bengali, question words do not move to the front as in English. They stay where the answer would go, usually just before the verb. Statement: আমি কলকাতায় থাকি (aami Kolkatay thaaki, "I live in Kolkata"). Question: আপনি কোথায় থাকেন? (aapni kothaay thaaken?, "where do you live?") — কোথায় takes the place of কলকাতায়. Likewise: তুমি কী খাচ্ছ? (tumi ki khaachchho?, "what are you eating?"), উনি কখন আসবেন? (uni kokhon aashben?, "when will he/she come?"). In short questions with no verb, the question word goes last: এটা কী? (etaa ki?), দাম কত? (daam koto?). Yes/no questions add কি after the subject.

## Tag questions — তাই না? (taai naa?)

<!-- type: phrase; level: elementary -->

To add "isn't it? / right?" at the end of a Bengali sentence, use তাই না? (taai naa?): আজ খুব গরম, তাই না? (aaj khub gorom, taai naa?, "it's very hot today, isn't it?"), তুমি কাল আসবে, তাই না? (tumi kaal aashbe, taai naa?, "you're coming tomorrow, right?"). A shorter spoken version is না? (naa?) at the end: ভালো, না? (bhaalo, naa?, "nice, isn't it?"). To react to news with "really?", say তাই? (taai?) or সত্যি? (shotti?, "really?"). Example: এটা তোমার বই, তাই না? (etaa tomaar boi, taai naa?) = "This is your book, isn't it?"

## Polite questions — "Could you…?" with -বেন (-ben)

<!-- level: elementary -->

To ask a polite question or request in Bengali, use the polite future form ending in -বেন (-ben) with আপনি: একটু সাহায্য করবেন? (ektu shaahaajjo korben?, "could you help a little?"), আরেকবার বলবেন? (aarekbaar bolben?, "could you say it again?"), জলটা দেবেন? (joltaa deben?, "could you pass the water?"). This sounds gentler than a direct command such as বলুন (bolun, "say"). Adding একটু (ektu, "a little") softens it even more. For "can I…?", use পারি (paari): আমি কি এখানে বসতে পারি? (aami ki ekhaane boshte paari?, "may I sit here?"). With friends use the তুমি future -বে (-be): একটু দেবে? (ektu debe?, "could you give it?").

## How do you say … in Bengali? — বাংলায় কী বলে? (baanglaay ki bole?)

<!-- type: phrase -->

To ask how to say something in Bengali, say এটাকে বাংলায় কী বলে? (etaake baanglaay ki bole?, "what do you call this in Bengali?"). For an English word: "water" বাংলায় কী বলে? ("water" baanglaay ki bole?). To ask what a word means: এর মানে কী? (er maane ki?, "what does this mean?") or এই কথাটার মানে কী? (ei kothaataar maane ki?, "what does this word mean?"). To ask for the spelling: এটা কীভাবে লেখে? (etaa kibhaabe lekhe?, "how is this written?"). If people switch to English: বাংলায় বলুন (baanglaay bolun, "please say it in Bengali").

## Answering questions — হ্যাঁ, না, জানি না

<!-- type: phrase -->

Short Bengali answers: হ্যাঁ (hyaan, yes), না (naa, no), হুঁ (hun, mm-hm), আচ্ছা (aachchhaa, okay / I see), ঠিক আছে (thik aachhe, all right). "I don't know" is জানি না (jaani naa); "I'm not sure" is ঠিক জানি না (thik jaani naa). Bengalis often answer by repeating the verb instead of saying yes: যাবে? — যাব (jaabe? — jaabo, "will you go? — I will"); আছে? — আছে (aachhe? — aachhe, "is there? — there is"). For "of course": অবশ্যই (oboshshoi) or নিশ্চয়ই (nishchoyi). For "maybe": হয়তো (hoyto).
