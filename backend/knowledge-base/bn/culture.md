# Bengali culture and context

## Showing respect: দাদা, দিদি and আপনি

In Bengali, people are often addressed with family words. দাদা (daadaa, elder brother) and দিদি (didi, elder sister) are used for anyone a little older, and they are shortened to -দা (-daa) and -দি (-di) after a name: রবিদা (Robi-daa), মিতাদি (Mita-di). Older men are often called কাকু (kaaku, uncle) and older women মাসি (maasi, aunt). Use আপনি (aapni), not তুমি (tumi) or তুই (tui), with anyone older or unfamiliar.

## Where Bengali is spoken

<!-- topic: about-language -->

Bengali, called বাংলা (baanglaa) by its speakers, is the official language of Bangladesh and of the Indian states of West Bengal and Tripura, and it is one of the most widely spoken languages in the world. It is an Indo-Aryan language, related to Assamese and Odia. Rabindranath Tagore (রবীন্দ্রনাথ ঠাকুর, Robindronaath Thaakur), who won the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1913, wrote in Bengali; his songs became the national anthems of both India and Bangladesh.

## International Mother Language Day — 21 February

<!-- topic: about-language; level: elementary -->

21 February, known in Bengali as একুশে ফেব্রুয়ারি (ekushe phebruyaari, "the twenty-first of February"), commemorates the 1952 Language Movement (ভাষা আন্দোলন, bhaashaa aandolon) in what is now Bangladesh, when people demanded that Bengali be recognised as a state language and several protesters were killed. UNESCO later declared 21 February International Mother Language Day, celebrated around the world to honour all mother tongues.

## Festivals: Durga Puja and Pohela Boishakh

<!-- topic: festivals; level: elementary -->

দুর্গাপূজা (Durga Puja, durgaapujo) is the biggest festival for many Bengalis, especially in West Bengal, held in autumn (September or October). Images of the goddess Durga are set up in decorated pandals, and people visit them in new clothes. পহেলা বৈশাখ (Pohela Boishakh), also called পয়লা বৈশাখ (Poylaa Boishaakh), is the Bengali New Year in mid-April, the first day of the month বৈশাখ. People greet each other with শুভ নববর্ষ (shubho noboborsho, "Happy New Year").

## Everyday etiquette

<!-- topic: etiquette -->

Some useful Bengali etiquette: greet elders with নমস্কার (nomoshkar) or আসসালামু আলাইকুম (aassaalaamu aalaaikum), depending on the person; take your shoes off before entering a home or place of worship; offer and receive things with the right hand. Guests are usually offered tea and মিষ্টি (mishti, sweets), and it is polite to accept something. Use আপনি (aapni) with anyone older until they invite you to be less formal.
