# The Bengali script

## How Bengali writing works

Bengali is written in the Bengali script (বাংলা লিপি, baanglaa lipi), an abugida that grew out of the ancient Brahmi script and is very close to the Assamese script. In an abugida every consonant letter already carries a built-in vowel. In Bengali this inherent vowel is read "o", so ক is read "ko", not "ka". To write another vowel after a consonant you add a vowel sign to it. Bengali is written from left to right. A letter is called বর্ণ (borno) and the alphabet is বর্ণমালা (bornomaalaa).

## Vowels — স্বরবর্ণ (shoroborno)

<!-- topic: vowels -->

Bengali vowels are called স্বরবর্ণ (shoroborno). They are: অ (o), আ (aa), ই (i), ঈ (i, "long i"), উ (u), ঊ (u, "long u"), ঋ (ri), এ (e), ঐ (oi), ও (o), ঔ (ou). Note that অ, the first letter, is read "o" in Bengali. ই and ঈ, and উ and ঊ, are different letters with different spellings, but in everyday Bengali speech each pair sounds almost the same. Three extra signs are used with them: ং (anusvara, an "ng" sound), ঃ (visarga) and ঁ (chandrabindu, which makes a vowel nasal, as in হ্যাঁ, hyaan, yes).

## Consonants — ব্যঞ্জনবর্ণ (byanjonborno)

<!-- topic: consonants -->

Bengali consonants are called ব্যঞ্জনবর্ণ (byanjonborno). They are arranged by where the sound is made: ক খ গ ঘ ঙ (ko group, throat), চ ছ জ ঝ ঞ (cho group, palate), ট ঠ ড ঢ ণ (retroflex group, tongue curled back), ত থ দ ধ ন (dental group, tongue at the teeth), প ফ ব ভ ম (lips), then য র ল শ ষ স হ, and the extra letters ড় ঢ় য় ৎ. Each row goes plain, aspirated (with a puff of air), voiced, voiced-aspirated, nasal. Every Bengali consonant is read with the inherent "o" (ক ko, খ kho, গ go) until a vowel sign changes it.

## Vowel signs — কার (kaar)

<!-- topic: vowel-signs -->

When a vowel follows a consonant, Bengali writes a vowel sign called a কার (kaar) instead of the full vowel; আ-কার (aa-kaar) is the sign for আ, ই-কার (i-kaar) for ই, and so on. With ক: কা (kaa), কি (ki), কী (ki), কু (ku), কূ (ku), কৃ (kri), কে (ke), কৈ (koi), কো (ko), কৌ (kou). Watch the order: the signs ি (i), ে (e) and ৈ (oi) are written before the consonant even though they are pronounced after it, and ো (o) goes on both sides: কো. Examples: মা (maa, mother), দুধ (dudh, milk).

## The head-line — মাত্রা (maatraa)

<!-- topic: script-overview -->

Most Bengali letters hang from a horizontal line along the top called the মাত্রা (maatraa). When letters are joined into a word, their head-lines touch and form one continuous line, as in নমস্কার (nomoshkar, hello) and বাবা (baabaa, father). A few Bengali letters are written without a head-line, such as এ, ঐ, ও, ঔ, ঙ and ঞ. When writing by hand, many people write the letters of a word first and then draw the head-line across the top.

## Hasanta and conjunct letters — যুক্তাক্ষর (juktaakkhor)

<!-- topic: conjuncts; level: elementary -->

A Bengali consonant with no vowel at all is shown with the হসন্ত (hosonto) mark ্, as in ক্ (k). When two or more consonants come together with no vowel between them, Bengali usually joins them into one combined letter called a যুক্তাক্ষর (juktaakkhor, conjunct). Examples: বন্ধু (bondhu, friend) has ন্ধ = ন + ধ; নমস্কার (nomoshkar) has স্ক = স + ক; ধন্যবাদ (dhonnobad, thank you) has ন্য = ন + য. Some second letters take a short form called a ফলা (pholaa), such as য-ফলা ্য and র-ফলা ্র. The letter ৎ (khondo to) is a "t" with no vowel, used at the end of words.
